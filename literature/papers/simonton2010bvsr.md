# Creative thought as blind-variation and selective-retention

## TL;DR

A half-century review of Campbell's (1960) BVSR theory: creative thought
is combinatorial variation that is *blind* (not foresighted toward the
solution) followed by selective retention of the useful variants.
Simonton formalizes this with combinatorial models across individual,
domain, and field levels, and — crucially for this project — treats
"sightedness" (how much foresight really operates) as a continuous,
empirically estimable parameter rather than an all-or-nothing claim that
creators never see where they're going.

## Claims

- Creative ideation is best modeled as generate-then-select over a space
  of combinations, where "blind" does not mean random but means the
  variation process cannot fully anticipate which combinations will
  prove adaptive/useful before they are generated and tested.
- The theory is compatible with partial foresight: BVSR is a family of
  models parameterized by a "sightedness" variable, and highly expert
  creators may have partially-sighted variation (informed guessing) while
  still being blind with respect to the ultimate criterion of success.
- Combinatorial models make testable predictions about output rate,
  quality variation, and the career trajectories of eminent scientists
  (e.g., the equal-odds rule: quality is statistically independent of
  quantity, so more attempts produce more hits *and* more misses).

## Methods

- Literature review + formal combinatorial modeling (not new empirical
  data); synthesizes 50 years of psychometric, developmental, and
  bibliometric findings under the BVSR framework and re-derives them from
  the combinatorial models.

## Results

- The equal-odds rule and quality/quantity relationships in creative
  careers are shown to fall out of the combinatorial model's assumptions,
  without needing to posit a separate, deliberate "quality control"
  mechanism.
- Domain and field-level extensions predict when independent (multiple)
  discovery should occur — connecting BVSR to Mertonian multiples as a
  special case of correlated blind variation across independent agents
  drawing on a shared, newly-adjacent combinatorial space.

## Critique / open questions

- Published with several critical commentaries in the same journal issue
  (open peer commentary format) disputing whether "blind" is falsifiable
  or does real explanatory work versus more sighted, insight-based
  accounts — this project should read BVSR as a strong, contested prior,
  not settled theory.
- The theory explains *why* an idea-first self-report can be
  post-hoc-sighted narration of what was actually a partly-blind search;
  this is the strongest theoretical (not just observational) reason to
  expect H0's minority-idea-first result.
