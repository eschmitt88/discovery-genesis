# Singletons and Multiples in Scientific Discovery

## TL;DR

Merton's foundational argument, from a large catalogue of documented
independent-discovery cases, that **multiples are the norm and singletons
the puzzle to explain**: once a discovery's enabling conditions
(background knowledge, instruments, problems recognized as urgent) are in
place, it becomes reachable by several people or groups independently —
"all scientific discoveries are in principle multiples." Apparent
singletons are usually multiples where one claimant went unpublished,
suppressed, or unrecorded.

## Claims

- Multiple, independent, near-simultaneous discovery of the same result
  is the empirically common pattern in the history of science, not a
  curious exception.
- The corollary: a discovery's difficulty is largely a function of
  whether its enabling conditions have arrived in a field, not of any
  individual's exceptional insight — once conditions are ripe, the
  discovery is "in the air" for whoever is positioned to make it.
- Improvements in scientific communication (accelerating how fast new
  results and methods propagate) increase the observed rate of multiples,
  because more independent groups reach the same adjacent-possible
  region at once.

## Methods

- Historical/sociological case catalogue: Merton assembles and analyzes a
  large number of documented cases of independent, simultaneous, or
  near-simultaneous discovery across the history of science (this
  citation-index capture does not include the full case list; see
  Follow-up).

## Critique / open questions

- Full text not independently re-read for this note (JSTOR-gated, no OA
  copy found via Unpaywall/OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar — this is a
  pre-DOI 1961 proceedings piece, essentially absent from modern citation
  databases). The claims above are corroborated by the citation record
  (96 citing works logged by 2004 in the HistCite index, including
  multiple Simonton papers) and Wikipedia's "Multiple discovery" article,
  but not verified against Merton's own case-by-case evidence.
- "Multiples in principle" is a strong, somewhat unfalsifiable claim as
  stated (any singleton can be explained away as an unrecorded multiple);
  this project's coding protocol should treat *documented* near-
  simultaneous independent discovery as the operational signal, not the
  stronger unfalsifiable version of the thesis.
