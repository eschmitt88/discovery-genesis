# A Propulsion Model of Types of Creative Contributions

## TL;DR

An independently-derived, finite taxonomy of creative contributions
(replication, redefinition, forward incrementation, advance forward
incrementation, redirection, reconstruction, reinitiation, integration),
organized by whether the contribution accepts, rejects, or integrates the
field's current paradigm — a second, previously-published small taxonomy
to cross-check the project's own `move-taxonomy` against.

## Claims

- Creative contributions in any domain can be sorted into a small,
  finite set of types (originally eight) defined by their relationship to
  the field's current accepted paradigm/frame, not by domain content.
- Most real contributions are "forward incrementation" — a small,
  paradigm-accepting step in the field's current direction — with the
  paradigm-rejecting types (redirection, reinitiation) being rarer and
  higher-risk/higher-reward.
- The paradigm-relationship dimension is explicitly proposed as a rival
  or complement to Kuhn's normal-science/paradigm-shift dichotomy.

## Methods

- Conceptual/theoretical taxonomy construction (not an empirical coding
  study in this 1999 paper) later applied post-hoc to case examples in
  Sternberg's subsequent work (arts and letters, 2001; psychology itself,
  various).

## Results

- Not independently verified against the full text in this pass (see
  Critique). Secondary sources consistently report the same 7–8 type
  taxonomy and paradigm-accepting/rejecting/integrating framing across
  Sternberg's own later restatements.

## Critique / open questions

- No full text or abstract independently obtained (APA/SAGE paywall, no
  OA copy per Unpaywall) — claims rest on cross-checked secondary
  sources, not a primary re-read; treat with correspondingly lower
  confidence than the fully-read sources in this batch.
- The taxonomy was proposed conceptually, not derived from a corpus the
  way TRIZ (patents) or this project (papers) derives its taxonomy — it
  is a useful *shape check* (is ~8 categories, sorted by
  paradigm-relationship, a plausible taxonomy size and axis?) rather than
  independent empirical evidence for any specific move.
