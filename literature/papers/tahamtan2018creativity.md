# Creativity in science and the link to cited references

## TL;DR

Case studies and direct interviews with the authors of landmark
scientometrics papers (h-index, co-citation analysis, journal impact
factor variants) find that the "unusual reference combination = creative
idea" story does not hold up from the inside: authors themselves
attribute their ideas to practical problems, colleague conversations, and
prior work that was *not* cited — not to the atypical combinations
visible in their reference lists.

## Claims

- Direct interview evidence (not just bibliometric inference) that
  creative ideas in landmark scientometrics papers usually did not arise
  from combining cited references.
- The reference list systematically misses load-bearing influences:
  authors report reading and being influenced by works they did not cite,
  for reasons unrelated to the idea's origin (journal reference-count
  limits, conference proceedings page limits, or simply that the
  influence was conceptual/practical rather than citable).
- Ideas more often trace to: a practical problem the author needed to
  solve, a conversation with a colleague, or interdisciplinary exposure —
  categories invisible to a reference-list-only analysis.

## Methods

- Selected landmark/breakthrough papers in scientometrics (h-index,
  co-citation analysis, audience factor, RCR, and related indicators);
  contacted the corresponding/originating authors (Jorge Hirsch, Henry
  Small, Wolfgang Glänzel, András Schubert, Michel Zitt, and others) and
  asked directly where the idea came from and what role cited
  publications played.
- Cross-referenced author accounts against the papers' actual reference
  lists and against Uzzi et al.'s (2013) atypical-combination framing.

## Results

- Jorge Hirsch developed the h-index while personally evaluating job
  candidates' citation records — not from reading the bibliometrics
  literature; he had not intended to publish it, and the paper that
  reportedly interested him in bibliometrics is not cited in his
  breakthrough paper.
- Henry Small's co-citation idea arose from a documentation project on
  the history of nuclear physics at AIP's Niels Bohr Library, not from
  combining cited bibliometric references.
- Across a separate cited comparison, only ~50% of patents (Callaert et
  al. interviews with Belgian inventors) trace their inspiration to
  scientific references at all, and of those, 30% cite no scientific
  references despite reporting they were inspired by them — the citation
  record undercounts influence in both directions.
- Authors report material reference omissions driven by journal
  reference-count limits (e.g., Nature's ~50-reference cap) — a purely
  structural reason the reference list cannot be read as a complete
  ingredient list.

## Critique / open questions

- Small, non-random sample (hand-picked landmark scientometrics papers);
  authors interviewed decades after the fact are themselves subject to
  hindsight/narrative reconstruction — the paper does not fully control
  for this, though its finding (ideas *not* from cited references) cuts
  *against* the self-serving idea-first narrative, which makes the
  finding more credible, not less.
- Domain is scientometrics itself — a reflexive, unusually
  self-referential field; generalization to physical/life-science
  discovery (this project's sampling frame) is not established here.

## Trust signals

- **Credibility:** 4 — Max Planck Society co-author (Bornmann is a
  leading bibliometrician), peer-reviewed Journal of Informetrics
  (Elsevier); citation count is modest (6, published version) for the
  paper's evident influence, likely reflecting its narrow scientometrics
  audience rather than weak quality.

## Follow-up

- **Relevance:** 5 — directly falsifies (or at least strongly bounds) H4
  as stated: for at least this cluster of landmark papers, the ingredient
  most responsible for the idea was *not* in the reference list at all,
  and the missing ingredient is a specific, recurring category
  (practical problem faced by the author; colleague conversation). This
  is the concrete evidence base for seeding a new concept
  (`uncited-social-ingredient` / `literature-based-discovery`, see
  below) and for treating the genesis card's `external_story` field as
  load-bearing rather than opportunistic.
