# Fish oil, Raynaud's syndrome, and undiscovered public knowledge

## TL;DR

The founding case of literature-based discovery: Swanson noticed two
disconnected medical literatures — fish oil reduces blood viscosity/
platelet aggregation (A→B), and reduced viscosity/vasospasm eases
Raynaud's syndrome (B→C) — that never cited each other, and proposed the
untested transitive hypothesis A→C purely by combinatorial search over
published titles/abstracts. No new data, no new instrument: a limit case
of discovery as pure recombination of existing, cited-able (but
uncited-by-each-other) public knowledge.

## Claims

- A testable scientific hypothesis can be generated by finding two
  literatures that share an intermediate concept (B) but do not cite each
  other, without collecting any new data.
- Such "undiscovered public knowledge" exists because science's growth
  and specialization make it structurally likely that A-related and
  C-related literatures diverge (different journals, different author
  communities) even when a valid A→B→C chain connects them.
- The specific hypothesis (fish oil for Raynaud's) was later tested
  clinically with results supportive of Swanson's literature-derived
  prediction.

## Methods

- Manual literature search across two independent medical/biomedical
  literatures (hemorheology and Raynaud's-syndrome treatment), looking
  for a shared intermediate concept with no direct citation link between
  the two bodies of work.

## Results

- Established the ABC model (A→B in one literature, B→C in a disjoint
  literature, therefore hypothesize A→C) as an operational, repeatable
  procedure — Swanson and successors ran the same method to generate
  several other hypotheses (e.g., migraine/magnesium), founding the field
  of literature-based discovery (LBD), later automated (ARROWSMITH and
  descendants).

## Critique / open questions

- No abstract or full text independently obtained for this note (pre-1996
  MEDLINE record with no indexed abstract; publisher paywalled). Claims
  above rest on the well-documented secondary consensus across the LBD
  literature (this paper is cited, and its mechanism restated, in nearly
  every LBD survey since) rather than a primary re-read.
- The ABC model requires the connecting concept B to already be
  explicit and locatable via keyword search in both literatures — it is
  not clear how often real cases of accretion genesis have such a clean,
  searchable intermediate versus a fuzzier, non-lexical connection.

## Follow-up

- **Relevance:** 5 — the cleanest available case of accretion/
  recombination genesis with *zero* new empirical ingredient: every
  component of the discovery was already published and citable, only
  the arrangement was new. This is the limit case H4 needs on the
  "ingredients are almost always cited" end of the spectrum, and a
  concrete algorithmic precedent (ARROWSMITH-style AB-BC search) the
  `genesis` pipeline's `ingredients` field extraction could borrow from.
