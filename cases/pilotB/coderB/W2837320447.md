## Reasoning
The introduction's very first substantive move is naming a precise, narrow gap — not "the glymphatic system is important" in general, but specifically that no human study had shown brain-wide (rather than ROI-limited) tracer access and clearance — which is the problem-first signature in codebook §1. The ingredient that closes it, the group's own intrathecal-gadobutrol tracer protocol, was already established through their prior publications; what changed was the scope of analysis (whole-brain FreeSurfer segmentation) and the comparison cohort (dementia vs. reference), both applied to answer that specific, newly narrowed question.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is a real alternative: whole-brain FreeSurfer segmentation, newly paired with the group's existing tracer protocol, is itself a capability that had not previously been applied to this problem, and one could argue the paper's central move is "we can now analyze the whole brain, so let's see what that reveals" rather than "we set out to answer whether brain-wide access exists." I rate problem-first higher because the introduction's rhetorical structure leads with the specific unanswered question (brain-wide vs. ROI-limited) before introducing the analytic approach.
