## Reasoning
The paper names its open question first and explicitly ("bystander or driver"), then deploys an already-published genetic system (kinesin-13 overexpression, refs 5–7, up to 5 years old) as the ingredient that lets it answer that question — a clean match to codebook §1's problem-first criterion #3, which explicitly allows an ingredient older than 3 years as long as the question is what's new. The specific mechanistic hook (cGAS sensing of micronuclear rupture) was itself only established the year before (2017), which is why I mark `problem_age_specific` as new despite the broad CIN-metastasis question being a decade-plus old.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is plausible: one could argue the 2017 papers establishing cGAS-micronuclei sensing are the capability that made this specific causal study conceivable, and the paper's logic runs from "we now know micronuclei are sensed by cGAS" to "let's see if this drives metastasis." I rate problem-first higher because the abstract and introduction foreground the causality question (bystander vs. driver) as the framing, with the cGAS mechanism arriving as the explanatory pathway discovered along the way, not as the paper's starting capability.

## Notes for the codebook
A textbook case of `problem-first` with an ingredient that is itself very recent (1 year) rather than old — worth noting because most problem-first exemplars in the pilot lean on older ingredients; here the "ingredient need not be ≤3y old" rule is not being stretched in the direction it was written for (it works fine for young ingredients too, just less interestingly).
