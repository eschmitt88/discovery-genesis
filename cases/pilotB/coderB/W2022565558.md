## Reasoning
With no abstract or full text, the title itself is the strongest signal: it foregrounds the deposition instrument ("co-sputtering assisted by multi-dipolar microwave plasma") ahead of any stated scientific finding, which is the classic means-first tell — the tool is the subject of the sentence, and the paper is expected to report what that tool reveals about film quality/composition for this hard-to-deposit compound. The plasma-source technology itself (1995, 2004 refs) predates the paper by 7-16 years and originates from a different application domain (ion implantation), reinforcing that this is an existing instrument transferred into a new application rather than a newly invented technique or a stated hypothesis about Mg2Sn film behavior. Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing the authors predicted specific stoichiometric/phase outcomes from plasma parameters before running the depositions, which would push toward idea-first.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a plausible alternative: depositing stoichiometric Mg2Sn thin films is an old, standing problem, and one could argue the multi-dipolar plasma source (most recently described in this application context in 2004, ~7 years before publication) is a "recently available" ingredient that made this specific attempt newly tractable — close to the problem-first pattern of an old problem meeting a recent-enough ingredient. I lean means-first over problem-first because the title's instrument-first framing more strongly suggests "let's see what this tool does here" than "this old problem can now be solved because X just became available."
