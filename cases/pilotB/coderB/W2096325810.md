## Reasoning
The introduction's own structure — a chronological list of prior phenomena demonstrated on the identical fs-laser-written waveguide platform (Bloch oscillations 1999, dynamic localization 2008, Bloch-Zener 2009, Landau-Zener 2009, Lieb bulk/edge transport 2014) — reads as a capability being serially applied to successive theoretical targets as each becomes experimentally reachable, which is the means-first signature in codebook §1. The specific target (flat-band localization) is very recent (immediate precedent one year prior), while the platform and the underlying flat-band theory are both well over a decade old, hence the broad/specific problem-age split.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is plausible: the paper explicitly names an unmet target ('diffraction-free propagation of a flat-band state has not been observed') inherited directly from Guzman-Silva et al. (2014), and could be read as using the pre-existing fabrication technique purely as a tool to close that specific gap. I rate this second because the introduction's own framing emphasizes platform versatility and control ('different physical platforms... offer high control... suggests an exciting route') ahead of the specific unmet observation, and the near-simultaneous competing paper suggests platform-driven timing rather than question-driven timing.

## Notes for the codebook
A cross-domain (physics vs. the biology cases in this batch) example of the same means-first/problem-first ambiguity that arises whenever a mature, reusable experimental platform is serially pointed at the next topical target — worth flagging as a recurring pattern for the codebook rather than a one-off.
