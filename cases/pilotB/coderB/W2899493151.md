## Reasoning
The abstract leads with a concrete, recurring operational failure (repeated interceptions of morphologically unidentifiable immatures) before describing the method used to close it, matching codebook §1's problem-first criterion: a named open question closed by a pre-existing ingredient (an already-established real-time PCR assay-design template, evidenced by the cited 2015 'toolbox' for related species). Because only the abstract and reference list are available, I cannot verify this against the paper's own introduction, so confidence is capped at medium.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is plausible: the same border-biosecurity program appears to have an established real-time PCR assay-development pipeline already applied to multiple other tephritid species (per the 2004, 2005, and 2015 precedents in the reference list), and this paper could be read as that pipeline being pointed at the next two species in a routine surveillance-tool expansion, rather than as a study organized around these two species' diagnostic gap specifically. I rate problem-first higher because the abstract explicitly foregrounds the operational trigger (repeated interceptions) before the method.
