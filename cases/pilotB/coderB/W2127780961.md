## Reasoning
My first instinct was means-first — the paper's power to estimate very low incidence rates (single digits per 100,000 women-years) depends entirely on a population scale (331,818 women) that only an existing, large integrated health system's routine program could supply, which felt like a capability-drove-the-finding case. But re-reading the abstract's own structure against codebook §1's decision order, the opening move is a named, specific evidence gap ('lack of evidence... in routine clinical practice') rather than a capability announcement, and the large cohort is best read as the pre-existing ingredient (KPNC's co-testing program, already running since 2003) used to close that named gap — which is the problem-first criterion, and problem-first and means-first are adjacent in the decision order so this distinction matters less than the accretion boundary, but I followed the abstract's explicit framing.

## Alternative reading
Means-first remains a live alternative: one could argue that without KPNC's uniquely large, already-linked lab-and-outcomes database, the specific claim (single HPV-negative test sufficient for 3–5 year reassurance) could not have been made with adequate precision by any group, making the database's existence the true generative cause rather than a tool selected to answer a pre-formed question. I rate problem-first slightly higher because the abstract's own rhetorical structure leads with the gap, not the resource.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a good candidate for the `scale` label (unattested in the v1 pilot so far) — the paper's central move is genuinely enabled by population scale in a way distinguishable from ordinary gap-filling; worth watching whether `scale` recurs at n≈50 as codebook §2 anticipates.
