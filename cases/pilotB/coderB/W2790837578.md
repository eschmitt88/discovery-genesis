## Reasoning
The abstract has the clean idea-first shape: a diagnosed gap ("using labels only prevents stakeholders from... understanding what business processes or services achieve"), immediately followed by a stated proposition ("we define a business capability meta-model..."), with validation (ontological evaluation + interviews) explicitly presented as a check performed afterward. Nothing in the references (Semantic Web 2001, capability theory 2004, ontological-modeling evaluation methodology 1999) is new to this paper — they are all established building blocks the authors formalize into a new artifact. That combination — old ingredients, a stated design proposition, validation-after-design — is the textbook idea-first pattern. Evidence that would change my mind: if full text showed the meta-model emerged from analyzing real capability-annotation data rather than being designed up front (which would push toward means-first).

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a plausible second reading: an established problem (label-only process modeling, documented since at least 2009 in the cited activity-labeling literature) meets a comparatively recent ingredient — the 2014 Formal Concept Analysis paper on sensor capabilities, or growing RDF/linked-data tooling maturity circa 2011-2014 — that made this formalization newly tractable. I rate it less likely because the core ingredients driving the design (Semantic Web, 2001; capability theory, 2004) are a decade or more old relative to the 2018 publication, not "recently available," which weakens the problem-first case relative to idea-first.
