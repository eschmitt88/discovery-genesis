## Reasoning
The paper is squarely problem-first: it names a specific open question (does clinically-selected, covered-stent early TIPS improve outcomes?) that a predecessor study (Monescillo 2004) had already gestured at answering but with design flaws that blocked adoption. The enabling ingredient — the e-PTFE-covered stent — pre-existed by six years and is treated as an off-the-shelf component, not something the paper had to build; the paper's contribution is the trial design and clinical answer, not the stent. Evidence that would change my mind: if the introduction had framed the covered stent's arrival as the reason to revisit TIPS timing (capability→observation), that would push toward means-first, but the text's causal chain runs the other way (question first, tool used to answer it).

## Alternative reading
Means-first is the plausible second reading: the 2004 covered stent, by solving TIPS's durability/dysfunction problem, is arguably what made early (rather than rescue-only) TIPS a defensible strategy at all — so the capability could be read as prior to and generative of the question. I judge this less likely because the text's own causal narrative subordinates the stent to a named clinical question inherited from a specific earlier trial.
