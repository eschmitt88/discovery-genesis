## Reasoning
This is a theoretical/interpretive humanities paper, not an empirical discovery, so the genesis question is really "where did the organizing proposition come from." The abstract states the Derridean/deconstructive framing as the lens applied to the biology, not as something derived from it — the theoretical stance reads as portable and pre-existing (continuing a recognizable STS-of-microbiology program with cited precedents like Helmreich 2009 and Hird-adjacent individuality debates 2012). That is the idea-first signature: a proposition ("microbial death deconstructs bounded individuality/human-exceptionalist time") that could have been stated before this specific case survey. Evidence that would change my mind: if the full text showed the phytoplankton PCD findings themselves forced a novel theoretical move not present in prior STS-of-biology work.

## Alternative reading
Accretion is a real alternative: read narrowly, this could be one more application of an already-standard critical-theory toolkit (Derrida + feminist STS) to a new empirical domain, within an established subfield that routinely produces such essays — no single new idea, just another case study extending a genre. I lean idea-first over accretion because the abstract frames the case as doing real conceptual work (reconstructing life/death and individuality), not merely illustrating an established point.

## Notes for the codebook
The v0 schema (ingredients with status/year/role, "move" as a verb-first action on prior art) is built for empirical-science papers and fits awkwardly here: there is no "ingredient the contribution depends on" in the experimental sense — theory papers depend on other theories and on borrowed empirical results, which is a different shape than method/data/instrument dependency. `is_primary: no` (perspective) is the right bucket but the codebook's move labels (transfer, recombination) undersell what's happening, which is closer to "reframing" — a move not in the v0 list. I used `transfer` (theory imported from human/animal philosophy into microbiology) plus `recombination` (biology + critical theory) as the closest fit but neither captures the genre well.
