## Reasoning
The paper's own methods section narrates a genuine act of theoretical construction: the COM-B model is explicitly built by combining two conceptually independent traditions (a 1991 psychological consensus statement and a criminal-law principle) into one novel proposition, which then organizes everything that follows. That is the idea-first signature in codebook §1 — a stated proposition, not a gap statement, that the paper's design follows from — and it is checked only after ruling out accretion (the paper does construct something new, not merely aggregate) and means-first (no single capability drives the finding; the systematic review is a supporting method, not the generative cause).

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a plausible alternative: one could read the paper as primarily closing the diagnosed gap (no framework meets all three usefulness criteria) using the systematic review's output — 19 frameworks' worth of intervention functions and policy categories — as the pre-existing ingredient, with COM-B serving mainly as an organizing device layered on top. I rate idea-first higher because the text treats the COM-B synthesis as a distinct, load-bearing design decision made before and independently of the systematic review's cataloguing work.

## Notes for the codebook
`is_primary: partial` fits well here (framework/taxonomy contribution, artefact not finding), but this is also a strong idea-first exemplar worth keeping as a calibration case — the "two independent traditions combined into one model" pattern is a clean, textually explicit instance of what idea-first is supposed to capture, in contrast to the vaguer "gap statement masquerading as a hypothesis" pattern v0 over-counted.
