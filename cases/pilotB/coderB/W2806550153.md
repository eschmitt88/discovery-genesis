## Reasoning
This is a CDC/FDA MMWR surveillance report analyzing the newest wave of an already-running annual survey (NYTS). The abstract and reference list (only 5 items, almost entirely the agencies' own prior reports) show no new method, sampling design, instrument, or theoretical claim — the entire contribution is fresh numbers from a routine, recurring data collection. That is exactly what the codebook's accretion definition describes: "a large trial of an established question," here literally the next scheduled data point in an ongoing monitoring series. Evidence that would change my mind: if the full text had introduced a new survey module, weighting method, or analytic technique not used in prior NYTS reports — none of that appears.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a weak alternative: the recent, rapid rise of e-cigarette use (a genuinely newer phenomenon within the survey's history) could be framed as a "new ingredient" that makes this particular data release more analytically interesting than a routine update. I rate this less likely because the paper doesn't test or explain the e-cigarette rise with any new ingredient — it just reports prevalence numbers descriptively, same as for all other product categories.
