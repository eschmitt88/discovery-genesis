## Reasoning
The introduction is unusually explicit about the gap it targets: prior work established S/G-ratio and methoxyl differences between bark and wood lignin (references 9-11) but left β-O-4 linkage stereochemistry in bark completely uncharacterized. The paper closes this by applying the ozonation method (already ~9 years old, from a 2002 reference) — a well-established but not-yet-applied-to-bark technique — to new plant material. That is the definitional problem-first shape: a specific, already-articulated open question meets an existing, ready-to-use ingredient, and the move is close to a natural next step once someone chooses to collect and process bark samples. Evidence that would change my mind: if the ozonation method itself had required nontrivial adaptation for bark material (which would push toward means-first, an instrument-adaptation story).

## Alternative reading
Accretion is a real alternative: this could be scored as one more species/tissue-fraction characterization study in a long-running lignin-chemistry program using a standard toolkit (nitrobenzene oxidation, ozonation, methoxyl determination, NMR) — incremental extension rather than a genuinely new "move." I lean problem-first because of the explicit, specific gap statement in the text, which distinguishes this from a pure survey/consolidation exercise, but the narrowness of the resulting finding (a modest quantitative comparison) keeps accretion a close second.
