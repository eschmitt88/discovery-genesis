## Reasoning
The abstract states its motivating question explicitly ("Little is known about whether the duration of adjuvant imatinib influences the prognostic significance of KIT and PDGFRA mutations"), and the analysis becomes possible specifically because the underlying randomized trial (published 2012) has now accumulated median 88-month follow-up. This is the classic problem-first pattern: an established, already-articulated open question (does genotype modify duration-of-therapy benefit?) meets a necessary ingredient — sufficient follow-up time on an already-existing trial cohort — that recently became available, making the analysis close to a natural, expected next step for the trial's investigators. Evidence that would change my mind: if the abstract framed the mutation-subgroup breakdown as an unplanned, serendipitous finding from routine monitoring rather than a designed exploratory objective.

## Alternative reading
Accretion is a plausible alternative: post hoc genotype-by-treatment subgroup analyses of matured randomized trials are a fairly routine, almost templated practice in oncology once trial cohorts accumulate enough follow-up and events — this could be read as "a large trial of an established question" rather than a genesis-worthy move. I rate problem-first as more likely because the abstract explicitly frames a specific, previously unanswered interaction question (not just "let's see what subgroups show"), and the clinical finding (codon-557/558-specific duration benefit) is a genuinely new, actionable result rather than a confirmatory update.
