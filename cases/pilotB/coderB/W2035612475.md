## Reasoning
The abstract's own structure — naming a specific concept ('integrated healthscape strategies') as its Conclusions-section deliverable, built from an explicit theoretical import (ecological systems theory) rather than from a reported empirical finding — matches codebook §1's idea-first criterion: a stated proposition that organizes the paper's design, not just a gap statement. Since the source theory (developmental/environmental ecological psychology) has a different primary field from the target literature (healthcare facility design/health services research), I coded the move as `transfer` rather than `recombination`.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a real alternative: one could read the paper as centered on the specific, named methodological gap in EBD (no differentiation of research purposes, no ecological theory) with the imported ecological framework simply the pre-existing means used to close it, rather than as a proposition stated in advance that the paper's design then follows. I rate idea-first slightly higher because the abstract explicitly names a new concept as the contribution rather than framing the paper as answering a question with an existing tool.
