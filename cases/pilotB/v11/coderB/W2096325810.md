## Reasoning
Both key ingredients — femtosecond-laser-written waveguide-array fabrication and flat-band lattice theory — are established and cited to prior work (fabrication technique to 1996, flat-band theory to 1992, and the specific photonic-Lieb-lattice platform to a directly preceding paper by a different group, Guzman-Silva et al.). The gap is narrow and explicitly named: that prior work demonstrated transport phenomena in the same lattice type but not the flat-band state itself. This is a clean problem-first, `gap-filling:first-measurement` case: an established fabrication platform used to observe a specific, previously unobserved phenomenon predicted by decades-old theory.

## Alternative reading
means-first is a weak alternative if the specific mode-preparation technique used to excite a flat-band state (as opposed to bulk or edge modes) is treated as a novel capability — the full text does describe careful input-beam preparation to selectively excite the flat band, which could be read as a methodological innovation. I weighted problem-first higher because the introduction frames the fabrication platform itself as pre-existing and credits the paper's novelty to the specific observation, not to a new instrument or fabrication capability.
