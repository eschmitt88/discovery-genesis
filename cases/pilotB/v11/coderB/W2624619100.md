## Reasoning
The reference list is bimodal: cell-biology/marine-ecology papers on programmed cell death (2001-2014) and continental-philosophy/STS texts (Derrida, Haraway, Barad — 1992-2019). The source of the organizing theoretical apparatus (Derridean deconstruction) is a different primary field (philosophy) from the empirical material it is applied to (marine microbiology), which makes `transfer` the clearest single label — not `recombination`, since the two source literatures don't share a field. I coded idea-first because the abstract states the interpretive lens as the paper's method before laying out its argument, matching the codebook's idea-first test (a proposition — deconstructive reading reveals non-anthropocentric alternatives — that the design/argument follows from).

## Alternative reading
This could be read as accretion if treated as primarily synthesizing existing literature without a novel claim, but the abstract's closing sentence ("this article explores how the deconstruction of individuality... may suggest alternatives") states an original argumentative claim, not just an aggregation, which rules that out. problem-first is a weaker second reading: it would require treating "what does microbial death mean for individuality/temporality" as a pre-existing open question in the field the paper merely answers with an existing tool (Derrida), but Derridean death-deconstruction long predates this specific application and the novelty is squarely in the combination/argument, which the codebook's idea-first framing captures better than problem-first's "old question, existing ingredient" framing.
