## Reasoning
The abstract's structure is unusually explicit for idea-first: it states the general physical principle (valley degeneracy → high zT), notes the field's existing route to it (nanostructuring), and then poses a proposition — that convergence can instead be *directed* in bulk via doping/composition — before presenting the PbTe1-xSex results as its test. That the paper closes by generalizing the result into "a general strategy... in the search for and improvement of bulk thermoelectric materials" reinforces that the organizing idea, not a specific instrument or dataset, is what's driving the paper. No new instrument, assay, or dataset is claimed; doping/alloying control is routine in this subfield.

## Alternative reading
gap-filling:first-measurement (problem-first) is a reasonable second reading if one treats "can valley convergence be achieved in bulk" as a pre-existing open question in the field rather than a proposition this paper originates — the nanostructuring literature (1998-2009) does implicitly raise it. I weighted idea-first higher because the abstract's rhetorical structure states the mechanism-level proposition first and generalizes from it, which is the v1.1/codebook idea-first signature, rather than naming a narrow pre-existing question.
