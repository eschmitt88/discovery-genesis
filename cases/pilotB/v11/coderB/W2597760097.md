## Reasoning
Although Tn5 transposon mutagenesis is a decades-old, generic technique, the enabling capability here is the *combination*: a purpose-built triple-knockout strain (new to this paper) that removes all known iron-acquisition routes, making a previously masked residual pathway detectable by an unbiased screen. That's the means-first signature — the paper's logic runs from building this new experimental system, to an observation (residual growth, then a screen hit), to the claim (a novel T6SS/PQS/OMV iron-acquisition mechanism). This isn't the authors reapplying an established assay to a "next target" gene they already suspected; the screen was unbiased and the discovered gene/mechanism was unanticipated.

## Alternative reading
problem-first is a plausible second reading if one treats "what explains residual growth in iron-depleted media" as a pre-existing open question in P. aeruginosa iron-biology and the Tn5 screen as simply an existing, well-worn tool used to answer it — favoring problem-first would require weighting the screening method's un-newness over the newness of the purpose-built triple mutant it was applied to. I weighted means-first higher because the triple-knockout strain is explicitly new-here and is what makes the screen informative at all.

## Notes for the codebook
Good test case for the boundary between means-first and problem-first when the "capability" is a newly engineered experimental system (a strain) rather than a new instrument or assay in the more literal sense — the codebook's enabler vocabulary (`new-instrument`) accommodates this, but it's worth flagging that "instrument" in the move-label sense stretches naturally to cover "purpose-built biological system + established screening method" combinations like this one.
