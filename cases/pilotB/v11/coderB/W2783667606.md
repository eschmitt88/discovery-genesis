## Reasoning
The core experimental tool — kinesin-13 (Kif2b/MCAK) overexpression to suppress chromosome missegregation independent of aneuploidy — is presented as already established (cited to refs 5-7, consistent with the authors' own prior mechanistic work on CIN), which under the v1.1 tell points to problem-first: an established platform applied to answer a new question (CIN's causal role in metastasis specifically, as opposed to tumor evolution/heterogeneity generally, where the tool was previously used). The paper's opening sentence states the exact open question this closes. I listed both `unification` and `gap-filling:first-measurement` as move labels because the paper does two distinct, load-bearing things: it is the first causal demonstration that CIN drives metastasis (first-measurement), and its central mechanistic discovery mechanistically links two previously largely separate literatures — mitotic chromosome-segregation error biology and innate-immune cytosolic-DNA sensing (cGAS-STING) — as one pathway (unification). Removing either label would misrepresent what the paper's abstract itself claims as its contribution.

## Alternative reading
means-first is a plausible and closely competing reading if the cGAS-STING pathway's role in this context, or the specific causal-manipulation experimental design (kinesin-13 suppression + in vivo metastasis tracking + RNA-seq), is treated as itself the new capability that generated the finding — the paper does combine several established techniques (bioluminescence tracking, RNA-seq, genetic knockdown of cGAS/STING) in a way not previously assembled together for this question. I weighted problem-first higher because the CIN-suppression tool specifically (the paper's stated key enabling method, cited as pre-existing) is what the introduction credits with making the causal test possible, rather than presenting any of the individual techniques as new.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a good stress-test for the v1.1 tell in a case where the discovered *mechanism* (cGAS-STING) is strikingly novel even though the *tool* that enabled the discovery is not — the codebook's problem-first/means-first distinction tracks the tool's novelty, not the finding's novelty, and I think that's the right reading, but it's worth flagging explicitly since a coder focused on how surprising the result is could easily default to means-first here.
