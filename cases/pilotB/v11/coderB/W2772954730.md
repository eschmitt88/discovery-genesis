## Reasoning
Both technical ingredients — the biomarker assay panel and the Xbox Kinect exergame — are established, off-the-shelf tools cited to prior work; no new instrument or assay is introduced. What's new is the specific measurement: applying the established biomarker panel to this specific, previously untested intervention (exergaming) in this population. That is a clean `gap-filling:first-measurement` case under problem-first: the question (does exergaming produce the same cardiometabolic biomarker benefits as conventional exercise) is new, the means to answer it are not.

## Alternative reading
means-first would require treating the biomarker-panel-plus-exergame combination itself as a novel measurement capability, but neither piece is presented as new — the introduction cites both independently as established. I don't see a strong case for it.

## Notes for the codebook
Uncomplicated single-arm pre/post intervention study; no real coding friction. Notable as a domain (exercise physiology/exergaming) where `gap-filling:first-measurement` recurs as "established outcome panel applied to a newly popular intervention modality," distinct from the biomedical `next-target` pattern of extending a diagnostic/genetic assay to a new disease or species.
