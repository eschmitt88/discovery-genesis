## Reasoning
This is a direct instance of the v1.1 rule §6 worked example (a framework paper proposing a stated model, explicitly named COM-B): the paper's central generative act is proposing a structure (the behaviour-system model and the wheel built around it), and the reliability testing and framework-comparison data are organized around validating that pre-stated structure rather than the structure emerging from a hypothesis-testing result. I coded `is_primary: partial — framework` because, despite containing a systematic review and reliability data, the contribution the paper itself claims is the artefact (BCW/COM-B), not an empirical finding. I listed both `formalisation` (a new formal taxonomy/model created) and `consolidation` (explicitly built by synthesizing 19 existing frameworks) as move labels because the text supports both as load-bearing — removing either would misstate what the paper says it did ("derived from classifications already available" for consolidation; "a new framework... constructed... explicitly to overcome their limitations" for formalisation).

## Alternative reading
means-first is a plausible second reading if the systematic-review-plus-evaluation-criteria methodology is treated as the enabling "capability" (new to the authors — the paper claims to be "the first attempt to undertake a systematic analysis of behaviour intervention frameworks and apply usefulness criteria to them"). I weighted idea-first higher because the codebook explicitly resolves this exact paper type toward idea-first, and because the paper's own narrative foregrounds the proposed model/structure as the contribution, with the systematic review serving as background evidence for the model's necessity rather than as the source of the model itself.

## Notes for the codebook
This paper is literally the codebook's own worked example for rule §6 (framework paper, explicit model, e.g. COM-B), so it's reassuring rather than surprising to land exactly where the rule predicts — but it's a good test that the rule handles the "framework WITH some validation data" case (not just no-data essays) correctly, since this paper does include a systematic review and reliability testing, which could tempt a coder toward `yes`/empirical without the explicit rule.
