## Reasoning
Every technical ingredient — TIPS, e-PTFE-covered stents, hepatic venous pressure gradient risk stratification, standard pharmacotherapy/EBL — is established, cited technology and practice. What makes the trial necessary and new is a specifically named methodological gap in prior evidence: the earlier trial supporting early TIPS used a comparator arm that didn't reflect real standard of care, so the true magnitude (and even direction) of benefit against proper contemporary treatment was unconfirmed. That is a clean problem-first case; I coded `gap-filling:first-measurement` because this is, in effect, the first rigorous measurement of early-TIPS benefit against a valid contemporary comparator, correcting a confound rather than merely adding sample size.

## Alternative reading
means-first is a weak alternative if the e-PTFE-covered stent (as opposed to older bare stents) were treated as a new-to-the-field capability enabling a fairer test — covered stents reduce shunt dysfunction relative to earlier bare-stent TIPS. I did not weight this highly because the text presents the covered stent as already-established technology being used, not as a novel capability the paper introduces; the explicitly named gap is about the comparator arm's validity, not about a new device.

## Notes for the codebook
Useful example distinguishing `gap-filling:first-measurement` from `gap-filling:bigger-n`: this isn't a larger/longer version of the same trial — it's a rerun designed specifically to close a validity gap (a fair comparator) left by a prior, methodologically compromised RCT. Worth considering whether the codebook wants a sub-label for "replication with a corrected design," since that's a distinct and fairly common pattern in clinical trials that `first-measurement` only approximately captures.
