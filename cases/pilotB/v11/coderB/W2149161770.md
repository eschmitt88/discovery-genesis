## Reasoning
This is an unusually clean means-first case: the abstract explicitly names the missing capability (adult-zebrafish lineage tracing) as the reason the question was open, then reports building it and using it. The Poss lab's own prior zebrafish-regeneration work (2002, 2006, 2009 in the reference list) establishes the biological model but not the fate-mapping technology used here, so the v1.1 "serial reuse of one's own platform" tell for problem-first does not apply — the lineage-tracing capability itself, not just the target, is new.

## Alternative reading
problem-first is the natural second reading if one treats "what is the cellular origin of regenerated myocardium" as the pre-existing open question and the new fate-mapping tools as merely instrumental to answering it rather than the paper's central contribution. I weighted means-first higher because the abstract foregrounds the technology gap as the explicit reason the question was unanswerable, not just as incidental methodology.

## Notes for the codebook
Good worked example for the v1.1 means-first tell in the opposite direction from the "serial platform reuse" pattern: here the paper explicitly narrates the capability as newly built rather than reused, which is exactly the contrast the rule is trying to capture.
