## Reasoning
The survey instrument (ESPAD) and the individual risk factors tested (peer smoking, parental monitoring, truancy, etc.) are all established from prior literature (references back to 1983); nothing new is built. What's new is the specific combined analysis — a 20-year trend plus a multivariate factor-association model applied to Ireland's accumulated survey waves — which fits problem-first. I listed both `gap-filling:first-measurement` (first analysis of the closing gender gap and its associated factors for Ireland) and `gap-filling:bigger-n` (leveraging the full accumulated multi-wave run rather than a single survey wave) because the abstract states these as two co-equal, separately load-bearing aims.

## Alternative reading
accretion is a plausible partial reading for the trend-description component alone (smoking prevalence by year is close to a routine survey report), but the multivariate regression testing specific risk-factor associations — with a stated hypothesis-adjacent question about what explains the gender-gap closure — goes beyond aggregation into genuine problem-first analysis, which is why I did not code this as accretion.

Reasonable test of the `gap-filling:bigger-n` vs `first-measurement` split when a paper genuinely does both at once (long time-series trend + a specific new factor-association question) — I kept both since the abstract's own OBJECTIVES section names them as distinct aims, not a single move stretched into two labels.
