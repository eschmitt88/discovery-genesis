## Reasoning
Co-author Jan P. Schouten (MRC Holland) is associated with the original MLPA technique (2002, cited 2699x), so this reads as the inventing lab's own team extending MLPA into a new operating regime. Per the v1.1 tell, that alone would suggest problem-first (serial reuse of an established platform), except that the paper's own narrated logic is explicitly capability-first: a new probe design was engineered because the existing assay's DNA requirement couldn't be met, and that new design is validated before any downstream retrospective claim is made. The capability (novel low-input probe design) is new to this paper, not routine reuse of MLPA as-is, which is why I coded means-first despite the surface resemblance to "same team, same method."

## Alternative reading
problem-first is a reasonable second reading if MLPA-on-limited-DNA is treated as a routine parameter adjustment rather than a new assay — in which case the "problem" (need DBSS-based retrospective study) drove a minor tweak to an existing method. I weighted means-first higher because the abstract frames the redesign as the paper's central technical contribution, validated on its own terms (specificity/sensitivity), not merely as a means to an end that's glossed over.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a good worked example for the "inventor's own lab extends their own method" boundary case the v1.1 rule doesn't fully cover: the tell (own-lab serial reuse → problem-first) and the capability-newness rule (novel probe design → means-first) point in opposite directions here, and I resolved it by weighting what the abstract's own narrative foregrounds as the achievement.
