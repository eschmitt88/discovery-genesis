## Reasoning
The genotyping method and the "polymorphism X vs cancer Y" study design long predate this paper and were not modified here; the only new element is the target combination (cervical lesions, Portuguese population). This fits problem-first cleanly under the decision order: an existing ingredient (established genotyping method) closes a specific, named, not-yet-tested question (does this polymorphism affect cervical-lesion risk in this population). Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing this specific research group had previously run the identical p73/cancer design many times themselves (which per v1.1 would still be problem-first, but for the "own platform" reason rather than the field-level established-method reason).

## Alternative reading
Accretion is a weak alternative if the paper is read as simply adding one more data point to an already well-populated meta-analytic literature of p73-polymorphism/cancer-risk studies (the reference list shows at least eight near-identical prior papers). I judged problem-first over accretion because the abstract frames a specific testable hypothesis about this population/lesion combination, not merely an aggregation exercise.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a common pattern in candidate-gene association literature: an established assay + established study design repeatedly retargeted at new population/cancer combinations by different groups (not the same authors). The v1.1 "own-platform" tell for problem-first doesn't strictly apply here (it's field-level reuse, not author-level), but the outcome (problem-first) is the same — worth noting the tell generalizes beyond strict self-citation.
