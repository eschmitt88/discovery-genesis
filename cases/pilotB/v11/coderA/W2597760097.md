## Reasoning
The gap here is unusually self-contained: the authors generate the anomaly (residual growth of a triple-knockout mutant) within the same study, immediately motivating a genetic screen using a well-established, unmodified transposon-mutagenesis method. That combination -- a specific, self-generated open question closed with an off-the-shelf screening approach -- is a clean problem-first case, and full text supports high confidence. The abstract's own language ("reveal links between type VI secretion, cell-cell signalling and classic siderophore receptors") points to unification as the central conceptual move: three previously separate research threads (T6SS biology, PQS quorum-sensing/iron-chelation, and siderophore-receptor biology) are shown to be one mechanism, which I judged as more central than the generic "first measurement" framing. Evidence that would change my mind: none plausible given the explicit stated logic.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is a weak alternative only if the Tn5 screening approach itself, applied to this specific purpose-built mutant background, is treated as a sufficiently novel capability; I judged it as routine application of an established method (cited to a 2005 paper describing the same mutagenesis system), which favors problem-first.

## Notes for the codebook
Strong worked example for "unification": three genuinely separate literatures (T6SS effector biology, PQS quorum-sensing/iron chemistry, classical TonB-dependent siderophore receptors) are explicitly tied into one mechanism, not merely co-cited -- a good positive contrast case against the codebook's warning that "the paper cites two fields" alone does not make unification.
