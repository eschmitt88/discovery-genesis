## Reasoning
The abstract explicitly names a specific unresolved measurement problem (ambiguous γ-ester LCC-linkage quantification) that the combined quantitative-NMR approach only partially resolves, and the methods themselves (Q-HSQC, quantitative 13C) are cited to established 2001-2004 papers rather than introduced fresh here. That combination -- named specific gap, existing methodological ingredients -- fits problem-first. Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing the specific combination of quantitative 13C + 2D-HSQC used together (rather than either alone) required a genuinely new calibration/internal-standard approach developed in this paper, which would push toward means-first.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is plausible if the "combination" of quantitative 13C and 2D-HSQC NMR, applied together for LCC-linkage quantification specifically, is itself the paper's real methodological innovation (a new-to-the-field analytical capability) rather than a straightforward reapplication of two separately established techniques. Abstract-only evidence cannot settle this.

## Notes for the codebook
This paper sits close to the boundary between "established method applied to a next target" (problem-first) and "combining two established methods yields a new capability" (means-first); without full text I could not confirm whether the combined-NMR protocol itself required non-trivial new development.
