## Reasoning
The paper's deliverable is a stated formal structure (a meta-model) that organizes everything else in the paper -- the RDF implementation and both validation methods follow from the proposed structure rather than the structure emerging inductively from data, which fits the idea-first pattern for framework papers under v1.1 rule 6. I weighed problem-first (established RDF/Semantic-Web tooling applied to a new target -- business capability representation) as a real alternative, since the underlying formalism technology is not new to the field; the meta-model's specific content (capabilities as action+property entities), however, is the paper's stated proposition, not merely an established tool retargeted. Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing the meta-model's structure emerged from the domain-expert interviews (an inductive/problem-first process) rather than being proposed prior to validation.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a close alternative: RDF and Semantic Web ontology-building are established technologies (cited 2001, 2011), and the paper could be read as this established formalism toolkit applied to a not-yet-formalized target (business capability semantics) -- a next-target move rather than a fresh theoretical proposition. I did not judge this the stricter means/idea distinction the codebook caps confidence for, but it is close enough that I kept confidence at medium rather than high.

## Notes for the codebook
This paper illustrates that the idea-first/problem-first line for "framework" papers can be genuinely close when the framework's implementation technology (here, RDF) is well-established even though the specific conceptual structure is new -- the codebook's rule 6 example (COM-B, a genuinely novel synthesized model) is a cleaner case than an RDF meta-model built on off-the-shelf Semantic Web tooling.
