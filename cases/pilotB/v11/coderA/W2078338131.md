## Reasoning
The abstract's own logic is proposition-first: the general strategy ("band engineering to converge... bands... should be a general strategy") is stated as the paper's organizing claim, with the specific PbTe1-xSex result offered as the validating demonstration -- the textbook idea-first pattern (a design proposition that could have been stated before the specific alloy work began, with the experiment following from it). I weighed the v1.1 "own established platform" tell against this: the group's own related PbTe paper is cited once ("High thermoelectric figure of merit in heavy hole dominated PbTe," 2011), which is a single self-citation, not the 4-8x pattern that would clinch problem-first via serial platform reuse. Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing the "general strategy" framing was added post hoc in the discussion while the actual research program was driven by incrementally improving zT in an already-established PbTe1-xSex system the authors had been using for other purposes (which would tip this to problem-first).

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a real and close alternative: the underlying alloy system (doped PbTe1-xSex) was already established, including by this same group, and the paper could be read as applying that established system to close a well-known open question ("how do we push zT past ~1.5") rather than as a fresh theoretical proposition. I could not distinguish these readings confidently from the abstract alone, which is exactly the case the v1.1 rules flag as the central unresolved catch-all tension in the codebook.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a strong worked example for issue #1 in the v1.1 changelog (the means-first/problem-first catch-all): the paper's rhetorical framing (a stated general design principle) points to idea-first, but the underlying materials system's history (an established alloy platform) points to problem-first, and abstract-only evidence genuinely cannot arbitrate between them.
