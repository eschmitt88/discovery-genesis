## Reasoning
The device described is genuinely an instrument-development paper: no single ingredient (Rb stabilization, SHG, EOM comb generation) is new to the field, but their specific combination to create a compact 1.55 μm multi-frequency Rb-referenced source is presented as the contribution, and the paper's entire logic runs capability (the assembled device) → observation (stability measurement) → claim (comb source works). Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing this exact combination had already been demonstrated by another group, which would push this toward gap-filling:next-target (established combined-technique applied to an incremental improvement) rather than a new instrument.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a plausible alternative if the underlying motivation (need for a stable 1.55 μm reference for CO2 lidar, per the atmospheric-sensing references) is read as the paper's real starting point, with the device assembly treated as routine engineering using already-established sub-techniques rather than a novel capability. Abstract-only evidence cannot fully distinguish "assembling known parts is the contribution" from "the parts already existed in combination and this closes an application gap."

## Notes for the codebook
This is a useful test of where the closed enabler vocabulary's new-instrument category meets recombination: the device is new, but every one of its components is old and established, raising the question of whether "new instrument" should require more than novel combination of existing parts.
