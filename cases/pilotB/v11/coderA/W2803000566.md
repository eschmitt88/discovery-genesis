## Reasoning
This is a genuinely borderline case between accretion and problem-first, which I resolved toward accretion for consistency with the codebook's explicit guidance that survey-wave/trend-report papers (naming ESPAD as an example alongside MMWR) default to accretion unless the dossier shows otherwise. The trend-description component (prevalence over six waves, gender-gap closure) fits the accretion test cleanly: its conclusion's form could largely be written before the 2015 numbers came in, with only the specific percentages left blank. Evidence that would change my mind: the paper's own hypothesis-driven multivariate regression of specific correlates (peer smoking, parental monitoring, family structure) genuinely goes beyond a bulletin-style trend report and tests specific, falsifiable relationships -- this is real tension with the accretion call, discussed below.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a strong alternative and, on balance, may be the better call: the regression analysis of correlates (2007-2015 waves) tests specific hypotheses about what drives the observed decline (access, monitoring, peer effects), using the existing survey-wave data as an ingredient closing a named analytic question rather than merely reporting an aggregate. Unlike its MMWR twin (W2806550153), this paper is a peer-reviewed secondary analysis, not the official ESPAD bulletin itself, and its correlates analysis has real explanatory ambition. I ultimately favored accretion because the codebook explicitly flags this genre, but I flag this as a genuinely close call.

## Notes for the codebook
This paper and its topic-pair twin (W2806550153, the official MMWR NYTS report) illustrate a real distinction the codebook's genre guidance may be collapsing: an official surveillance bulletin (MMWR) vs. a peer-reviewed secondary analysis built on a recurring survey (ESPAD-sourced but not itself the ESPAD report) can have different genesis logic even when both aggregate repeated survey waves. Worth considering whether "ESPAD" as a blanket accretion example should be narrowed to the official ESPAD reports themselves.
