## Reasoning
The abstract itself names the missing capability ("in the absence of lineage-tracing technology applicable to adult zebrafish") as the reason the question had remained unanswerable, and explicitly credits "new genetic fate-mapping approaches" for enabling the finding -- the clearest possible means-first signal, satisfying the v1.1 requirement that the capability be new to the field (adult-zebrafish-applicable lineage tracing did not exist before this work). Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing the fate-mapping reporter lines were substantially the same tools the Poss lab had already used in prior zebrafish regeneration papers (which would push toward problem-first via the "own platform" tell) rather than a genuine methodological first.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is the main alternative: the underlying zebrafish heart-regeneration model itself is well-established (Poss lab, 2002), and this could be read as that established experimental system extended with incremental fate-mapping refinements rather than a genuinely new capability. I judged means-first as more likely given the abstract's explicit "absence of technology... using new approaches" framing, but I cannot confirm from the abstract alone how novel the specific transgenic reporter lines were relative to the authors' own prior zebrafish work.

## Notes for the codebook
Good worked example for the means-first side of the v1.1 rule #1 catch-all: the "capability new to the field" criterion is satisfied by the abstract's own explicit statement that the enabling technology simply did not previously exist for this system, which is a stronger and more legible signal than the "own-platform" self-citation tell used for the problem-first side.
