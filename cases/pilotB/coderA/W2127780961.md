## Reasoning
Problem-first fits the decision order: a specific named evidence gap (routine-practice performance, interval safety) is closed with an ingredient that already existed — the co-testing program and its accumulated clinical data, not a new test or instrument. I did not choose means-first because, although the large database is doing the empirical work, the paper explicitly frames itself as answering a stated policy/practice question rather than as an incidental discovery enabled by data availability (contrast with W2015267536, where no such question was pre-named).

## Alternative reading
Means-first is a reasonable second reading: one could argue the sheer availability of a multi-hundred-thousand-woman co-testing database is itself the capability that made this analysis possible, and the "gap" language is standard framing added after the fact. Evidence tier (tldr-only, no full-text introduction) makes this hard to adjudicate confidently, hence the low confidence.

## Notes for the codebook
Another case where the machine-generated-abstract flag (`(via semanticscholar)`) forced a `tldr-only` tier even though the abstract itself is unusually long and structured (Background/Methods/Findings/Interpretation) — closer in informativeness to a real abstract than a typical TLDR one-liner. Worth the codebook clarifying whether the tier should track abstract *length/structure* rather than just its labelled source.
