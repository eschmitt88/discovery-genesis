## Reasoning
With title and reference list as the only real evidence, the case for problem-first is the pairing of an old, well-recognized clinical gap (no newborn screen for 22q11.2 deletion, syndrome long characterized since 1991–1993) with an assay (MLPA) that was itself established for this exact syndrome by 2002–2009 — not brand-new, but the paper's apparent innovation is redirecting it at a new, logistically convenient sample source (banked DBS) rather than fresh venous blood. That's a modest, forced-feeling move once the assay and the sample archive both already exist, which is why I lean problem-first over means-first (DBS as "resource" is old too, just not previously combined with this assay for this syndrome).

## Alternative reading
Second-most-likely: accretion. If the paper is read as primarily a validation/feasibility study (confirming an existing method works "well enough" on a suboptimal sample type, without a sharp novel claim), it would fit accretion — incremental, confirmatory, no single locatable idea. I did not choose this as primary because the title still implies a specific enabling combination (assay + DBS) aimed at a named screening goal, which tips it toward a locatable (if modest) move.

## Notes for the codebook
Another case starved of abstract/full-text evidence — genesis_confidence is capped at medium purely because of dossier thinness, not because the paper itself is ambiguous. The transfer/gap-filling double-label also shows a general schema friction: many clinical-application papers are simultaneously "move an existing method to a new substrate" (transfer) and "close a specific named clinical gap" (gap-filling), and the codebook doesn't clarify whether these should be ranked or treated as co-equal.
