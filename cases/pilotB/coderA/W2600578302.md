## Reasoning
The abstract opens with an explicit statement of what was not previously known (duration-by-genotype interaction) and answers it entirely from an already-existing trial's banked specimens and follow-up data — no new patients, no new assay, no new theoretical claim about mechanism. That combination (old, named prognostic-genotype problem; a specific, not-brand-new-but-recently-completed randomized trial as the enabling resource) fits problem-first, though the "recently available" ingredient here (trial completed ~2012, this analysis published 2017) is on the older edge of what the codebook's guide treats as recent.

## Alternative reading
Second-most-likely: accretion, if this is read simply as one more mutation-subgroup analysis in a long-running series of GIST genotype-prognosis papers (the reference list shows a dense, continuous stream of similar subgroup analyses from 2003–2015). I placed problem-first ahead of accretion because the abstract states a sharply specific, previously unaddressed interaction question (duration × genotype) rather than presenting itself as a general update or consolidation of the genotype-prognosis literature.

## Notes for the codebook
Secondary/exploratory analyses of already-completed randomized trials are common in clinical oncology and sit awkwardly between problem-first and accretion: the "ingredient" (the trial) is a resource that already existed for a different primary purpose, repurposed for a sharper question. The schema's `enabler` field captures this reasonably well (`resource`), but `genesis_model`'s problem-first criterion (ingredient ≤3y old) doesn't quite fit a 5-year-old completed trial being mined for a new question — a distinct "resource-repurposing" pattern might deserve its own guidance.
