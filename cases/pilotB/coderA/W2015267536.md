## Reasoning
Two aims are entangled here: a guideline-compliance audit (accretion-flavoured) and a mortality-association finding (the paper's actual headline). I did not choose accretion because the title/abstract foreground a specific, non-obvious empirical claim rather than a report-card of practice; the "form" of that claim (jaundice -> survival benefit) could not have been sketched with blanks left for numbers, since it runs against clinical intuition. I chose means-first over problem-first because there is no prior literature cited that frames "does jaundice predict survival in elderly sepsis" as an open question — the association emerges from having a large routine dataset to interrogate, which is the means-first signature (capability/data -> observation -> claim).

## Alternative reading
Accretion is plausible for the guideline-compliance half of the paper (comparing local practice against AGA standards, a report-card structure). If the paper were scored purely on that half, accretion with `problem: "none stated — audits LFT follow-up practice against AGA guidelines"` would apply. I weighted the title's headline claim more heavily since it is what the paper is named for.
