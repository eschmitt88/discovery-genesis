## Reasoning
Problem-first is clear-cut: a specific, explicitly named translational gap (no brain-wide human in vivo CSF tracer evidence) is closed using ingredients that already existed — an approved contrast agent, an established administration route, and standard imaging/segmentation software. No new instrument or assay is introduced; the novelty is in the question and its human/brain-wide scope, not in a new capability. I did not call this transfer because the source (animal neuroscience/physiology) and target (human neuroscience/physiology) share the same primary field — this is scale/translation within-field, closer to gap-filling with a resource-repurposing flavor.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is a plausible second reading if one emphasizes that gadobutrol's property of being confined to the extravascular compartment by an intact BBB is the capability that makes tracer imaging possible at all, and the paper is essentially "we have a tracer that can do this, let's see what it shows." I ranked problem-first higher because the introduction's framing is explicitly gap-naming rather than capability-announcing.

## Notes for the codebook
"resource" (unattested in the v1 pilot list) fits this paper well — an existing clinical diagnostic agent repurposed as a research tracer is a repeatable pattern distinct from "transfer" (no field-boundary crossing) and from generic "gap-filling" (the repurposed *resource*, not just the topic, is the notable move).
