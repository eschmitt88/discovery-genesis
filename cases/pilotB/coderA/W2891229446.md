## Reasoning
Straightforward problem-first: the specific question (RIG-I's role in gastric cancer) is named as unexplored, while the broader claim (RIG-I matters in cancer generally) is established via cited prior work. The tools used (IHC, RNAi, microarray) are standard and pre-existing, not new instruments, ruling out means-first. Move is gap-filling (closing an untested tissue-type gap) with a consolidation flavor since it adds one more cancer type to a pattern already seen elsewhere, rather than transfer (RIG-I/oncogenesis is within the same field, not imported across a field boundary).

## Alternative reading
Could be read as means-first if the Affymetrix microarray screen is treated as the driving capability that surfaced downstream pathway hits — but the paper's framing subordinates the microarray to a hypothesis-driven design (RIG-I knockdown -> phenotype -> pathway explanation), so problem-first remains the better fit.

## Notes for the codebook
Borderline transfer/recombination case worth flagging: RIG-I's role was established in "other cancers" (unspecified which) — same broad field (oncology), so recombination/gap-filling rather than transfer, but the dossier doesn't name which cancers, so this reading rests on an inference from the abstract's phrasing.
