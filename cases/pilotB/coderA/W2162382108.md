## Reasoning
This case has full text, and the introduction is unusually direct about the genesis: it names the exact missing data point (β-aryl ether linkage content of bark lignin) and proceeds straight to methods that are all decades old. There's no engineered anomaly, no new instrument, and no stated design proposition beyond "let's measure the thing nobody has measured for this material" — the accretion label (filling a known descriptive gap with mature technique) fits with high confidence given the explicit gap statement.

## Alternative reading
Second-most-likely: problem-first, if kenaf/mulberry/paper-mulberry's industrial relevance (fiber/paper feedstocks) is read as a recently sharpened applied problem that these older techniques were then aimed at. I rate this below accretion because nothing in the introduction frames the paper as responding to a recent external driver (e.g., a new biofuel or pulping application) — the framing is purely a descriptive-literature gap, not an applied urgency.
