## Reasoning
Per codebook decision order: not accretion (a specific trial result, not an aggregate); not means-first (no new capability created the finding — the covered stent and TIPS procedure both pre-date the trial, cited to 2004 and earlier); problem-first fits cleanly — the introduction and abstract name a precise open question (timing of TIPS in a specific high-risk subgroup) and the ingredient that answers it (covered TIPS) is an existing, previously validated technology. Reference list is dominated by 1999-2009 TIPS outcome/meta-analysis papers, consistent with an established (not brand-new) specific question. Evidence that would change my mind: if the introduction had framed the covered stent itself as newly available/experimental rather than an established option — it doesn't; PTFE-covered stents are cited to a 2004 randomized study of "improved clinical outcome."

## Alternative reading
Accretion is a distant second: this is a consensus-adjacent field (Baveno workshops cited) and the paper could be read as consolidating standard-of-care evidence. But the trial reports a genuinely new causal result (a specific RCT finding, P<0.001) rather than aggregating prior numbers, so accretion is not the better fit.

## Notes for the codebook
Clean case for the codebook's problem-first vs. accretion distinction — RCTs testing timing/sequencing of an already-approved intervention are a recurring, easy-to-classify problem-first pattern; may be worth naming as a canonical example.
