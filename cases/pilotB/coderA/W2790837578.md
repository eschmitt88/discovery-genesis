## Reasoning
The abstract is unusually explicit about the motivating proposition ("Using labels only prevents stakeholders from easily and quickly understanding...") stated before any description of the artefact, and the artefact (meta-model of actions + properties, RDF vocabulary) reads as the direct formal consequence of that proposition. This is the textbook idea-first pattern: a sentence that could have been written before the work began, with the design following from it. The move is unambiguously formalisation — turning an informal label into a structured, machine-processable construct.

## Alternative reading
Second-most-likely: problem-first, if one treats "capability" (from 1993 strategic-management literature) as an old problem newly meetable by relatively recent semantic-annotation machinery (SAWSDL 2007, WSMO-Lite 2014). This is less likely as the primary reading because the abstract's own framing centers on a stated conceptual claim about labels being insufficient, not on a recently-available technical ingredient making a long-standing question suddenly tractable.
