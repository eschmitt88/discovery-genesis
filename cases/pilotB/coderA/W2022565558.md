## Reasoning
The title's own emphasis — naming the deposition instrument before the material — combined with the reference list's split between the authors' own plasma-source engineering literature (a distinct application area, ion implantation, 1995–2004) and the separate Mg2Sn thermoelectric literature (1968–2011) points to an existing in-house instrument being pointed at a new material target, i.e. means-first: the film's properties are what the deposition method reveals, not a predicted outcome from a stated design principle. Confidence is medium rather than high because the abstract that would confirm this ordering is missing.

## Alternative reading
Second-most-likely: problem-first, if the paper is read as motivated primarily by a device-integration need (thermoelectric thin films for micro-generators, an established but device-relevant problem) that this specific plasma technique happened to be well-suited to solve. I placed means-first ahead of this because the title's framing puts the deposition method first, and the reference list shows the plasma-source technology predates this specific material application by 7–16 years — closer to "an existing capability redirected" than "a recently available ingredient meeting an old problem."

## Notes for the codebook
Materials-science instrument papers like this one are a recurring pattern in the batch (also W2318832362, W2162382108): the "ingredient" is often the authors' own previously-published apparatus, redirected at a new material. The schema's `enabler` vocabulary (new tool/data/compute/theory/collaboration) handles this reasonably, but distinguishing "genuinely new instrument" from "existing instrument redirected at a new target" (this case) would sharpen the means-first vs. transfer boundary, which currently has to be inferred from reference-list self-citation patterns rather than a schema field.
