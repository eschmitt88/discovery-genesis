## Reasoning
This is a harder call than most: the paper both names a specific problem (fragmented, incoherent existing frameworks) and creates something genuinely new (the COM-B/BCW synthesis) rather than deploying a single existing ingredient. I still assigned problem-first over idea-first because the design follows from a stated evaluative gap discovered via systematic review of existing frameworks (the "ingredient" being the pre-existing frameworks and behaviour theory, formalised into a new structure), rather than from an a priori proposition stated before the review began. The move is best read as formalisation (giving 19 informal, scattered frameworks one coherent formal representation) with a consolidation flavor.

## Alternative reading
Idea-first is the closer alternative here of any card in this batch: if the COM-B system was in fact the authors' starting proposition (motivating why existing frameworks fall short) rather than an output of the systematic review, this would be idea-first. The dossier's methods ordering (search frameworks -> evaluate against criteria -> develop new framework) reads as the former, so I kept problem-first, but this is genuinely closer to the boundary than the codebook's clean examples suggest.

## Notes for the codebook
This paper is a strong test of the idea-first vs. problem-first boundary for "synthesis that creates new theory" papers — the decision order's problem-first criterion #3 requires "the ingredient that closes it already existed," but here the closing device (COM-B/BCW) is new while its *raw material* (existing frameworks) is old. Worth an explicit codebook note on how to handle synthesis-that-creates-new-formalism cases specifically.
