## Reasoning
The genesis-deciding evidence is procedural, not conceptual: the authors built a triple-mutant to falsify the "three systems are exhaustive" assumption, observed unexplained residual growth, and then screened blindly for the responsible gene rather than starting from a hypothesis about T6SS/OMV/PQS convergence. That ordering — engineered anomaly, unbiased screen, mechanism reconstructed post hoc — is the textbook means-first pattern ("the idea is recognised in the data"). The move reads as anomaly-driven discovery that ends in a unification of previously separate subfields (secretion systems, vesicle biology, quorum sensing, siderophore receptors), hence the two move labels.

## Alternative reading
Second-most-likely: problem-first. The field had an established, named problem ("H2- and H3-T6SS function remains largely unknown") and the paper could be read as deploying an available technique (Tn5 screening, already decades old) against it. This is less likely as the primary framing because the paper's own narrative centers on the surprising phenotype (residual growth) as the trigger, not on a standing unresolved question that the authors set out to answer with a known-recent tool.

## Notes for the codebook
"Unification" isn't in the v0 list but is close to "consolidation"/"recombination"; I used `unification` per the schema's stated vocabulary since the paper's main payoff is literally connecting three separately-studied systems, which "recombination" (usually cross-domain borrowing) undersells. Screens-that-surface-a-mechanism are common in molecular biology and don't map cleanly onto "instrument" (no new physical tool) or "resource" (no new dataset) — `anomaly` was the best fit for the trigger, but the codebook could use an explicit "unbiased-screen" ingredient status/role.
