## Reasoning
This is about as clean an accretion case as the codebook describes: a "large trial of an established question" — here, a recurring official surveillance report rather than a trial — extending a standing time series by one wave, with no new instrument, dataset source, or theoretical claim. The tiny reference list (5 items) versus the size of the survey program itself is itself diagnostic of a report genre rather than a discovery-shaped paper.

## Alternative reading
Second-most-likely: problem-first, if one treats "how has the*e-cigarette* product mix changed" as the sharper, more recent question (e-cigarettes only entered NYTS in the 2010s) that the paper specifically closes. I rate this below accretion because the report's structure and framing treat all seven product categories symmetrically as part of routine monitoring, rather than being organized around a single sharpened question about e-cigarettes.

## Notes for the codebook
Institutional surveillance reports like this MMWR piece are a recognizable genre the codebook's guide anticipates ("a large trial of an established question... accretion") but the schema's `is_primary` field doesn't have a clean slot for "official recurring surveillance report" — I used `yes` because it is original data analysis, not review, but it isn't a typical "primary research paper" either. `problem_age` is also awkward: the underlying public-health problem (youth tobacco use) is old, but the specific reporting cadence resets every year, which the schema's fixed age buckets don't capture well.
