## Reasoning
Not accretion (a specific first-observation claim). Considered means-first because a fabrication technique (fs-laser waveguide writing) is central, but the paper's own framing is explicitly gap-closing ("has not been observed... we present the first...") rather than capability-driven exploration, and the same fabrication technique had already been used on Lieb lattices one year earlier (2014 ref) for a different observable (bulk/edge transport) — so the technique is not new here, only its targeted use is. That places it under problem-first per the decision order (means-first requires the capability itself to be the reason the finding exists; here the reason is a named unobserved state).

## Alternative reading
Means-first is a close second: one could argue the general capability of building flexible photonic lattices via fs-laser writing is what makes this whole line of flat-band photonics work possible, and this paper is one more capability-driven demonstration in that program. I ranked problem-first higher because of the explicit "has not been observed" framing.

## Notes for the codebook
Physics/photonics "first observation of a predicted state using an established platform" is a recurring pattern likely to recur at n=50 — worth watching whether it consistently resolves to problem-first or splits with means-first the way accretion/problem-first did in v0.
