## Reasoning
The abstract's own ordering — method combination stated first, findings following, and an explicit residual limitation named at the end — reads as a means-first paper: the two NMR techniques (one refined for quantification only a few years earlier) are the instrument that let the authors resolve a specific, previously-ambiguous structural question. This is not a case of a stated proposition preceding the work; it's characterization enabled by a sharper measurement tool applied to known material.

## Alternative reading
Second-most-likely: accretion. Given how mature both quantitative 13C NMR (since ~1985–2004) and lignin isolation protocols (since the 1950s) already were by 2011, one could read this as an incremental, "more precise version" characterization study rather than a genuinely new capability — a routine application of established techniques to yet another wood species/fraction combination. I favored means-first because the abstract explicitly ties the result (quantifying previously-ambiguous linkages) to the specific technique combination, but the line between "sharper instrument" and "just another careful characterization" is thin here.

## Notes for the codebook
Structural-characterization chemistry papers like this one repeatedly strain the move vocabulary: the "instrument" is really a combination of two established analytical methods rather than a single new tool, which fits `recombination` about as well as `instrument`; I used both. `problem_age` is also awkward — the specific named gap (γ-ester LCC quantification) is narrower and probably younger than the broad structural-characterization problem, but the schema only has one `problem_age` slot per card.
