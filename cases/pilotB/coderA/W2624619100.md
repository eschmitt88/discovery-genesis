## Reasoning
This is a humanities/STS paper coding prior art from natural science: the established problem is old (the human/animal/death boundary in continental philosophy, and biology's individuality assumptions), but what makes the paper possible now is a recent empirical development (phytoplankton PCD research clustering 2004–2014) that the author treats as newly available evidence for an old argument. That is the problem-first pattern almost by definition. I weighted this over accretion because the abstract signals a genuine argumentative move (biology's findings force a revision of an assumption) rather than a survey or consolidation.

## Alternative reading
Second-most-likely: idea-first. One could argue the paper's core proposition — that deconstructing microbial individuality supports an anti-anthropocentric account of death and time — was fully statable before engaging the specific 2004–2014 biology literature, since it follows from Derrida's existing corpus. I rate this less likely because the abstract's own ordering puts the biological revision first ("recent research suggests...") and the philosophical move second ("this article explores how... scientists change... this article explores how the deconstruction...").
