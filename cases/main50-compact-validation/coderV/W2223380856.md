## Reasoning
The abstract describes a specific hypothesized mediation structure (memorable experience to place attachment/place identity to behavioral intention) tested with survey data and SEM, which is a proposition that could have been stated before data collection and that the survey design directly follows — matching the codebook's idea-first criterion rather than a plain gap statement.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first/recombination is an equally strong alternative reading if one treats this as simply joining several already-established tourism-research constructs (memorable experience, place attachment, behavioral intention) via routine SEM survey methodology to close a straightforward "has anyone tested this combination for local food" gap, without a genuinely novel theoretical proposition; the abstract alone cannot fully distinguish a pre-registered specific hypothesis from a routine combinatorial study design, which is why confidence is kept at medium rather than high.

## Notes for the codebook
Survey-based tourism/consumer-behavior papers that test a named mediation model built from established scales sit exactly on the idea-first/problem-first boundary the codebook's rule 4 addresses ("a gap statement is not a proposition"); this is a useful non-STEM worked example for that boundary, since the field's SEM-mediation-model convention makes almost every such paper look like it states a testable proposition.
