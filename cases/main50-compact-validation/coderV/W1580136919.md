## Reasoning
The self-citation chain (2008, 2009, 2010) shows the authors had already published a specific, stated hypothesis — that acetaminophen's endocannabinoid activity could underlie neurodevelopmental harm — before running this experiment, and the abstract explicitly frames the study as a designed test of "a first step in establishing" that link, matching the codebook's idea-first criterion (a proposition stated before the work, with the design following from it) rather than a gap statement.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a plausible alternative if one treats "does acetaminophen's mechanism of action have neurotoxic potential" as an externally posed open question that the authors merely answered with a standard in vitro assay, but the tight self-citation chain to the authors' own specific prior claim tips this toward idea-first.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a useful idea-first worked example distinct from a "we hypothesised that..." boilerplate case: the proposition here is traceable to the authors' own named prior publications rather than being freshly stated in this paper's introduction, which the codebook's idea-first rule doesn't currently distinguish from in-paper-only hypotheses.
