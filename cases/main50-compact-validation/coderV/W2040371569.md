## Reasoning
The full text explicitly names and defines a new mechanism (FD-FWM) as the reason stable operation is achieved, with the paper's logic running directly from that new mechanism to the observed stability/linewidth results to the claim of a first demonstration — the textbook means-first shape. The four self-citations are to the authors' own resonator hardware papers (2008-2011), not to prior uses of this specific mode-locking scheme, so the mechanism itself is new even though the hardware platform is the authors' own.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first could apply if one treats the microring resonator hardware as the "old ingredient" and the paper as merely answering the established open question of stable high-repetition-rate mode-locking, but the paper's own framing credits a newly named mechanism (not just applying the resonator to yet another target) with solving the problem, so means-first is the better fit.

## Notes for the codebook
This case shows the means-first rule needs to look at what specifically is new (a mechanism/method) versus what is the authors' established resource (the hardware platform) — the two can be self-citation-linked differently, and the codebook's "capability new to the authors" tell should be read at the level of the specific technique, not just the underlying instrument.
