## Reasoning
The paper explicitly identifies a paradox in the authors' own earlier model (which the self-citations trace directly) and attributes its resolution to a new capability — systematic quantitative measurement plus a purpose-built Hele-Shaw cell that directly visualizes the freezing front — that had not been available before ("a lack of systematic experiments"). This capability-to-observation-to-claim structure is the means-first pattern even though the underlying scientific question is the authors' own.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a plausible alternative if the Hele-Shaw cell and angle-measurement protocol are read as routine experimental technique rather than a genuinely new capability, in which case this would be the authors closing their own previously-opened question with existing tools; the full text's emphasis on the *lack* of prior systematic experiments is what tips the reading toward means-first.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a good worked example of "authors resolve a paradox in their own earlier model via a new measurement capability" — a means-first pattern distinct from the more common "external capability applied to an old question" cases, and could sit alongside the codebook's existing means-first examples.
