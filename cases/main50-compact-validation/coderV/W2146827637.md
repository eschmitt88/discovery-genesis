## Reasoning
The self-citation lineage shows this group has an established pipeline for cloning and functionally testing individual defense genes in transgenic poplar (chitinase, WRKY22, PtoMYB216), and this paper applies that same pipeline to the next WRKY family member — the v1.1 next-target pattern rather than a new instrument or theory. The broader WRKY-SA-defense framework being extended to is itself well established from Arabidopsis and earlier Populus WRKY studies.

## Alternative reading
Idea-first is a weak alternative if the choice of PtoWRKY60 specifically (based on its phylogenetic closeness to Arabidopsis defense WRKYs) is read as testing a stated hypothesis, but the abstract's framing (routine characterization: expression profile, phylogeny, overexpression phenotype) reads as standard gene-function characterization rather than a stated proposition driving the design, so problem-first fits better.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a very common genre in plant molecular biology — "characterize the next paralog/ortholog of an already-studied gene family in a new species" — and is functionally similar to the next-target pattern seen in other single-gene functional-characterization papers in this batch; a family-level worked example (WRKY, MYB, etc.) could help distinguish it from genuinely novel network-mapping papers.
