## Reasoning
The self-citation cluster (2005, 2009, 2010, 2014) shows this paper reuses the authors' own established mechanical-texture and phenolic-profiling methodology, previously applied to grape ripening, now redirected at a different but related question (noble rot progression) — the v1.1 next-target pattern rather than a new instrument. The specific problem (lack of objective noble-rot markers) was already framed by other groups' work on grey rot vs noble rot (2004), with the authors supplying an established toolkit as the closing ingredient.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is a weak alternative if the specific combination of puncture+compression+colour is read as a new integrated assay, but each component technique is individually established and self-cited by the authors from ripening work, so problem-first fits better.

## Notes for the codebook
This "own established assay, redirected from one biological process (ripening) to an adjacent one (fungal infection)" pattern is a slightly different flavor of next-target than pure species/population substitution, and could use its own worked example in the codebook.
