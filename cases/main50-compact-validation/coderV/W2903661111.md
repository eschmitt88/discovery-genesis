## Reasoning
The paper joins two already-established techniques — standard pET-vector recombinant cloning and solid-lipid-particle encapsulation (previously validated on unrelated small-molecule drugs) — and applies both, for the first time, to the authors' own previously characterized enzyme; no new instrument or theory is introduced, matching problem-first/recombination rather than means-first. The single self-citation anchors the enzyme itself as the authors' established prior finding, not the delivery technology.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is a plausible surface reading given the heavy characterization/technique language (SEM, FT-IR, TGA, DPV), but each individual technique is applied off-the-shelf to a new pairing (this enzyme plus this delivery vehicle) rather than any one being newly developed here, so the recombination reading is the better fit.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a common biotech pattern — take an established delivery/formulation technology from one class of molecules (small-molecule drugs) and apply it for the first time to a different class (a specific therapeutic enzyme) — that sits between recombination and next-target; the codebook could use a clearer rule for when a "first application of established tech X to specific molecule Y" counts as recombination versus next-target.
