## Reasoning
This is an event-driven paper: the airburst itself is what is new, not any single method, and the paper's logic runs from many pre-existing instruments and analytical techniques (camera trajectory triangulation, isotope chronology, infrasound energy estimation) converging on one unprecedented occurrence — the v1.1 problem-first criterion (old ingredients, a newly opened specific question). The heavy SELF-citation count reflects the huge multi-institution author list contributing their own established sub-specialty methods, not a single new capability.

## Alternative reading
Accretion is a weak second reading if one views this as primarily a survey-style compilation of many pre-collected observations, but the abstract's framing ("unprecedented measurements were made") and the physical meteorite recovery/dating work indicate genuine new primary data collection, not aggregation of existing literature, so problem-first fits better.

## Notes for the codebook
A single-event, many-instrument disaster-characterization paper does not map cleanly onto any one move label; "gap-filling:first-measurement" (first comprehensive characterization of this event) captures the core but the multi-disciplinary synthesis (astronomy + seismology + meteoritics + social science) also has a real recombination flavor that a single label undersells.
