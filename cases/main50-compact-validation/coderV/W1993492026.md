## Reasoning
The abstract's self-description as introducing "a novel procedure" is directly complicated by the reference list itself, which shows an essentially identically named and structured "convergence seminar" method already published in 2009 for a different technology (nanobiotechnology) — evidence internal to the dossier outweighs the abstract's own novelty claim, pointing to problem-first (established deliberation method, new topic domain) rather than means-first or idea-first.

## Alternative reading
Means-first remains plausible if the 2012 paper's specific seminar design or its explicit grounding in "hypothetical retrospection" constitutes a meaningfully new procedural capability relative to the 2009 nanobiotechnology seminars, in which case the abstract's own framing would be the more reliable signal; this is why confidence is kept low rather than resolved either way.
