## Reasoning
The abstract's own explicit flagging of the resource-selection-function method as "traditionally used for habitat and food selection" signals a deliberate methodological recombination — an established ecological analysis technique brought into marine-debris research — applied to a question (why do turtles selectively ingest certain debris) that has been open and hypothesized about since at least 1987-1990. Since habitat/foraging ecology and marine-debris ecology sit within the same broad field (wildlife/conservation ecology), this is recombination rather than transfer per the codebook's field-boundary test.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is a plausible alternative if the RSF/Manly's-ratio approach is treated as a genuinely new capability that makes the finding possible (rigorous selectivity testing against measured environmental availability, not previously done in this literature), but because the method itself long predates this paper (1985) and its novelty lies in the pairing with a specific old question rather than the method's own newness, problem-first/recombination is the better fit.
