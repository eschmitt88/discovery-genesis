## Reasoning
Idea-first fails cleanly: the paper states a descriptive study objective, not a proposition. Means-first also fails since intranasal fentanyl and its route of administration are well-established and used exactly as in prior pain-management literature; nothing new is introduced methodologically. Problem-first is the clean fit: a narrow, explicitly acknowledged evidence gap (INF for respiratory distress specifically, versus the much larger literature on INF for pain) is addressed by applying the existing drug/route to a broader cohort via routine retrospective chart review — classic gap-filling:next-target, where the "target" is a clinical indication (respiratory distress) rather than a species or population.

## Alternative reading
No other label is competitive. This is a straightforward case, consistent with several other retrospective-cohort / registry-style papers in this batch.

## Notes for the codebook
Another instance of gap-filling:next-target where the target shift is *indication* (pain → respiratory distress) rather than population, species, or region — worth noting as yet another variant, alongside age-subgroup (Galectin-3/BNP paper) and vaccine-formulation (cholera MBC paper) variants surfacing elsewhere in this batch.
