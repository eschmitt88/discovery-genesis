## Reasoning
Idea-first fails outright: there is no proposition, only a characterization objective. The real contest is means-first vs. problem-first, since the analytical method (pan-genome analysis) is pre-existing (developed for other bacterial species) while the genome collection itself is new and substantially larger than any prior comparative set for this species. I lean means-first because the paper's own narrative repeatedly attributes its specific new findings (pan-genome growth curve, quantified cross-species recombination, resolution of six lineages) to the scale and taxonomic breadth of this new dataset relative to the prior 17-strain benchmark — the classic "new-dataset-or-resource" means-first pattern (capability → observation → claim), similar to the E. coli whole-genome mutation-rate paper elsewhere in this batch.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a strong alternative: the pan-genome method itself long predates this paper, and one could argue the "capability" here is not new to the field, just applied at greater scale to the same species — which is exactly the gap-filling:bigger-n pattern. I recorded this as a tie given how finely balanced it is.

## Notes for the codebook
