## Reasoning
Under the reversed cascade, idea-first gets first hearing: there is a real candidate proposition ("carabrone acts on the mitochondria"), traceable to the authors' own 2017 companion paper on mitochondrial respiratory-chain effects. But that prior finding is itself very recent (same year) and empirical, not a standing theoretical claim the authors could have written down independent of any prior data — it reads more as a working hypothesis derived from pilot observations than as the organizing proposition of a genuinely idea-first paper. The stronger, better-evidenced story is means-first: the abstract is explicit that a fluorescent conjugate and an antibody were purpose-built ("thus designed and synthesized," "prepared") because existing tools could not answer the localization question, and the finding (Pearson's colocalization coefficients) directly follows from deploying those new tools. This is the paradigm means-first case the codebook describes: capability → observation → claim.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a real contender (a specific mechanistic question is named as unresolved), but per v1.1 the ingredient must already exist for problem-first — here the key ingredients (FC, antibody) are new-here, which pushes this out of problem-first and into means-first.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a good test case for the reversed procedure: idea-first "gets heard first" and has genuine partial support (a plausible pre-existing hypothesis), but the evidence tips clearly to means-first once you ask what specifically made the finding possible. The reversal did not manufacture a false idea-first here — worth flagging as a paper where idea-first was seriously considered and rejected on the merits, not skipped.
