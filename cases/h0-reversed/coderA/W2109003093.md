## Reasoning
Idea-first fails cleanly: the abstract's own framing ("we document the account of what happened") is explicitly descriptive, not propositional — there is no claim here that could have been written down before 15 February 2013. The real contest is means-first vs. problem-first. I lean means-first because the abstract makes the capability claim itself the headline of the paper's justification ("Because it occurred in an era with..."), which is exactly the means-first logic (capability → observation → claim) rather than "a standing question about NEO hazards, closed with old ingredients." The pre-existing question (understanding/calibrating airburst hazard) is real and could support problem-first, but the specific measurements reported (precise trajectory from citizen video, energy from global infrasound, isotope-based provenance) are framed by the authors as possible *because of* the recent maturity/density of these capabilities, not because a long-standing method was simply pointed at a new target.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a close second and is noted as a tie: near-Earth object hazard characterization is a genuinely old (>15y, since at least 1990s Spaceguard-era) open question, and most individual techniques used (infrasound networks since ~2009, isotope geochemistry decades old) are themselves pre-existing "ingredients" rather than novel capabilities built for this paper — which is the classic problem-first signature. The deciding factor for me was the abstract's own explicit capability-framing sentence.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a genuinely hard case for the means-first vs. problem-first boundary as extended to "society-level capability" (a swell in ambient consumer electronics) rather than an authors'-own or field's own instrument. The v1.1 rule is written for instruments/assays/datasets the *authors* or *field* adopt; it does not clearly address an ambient societal capability (smartphone/dashcam saturation) that no single field or author group "acquired." Recommend the codebook explicitly address this case — whether ambient/societal technology diffusion counts as a "capability new to the field" for means-first purposes.
