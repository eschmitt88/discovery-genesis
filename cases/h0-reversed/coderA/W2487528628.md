## Reasoning
This is the strongest idea-first candidate encountered in this batch. Idea-first, heard first under the reversed cascade, holds up under real scrutiny: unlike the "we hypothesized that..." boilerplate seen elsewhere in this sample, this paper cites a genuinely specific, decades-old, published proposition (chromosome 21 dosage of interferon receptors is sufficient to confer enhanced interferon sensitivity, Tan et al. 1974 and Slate et al. 1978) that predates the work by roughly four decades and that the entire experimental design — testing this claim comprehensively across fibroblasts, lymphoblastoid cells, monocytes, and T cells, then tracing functional consequences — is organized around confirming and extending. This is meaningfully different from a "gap statement": it is an actual prior claim about how the world is, not merely an acknowledgment that something is unknown. Problem-first is an equally well-evidenced alternative reading (an old, specific, largely unexamined question closed with existing genomics tools), which is why I recorded a tie; per the reversed-order tiebreak rule, idea-first is picked.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is the closest competitor and, under the standard (non-reversed) cascade, would likely have been the assigned label, since the tools used (RNA-seq, pathway analysis, shRNA screening) are all pre-existing and even the authors' own established platform. The genesis_tie reflects that this could reasonably go either way; a coder emphasizing the tools over the motivating claim would land on problem-first.

## Notes for the codebook
This card is offered as a candidate worked example for the idea-first/problem-first boundary when the "proposition" in question is a genuine, specific, old, third-party claim from the literature (not the authors' own restated hypothesis). The current idea-first definition ("a proposition... that the AUTHORS could have written down before doing the work") is ambiguous about whether the proposition must originate with these authors or may be inherited from decades-old prior literature that the design directly tests — this case turns on resolving that ambiguity, and the codebook should state its position explicitly given how rare (1/192 under the standard cascade) idea-first calls are.
