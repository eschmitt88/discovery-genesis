## Reasoning
Idea-first fails outright — there is no proposition, only a mapping objective for a defined study area. Means-first is ruled out just as clearly: GIS and AHP are both decades-old, standard, off-the-shelf tools, and the introduction's own extensive literature review lists dozens of near-identical prior applications of the same method to other watersheds around the world. This is among the cleanest gap-filling:next-target (next-region variant) cases in the batch — an established methodology applied, largely unmodified, to a new geographic target where it had not yet been used.

## Alternative reading
Accretion was considered given how templated and widely-replicated this genre of paper is (comparable to the mitogenome paper in this batch), but the paper performs original spatial data collection/processing and produces a genuinely new map with field-validated accuracy for a specific area, rather than aggregating already-published results — so problem-first remains the better fit.

## Notes for the codebook
A strong cross-domain parallel to the Erannis ankeraria mitogenome paper (W2963245931) in this same batch: both are "established method applied to a not-yet-covered target" papers in fields (geospatial hydrology, insect mitogenomics) with very high replication rates of an identical template. Worth flagging as a recurring pattern across STEM subfields when the taxonomy discussion turns to how common gap-filling:next-target is at scale.
