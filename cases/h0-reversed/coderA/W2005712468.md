## Reasoning
Under the reversed hearing, idea-first is a real contender because a specific antecedent hypothesis (plastic resembles jellyfish) already existed and partially organizes the analysis (particularly the finding that benthic turtles selectively ingest soft clear plastic). But the paper's central methodological move — importing resource selection functions from habitat/food-selection ecology to rigorously test debris selectivity against measured availability — is the better-evidenced organizing contribution, and it addresses an explicitly named gap in prior debris-ingestion methodology (no availability-normalization, no life-stage comparison) rather than following from a single stated proposition. This is recombination as move (RSF from one ecological subfield brought into another) with problem-first as the genesis.

## Alternative reading
Idea-first is recorded as the tie: a coder weighting the jellyfish-resemblance hypothesis as the paper's true organizing claim (since it is explicitly confirmed for benthic turtles) would reasonably call this idea-first. I favored problem-first because the paper tests several open questions (species differences, life-stage differences, color preference, buoyancy) beyond just the jellyfish hypothesis, and its most distinctive contribution is methodological (the RSF framework itself).
