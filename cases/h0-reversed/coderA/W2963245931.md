## Reasoning
Idea-first is a non-starter under any hearing order: nothing here is a proposition, and the phylogenetic result is explicitly reported as confirming, not challenging, prior topology. Means-first also fails clearly since the method is textbook routine (a commercial sequencing service plus standard pipeline), used near-identically across a stack of cited precedent single-species mitogenome papers with matching phrasing — the tell for problem-first (a stable method applied to a new target) could not be more explicit here. Problem-first (specifically gap-filling:first-measurement — a mitogenome measured for the first time in this species) is the clear label.

## Alternative reading
Accretion was considered given the extreme routineness (borderline "database entry" character — one more entry in a large, template-driven single-species mitogenome literature), but the paper still performs original sequencing and a genuine (if confirmatory) phylogenetic analysis on newly generated data, rather than aggregating already-existing records, so problem-first is the better fit under this codebook's definitions.

## Notes for the codebook
This paper is a good illustration of how routine "next-species" mitogenome papers sit right at the problem-first/accretion boundary — the near-verbatim templated structure across four+ cited sibling papers ("It encoded 13 PCGs, 22 tRNA genes...and were similar with related reports before") could tempt a coder toward accretion on stylistic grounds alone. Worth a worked example distinguishing "routine but primary" (this paper: new sequence, new specimen) from true accretion (a database or review aggregating many such sequences).
