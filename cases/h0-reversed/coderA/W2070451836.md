## Reasoning
Idea-first fails: the paper explicitly frames its purpose as an investigation ("we aim to investigate"), not a proposition, and states no prediction of what the artifacts would be before running the experiments. Means-first fails equally clearly: every dye, instrument, and organism used is a standard, off-the-shelf choice, and the entire premise of the paper is that ordinary practitioners already use exactly this combination routinely. What remains is problem-first: a specific, well-posed validity question about an established method, closed with nothing but that method's own standard components used more carefully. The genre here (methodological critique/validation of routine practice) is a distinct pattern from the more common "established method applied to new target" gap-filling cases in this batch, but the underlying genesis logic is the same — pre-existing ingredients close a pre-existing (if under-examined) question.

## Alternative reading
Accretion was considered because the paper's rhetorical stance is corrective/consolidating rather than discovery-oriented, and it synthesizes several previously scattered anecdotal reports (SYTO9 not staining some Gram-negatives, yellow fluorescence artifacts, etc.) into a coherent systematic account. But the paper generates substantial new controlled comparative data (live/dead mixture titrations, bleaching time-courses, background-subtraction curves) rather than aggregating pre-existing results, so problem-first fits the codebook's definitions better than accretion.

## Notes for the codebook
Methodological-validation/critique papers (re-examining an established assay's accuracy) are a genre this codebook's examples don't explicitly cover; they sit closer to problem-first than to accretion because they generate new primary comparative data, but they lack the usual gap-filling:next-target flavor (no new target population/species — the "gap" is epistemic/methodological rather than empirical). A move label a bit more precise than "consolidation" (e.g., "gap-filling:other" with a methodological-validation note) might be worth adding.
