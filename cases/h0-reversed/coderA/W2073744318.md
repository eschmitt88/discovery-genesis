## Reasoning
This is one of the rare cases in this batch where accretion, heard last under the reversed cascade, is nonetheless the best fit. Idea-first and means-first both fail outright — there is no proposition and no new capability; the paper's own framing is explicitly that of filling a documentation gap ("there are few resources available... to address this deficit"), and its content (a checklist-style compilation of practical tips, each tied to prior technical papers or the authors' own experience) matches the codebook's accretion test almost exactly: the conclusion's *form* (a structured list of considerations) could have been outlined before any specific content was filled in.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first was the closer competitor: one could argue the "gap" (lack of a practical resource) is a specific pre-existing problem closed by pre-existing ingredients (the individual technical findings cited throughout, e.g. on hair artifacts or expression control). I recorded this as a tie, but ultimately classified it as accretion because the paper's own genre and self-description (a "report" and "practical guide," explicitly not a primary research study with a tested question) is closer to `is_primary: no — guideline/practical-guide` than to a primary empirical contribution, and the codebook explicitly directs guideline-type genres toward accretion by default.

## Notes for the codebook
This card is the clearest accretion case in the batch and a useful worked example: it demonstrates that the reversed cascade (idea-first first, accretion last) does NOT bias a coder away from correctly identifying a true accretion paper when the evidence is unambiguous — idea-first and means-first are dispatched on their own lack of merit, not by exhaustion of the ordering. Also worth noting: this dossier illustrates the `is_primary: no — <genre>` boundary extending naturally to "practical guide" as a genre alongside review/guideline/consensus-statement/essay already listed in the codebook.
