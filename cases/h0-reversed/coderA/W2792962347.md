## Reasoning
Idea-first does not hold under the reversed hearing: the paper investigates an actively-debated open question using a combination of techniques rather than testing one antecedent proposition stated in advance. The real competition is problem-first vs. means-first. I favor problem-first because cell-type-specific optogenetics, while central to the demonstration, is a well-established neuroscience technique by 2018 (Cre-driver lines, ChR2 constructs are standard tools, not introduced or substantially extended by this paper) — the old, specific, long-debated question (inhibition's role in preBötC rhythmogenesis) is what the paper closes, using existing tools applied with unusual rigor (combining in vitro and in vivo, targeting three distinct genetically-defined populations).

## Alternative reading
Means-first is a real alternative and recorded as the tie: one could argue that cell-type-specific optogenetic access to the preBötC's inhibitory population specifically is what finally made this long-standing question answerable, making the capability (rather than the question's age) the operative reason the finding exists. This would be a stronger read if the dossier showed this lab introducing or first applying this technique to the preBötC.

## Notes for the codebook
A good example of the "long-debated question, closed with an established (if relatively young, ~10y) technology" pattern — distinguishable from the true means-first cases in this batch (e.g., the carabrone fluorescent-conjugate paper) where the enabling tool is built fresh within the paper itself, versus here where the tool (optogenetics) predates the paper by a decade and its application to this exact circuit is not claimed as novel.
