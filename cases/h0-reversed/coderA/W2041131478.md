## Reasoning
Even hearing idea-first first, the "we hypothesized" sentence does not survive scrutiny as a genuine proposition: it is a direct, unsurprising extrapolation from already-published prevalence data in comparable settings (Papua New Guinea, Gambia, Kenya), and the study design (apply an existing instrument to an unmeasured population) does not follow from the hypothesis so much as simply test it via the most direct possible measurement. This is exactly the "v0 let 'we hypothesised that…' boilerplate count" pitfall the codebook warns against. Means-first fails outright — the instrument is old, standard, and even donated. Problem-first is clean and well-evidenced: a specific, explicitly stated data gap (no Zambian site; adults understudied) is closed with existing technology.

## Alternative reading
Accretion was briefly considered given the descriptive, prevalence-survey character of the paper, but the cross-sectional design collects genuinely new primary data from a defined cohort with inferential testing (one-sample proportion tests, Kruskal-Wallis), rather than aggregating pre-existing records — so problem-first remains the better fit.
