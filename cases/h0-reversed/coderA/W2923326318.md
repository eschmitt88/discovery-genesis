## Reasoning
Idea-first fails outright: there is no proposition, only a factor-significance question tested via regression on an existing algorithm's output. Means-first also fails cleanly under v1.1's explicit rule, since the paper itself states the localization algorithm is "previously developed" — this is exactly the "serial reuse of the authors' own established platform" pattern the codebook names, reinforced by four-plus self-citations of the algorithm's development across 2011-2015. The title itself ("Further Evidence of...") signals a bigger-n confirmatory study. Problem-first is the clean, high-confidence label even under the reversed hearing order.

## Alternative reading
Accretion is not plausible — this is inferential statistical work on a specific new cohort with a genuine analytic question, not a passive aggregation.

## Notes for the codebook
Clean confirmation of the v1.1 self-citation rule working as intended — this is precisely the case the rule was written to catch, and the reversed cascade doesn't disturb the result because means-first and idea-first both fail on their own merits rather than losing a tiebreak.
