## Reasoning
Idea-first fails: there is no proposition about the world stated in advance; the paper's motivating move is identifying and relaxing an acknowledged simplifying assumption (infinite N) in existing theory. Means-first also fails since the central modeling tool (the biophysical, near-parameter-free fitness landscape) is explicitly cited as "a previously developed approach" — the authors' own prior work, not a new capability introduced here. Problem-first is the clean fit: a specific, well-articulated theoretical gap is closed by combining pre-existing analytical and computational tools, incidentally revealing a new named phenomenon (lethal isolation) as a consequence.

## Alternative reading
No serious alternative competes. This is comparable to other "relax a known simplifying assumption in an established theory" cases, a distinct sub-pattern of problem-first from the more common "established method applied to a new empirical target."

## Notes for the codebook
Worth flagging as a theoretical/computational-biology instance of gap-filling:other where the "gap" is an acknowledged mathematical simplifying assumption in prior theory (N=∞) rather than an empirical population or system never previously studied — a useful category distinction from the empirical next-target cases dominating this batch.
