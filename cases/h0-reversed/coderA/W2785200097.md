## Reasoning
Idea-first gets a genuine, serious hearing under the reversed cascade: the introduction states a testable hypothesis before describing methods, and the whole pipeline is built to test it. But on inspection, that hypothesis ("more data closes the gap to specialist-level accuracy") is not new to this paper — it directly restates and extends a pattern already demonstrated in two cited papers (retinopathy 2016, skin cancer 2017); the design does not follow from an authors'-original proposition so much as from replicating an established result on a data-starved target. Means-first is the stronger read: the paper's own framing puts a specific, newly built capability (the automated R-CNN dataset-construction pipeline) forward as the explicit mechanism that makes the finding possible, and the discussion claims this pipeline itself as the paper's methodological novelty ("no previous reports on the fast generation of large skin-disease datasets using R-CNN").

## Alternative reading
Recorded genesis_tie: idea-first because the hypothesis-first framing is unusually explicit here compared to other papers in this batch — a coder applying the reversed cascade less conservatively could reasonably land on idea-first. I chose means-first because the specific claim tested was not original to this paper (it was already established for other conditions), which is exactly the codebook's distinction between a genuine prior proposition and a "we hypothesized..." restatement of prior art.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a strong test case for the reversed-cascade's core question: an explicit "we hypothesized/designed this study to investigate whether X" sentence is much more idea-first-flavored language than most papers in this sample, and the codebook's guidance ("a gap statement is not a proposition... v0 let 'we hypothesised that…' boilerplate count") is directly on point here — worth keeping as a canonical example of hypothesis-flavored language that should NOT default to idea-first when the underlying claim was already established elsewhere.
