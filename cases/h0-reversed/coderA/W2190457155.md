## Reasoning
Idea-first fails under any hearing order: the paper's organizing move is a confound-separation design, not a proposition about the world stated in advance. Means-first also fails clearly — the chemostat approach used to separate temperature from growth rate is the authors' own well-established platform (five self-citations spanning 2009-2014), squarely matching the v1.1 serial-reuse rule. The specific, named ambiguity ("the effect of mild hypothermia on post-translation cellular events remains poorly understood," and prior work "has revealed" the temperature/growth-rate confound without resolving it for ERAD specifically) is a textbook problem-first case: a precise pre-existing question closed with pre-existing tools.

## Alternative reading
None of the other three labels is close. Accretion is clearly wrong (this is inferential experimental biology, not aggregation); means-first is the nearest miss but is ruled out decisively by the self-citation evidence.

## Notes for the codebook
Good confirmation case for the v1.1 self-citation rule in a different domain (bioprocess engineering) than the rule's original examples (assay/platform reuse in molecular biology) — supports generalizing the rule across fields.
