## Reasoning
Q_problem is the only clear "yes": the introduction states the scale exists and is being applied to a population (Coronel Oviedo diabetic outpatients) it had not been used on. Q_means is "no" because the REGICOR/Framingham instrument (ref. [2006], cited as the basis) predates this paper by a decade and is used off the shelf — it is not new to the authors or the field. Q_idea is "no": nothing in the abstract or introduction reads as a proposition that could have been written before the data was collected; it is a measurement exercise. Q_accretion is "no": this is one hospital's cross-sectional cohort, not an aggregation of existing studies. Under the reversed order the strongest (and only "yes") answer is problem-first, so the order never has to break a tie.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is the only plausible second reading, if one treats "applying REGICOR to a diabetic population for the first time" as capability-driven. But the capability (the scale itself) is not new to the field or the authors' setting in any meaningful sense — it is routine clinical epidemiology tooling used exactly as designed. The v1.1 rule (capability new to authors/field) rules this out.

## Notes for the codebook
Clean, low-ambiguity case: single-site descriptive risk-scoring study. The four-question protocol converged immediately (one "yes", three "no"), so the reversed order made no practical difference here — worth noting for H0 that some papers are simply not close calls.
