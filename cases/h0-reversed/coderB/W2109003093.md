## Reasoning
Q_means is the clearest "yes" and is stated almost verbatim in the abstract: the capability (ubiquitous modern recording/sensing/lab technology) is named explicitly as the reason the unprecedented measurement set exists, with an implicit contrast to Tunguska's undocumented 1908 event. That is the textbook means-first pattern (capability → observation → claim). Q_accretion is a real but secondary consideration: the paper's organizing structure — "document the account... using comprehensive data obtained from [six named domains]" — reads like an aggregate compilation, and I rated it "partial" rather than "no." But two things keep it below means-first: the abstract stresses *why the data could be gathered at all* (the capability argument) more than it stresses *the aggregation as the contribution*, and the paper delivers substantive first-order findings (not merely summarized existing knowledge). Q_problem is "no" because no specific pre-existing open question is named — the near-Earth-object hazard-mitigation angle is stated as an implication of the findings, not the motivating gap. Q_idea is "no" — there is no proposition, only a stated documentation purpose.

## Alternative reading
Accretion is the genuine alternative and, under a stricter reading of "the aggregate itself," could be argued as primary: this is fundamentally a large coordinated compendium of independently collected data streams about one event, closer in spirit to a comprehensive report/database than to a hypothesis-driven study. I kept it at "partial" rather than promoting it because the abstract does not present the value as being the aggregation per se, but as the capability that made the aggregation — and the underlying scientific findings — possible in the first place; and because accretion's canonical form (survey/review/trend-report/standard) does not fit a first-hand, one-time observational campaign on a novel physical event.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a genre the current four labels handle awkwardly: a large-team, opportunistic, multi-domain characterization of a singular unrepeatable natural event, motivated by "we now have the instrumentation to do this properly" rather than by a stated open question or a stated proposition. It sits at the seam between means-first and accretion in a way the codebook's accretion test (review/survey/database/standard) doesn't quite anticipate — worth a worked example if similar "landmark natural event, comprehensively instrumented" papers recur in later batches.
