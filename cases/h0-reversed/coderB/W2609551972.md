## Reasoning
Q_problem is the strongest signal: the "NEW FEATURES AND UPDATES" section is organized as a sequence of named, specific weaknesses ("a continuing challenge," implicitly the limits of the three prior A-domain prediction methods) each closed by importing an algorithm that was already published by another group (CASSIS, ClusterFinder, and — per the abstract — SANDPUMA). Q_means only reaches "partial": these algorithms are new to the antiSMASH pipeline and to these authors' tool, but the v1.1 rule requires novelty "to the authors or the field," and the field already had these methods published separately — the contribution here is integration into a widely used platform, not invention. Q_accretion also only reaches "partial": there is a real incremental, versioned-release character (this is explicitly the fourth in a series), but the paper's substance is methodological integration solving named problems, not a summary/aggregation of prior findings, so I did not let the release-note form alone push it to a full "yes." Q_idea is "no" — nothing resembling a proposition organizes the design. With problem-first the only clean "yes" and accretion only partial (and lowest priority under the reversed order in any case), problem-first is the assignment.

## Alternative reading
Accretion is the more interesting alternative than usual for this genre: a tool paper that mainly reports "here is what's new in this version" has real family resemblance to a trend report, and if this were the fifth or sixth such release with diminishing novel content, I would weight accretion higher. Means-first is a weaker alternative — it would require crediting antiSMASH itself as a "new capability," but the platform predates this paper by six years and is treated throughout as established infrastructure being incrementally improved, not introduced.

## Notes for the codebook
Software/tool-update papers are a recurring genre the four-way cascade handles awkwardly: they are `is_primary: partial`, structurally resemble accretion (a versioned, incremental report) while substantively resembling problem-first (each feature closes a named, external, pre-existing gap via an already-published external algorithm). A dedicated worked example for "platform version-release paper" would help future coders separate the release-note *form* from the problem-first *content*.
