## Reasoning
This is a genuine tie under the reversed protocol. Q_idea is satisfiable because the introduction states, before presenting any result, the specific mechanism-claim the paper is built to test: that AI-generated datasets (via R-CNN) can resolve the data-scarcity bottleneck and let a CNN reach specialist-level (or better) accuracy — a proposition that organizes every design choice that follows. Q_means is equally satisfiable because that same sentence is, read the other way, a capability→observation→claim structure: build a new dataset-construction capability (R-CNN pipeline, novel in scale and automation for this application, new-here), observe the resulting classifier's accuracy, and claim superiority. Because the "idea" and the "means" are the same sentence read from two directions, I could not honestly separate them into a clear single strongest label — hence `genesis_tie: means-first`. Per the reversed decision order (idea-first considered first), I assign idea-first as the primary label. Q_problem is only partial: the dataset-scarcity obstacle is named, but what closes it is new-here, not a pre-existing ingredient, so a pure problem-first reading is not well supported. Q_accretion is clearly "no."

## Alternative reading
Means-first (the noted tie) is at least as defensible a primary label: R-CNN's specific *use* here — as an automated large-scale data-curation instrument rather than as a diagnostic detector itself — is the concrete novel capability, and it is what makes the accuracy claim possible at all. A stricter reading of Q_idea might also downgrade it: "investigate whether AI can achieve X by resolving Y" is phrased as a question, and the codebook explicitly warns that "a gap statement is not a proposition" — one could argue this sentence is closer to a research question than a risked claim, in which case means-first alone would win outright. I did not take that stricter reading because the sentence names a specific causal mechanism (dataset deficiency resolved by R-CNN → specialist-level accuracy), not merely an unfilled gap.

## Notes for the codebook
This paper is a strong example of why the reversed order matters: under the standard cascade (means-first before idea-first), this card would likely have been filed as means-first without ever seriously testing Q_idea, because means-first is satisfied cleanly and the cascade stops there. Independent, fair-hearing scoring surfaces that the "idea" and the "means" are the same design decision viewed from two angles — worth a codebook note on how to break such self-referential ties (capability-as-the-content-of-the-proposition) rather than always defaulting to means-first.
