## Reasoning
Q_problem carries this card: the Background is explicit that the assay is "routinely used" but its output "often not critically evaluated," and cites an existing, unresolved disagreement in the field over what "viable" even means operationally (membrane integrity vs. metabolic activity, refs 2-6) — a specific, pre-existing methodological gap closed entirely with pre-existing ingredients (the commercial kit, standard readers/microscopes, two textbook model organisms). Q_means is "no": nothing used here is new to the authors or field — the entire point is testing an existing, widely deployed tool rather than introducing one. Q_idea is "no": the stated aim ("to evaluate to which extent... validation is required") is a question, and no specific proposition about how the dyes would actually misbehave is staked out before the experiments — the headline finding (SYTO9 brighter in dead than live P. aeruginosa cells) reads as a discovered anomaly, not a predicted one. Q_accretion is clearly "no." One clean "yes," so the reversed order does not need to arbitrate.

## Alternative reading
Idea-first is not well supported, but the paper is close in spirit to what I'd call an "anomaly-hunting" problem-first case rather than a plain gap-filling one: the authors do not merely apply an existing tool to a new target, they interrogate an existing tool's own validity and surface a specific counterintuitive failure (dead cells outshining live ones on a "live" channel). Under a looser reading one might call this idea-first if the introduction had staked out "we suspect membrane-integrity assumptions break down in P. aeruginosa specifically" before running the assay — but the text frames the organism choice and the critical evaluation generically, not around a species-specific prediction.
