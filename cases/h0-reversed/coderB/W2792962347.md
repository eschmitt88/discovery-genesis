## Reasoning
Q_problem is the clear "yes": the introduction explicitly frames inhibition's role as "a topic of debate" and the frequency-range mechanism as "unresolved," both closed using an established experimental platform. Q_means is "no" — optogenetics and electrophysiology are established field-wide, and the specific Dbx1-lineage genetic targeting approach is this research group's own established platform (Ramirez lab preBötC work), matching the v1.1 "own platform reapplied" pattern for problem-first rather than means-first. Q_idea is "no": the interdependence conclusion is explicitly derived from and stated after the experiments, not staked out as a premise beforehand. Q_accretion is "no." One clean "yes" leaves the reversed order nothing to arbitrate.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is the plausible alternative if the specific experimental manipulations (removing sensory feedback while selectively activating inhibitory vs. excitatory neurons) are judged novel enough to count as a new capability rather than routine application of an established platform. I did not take this reading because the underlying tools (optogenetic Dbx1 targeting, electrophysiology) are established in this group's own prior work, and the paper's novelty is presented as resolving a debate, not as introducing new instrumentation.

## Notes for the codebook
A useful contrast case to the batch's several "own platform, next target" examples: here the platform is reapplied not to a new tissue/species/target but to resolve a specific long-debated mechanistic question within the same system — supporting "consolidation" (resolving debate/controversy) as a distinct move from "gap-filling:next-target" (extending to an unstudied target) even when both are problem-first and both rest on the self-citation "own platform" tell.
