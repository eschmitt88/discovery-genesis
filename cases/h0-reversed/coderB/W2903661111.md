## Reasoning
This card bundles two moves — cloning/overexpression (reuse of the authors' own established characterization work on this enzyme, a problem-first pattern) and a novel lipid-particle encapsulation (explicitly flagged as first-reported). Q_means is the stronger and cleaner "yes": the paper's own language marks the encapsulation as new, and the bulk of the reported findings — the improved stability, tolerance, cytotoxicity, and immunogenicity profile that constitute the paper's therapeutic claim — flow directly from that new-here capability, matching the means-first template. Q_problem reaches only "partial": both the cost-effective-production limitation and the general enzyme-instability/immunogenicity problem are old and specific, but the ingredient that actually delivers the paper's headline result (the encapsulation method) is new-here, not pre-existing — the same "old problem, new-here capability" configuration recurring elsewhere in this batch. Q_idea is "no," Q_accretion is "no."

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a real alternative for the cloning/overexpression component specifically (own-platform reuse to solve a stated cost-effectiveness gap), and a coder weighting that component over the encapsulation component could code this problem-first instead. I weighted the encapsulation component higher because it is explicitly flagged as novel and carries most of the abstract's substantive findings and the title's own emphasis ("...and therapeutic efficacy of Solid Lipid Particle formulations").
