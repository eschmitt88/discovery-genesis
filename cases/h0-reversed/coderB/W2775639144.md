## Reasoning
The abstract's own sequencing is the strongest signal: an initial, non-targeted observation (mitochondrial structural decline under carabrone exposure) precedes the deliberate synthesis of a fluorescent conjugate and the raising of an antibody — tools that did not exist before this paper and whose availability is what makes the colocalization measurement (and hence the mechanistic claim) possible. That is the means-first pattern precisely as defined: capability → observation → claim. No abstract-only evidence lets me weigh how much of the introduction the authors would have written as a proposition before starting (dossier has no full text), so I cannot confidently promote Q_idea past "partial." Q_problem is real (mechanism "remains unclear") but the closing ingredients are new-here, which the problem-first test does not admit.

## Alternative reading
Idea-first is the closest competitor: "carabrone may act on the mitochondria directly" reads like a proposition that then organizes the rest of the design (build tools to test mitochondrial localization specifically, rather than a generic subcellular screen). Under the reversed order this would be considered first. But the abstract frames that sentence as a conclusion drawn from an observation already made ("were observed, indicating..."), not a claim staked out beforehand — so it fails the "before the work began" test and I did not treat it as a full idea-first case. If the intro (unavailable here) shows the hypothesis was proposed from prior literature (e.g., other sesquiterpene lactones' known mitochondrial mechanisms, refs 2001/2004/2005/2006/2014) before any Ggt exposure was run, this would flip to idea-first.

## Notes for the codebook
Good test case for the means-first vs idea-first boundary the reversed protocol is probing: a hypothesis that visibly forms mid-paper (from a pilot observation) rather than in a stated literature-derived proposition. The codebook doesn't currently give guidance on "hypothesis formed from an internal pilot result" vs "hypothesis formed from prior literature" — the former should probably not count toward idea-first even under the fair-hearing procedure, since the "before the work began" clause is violated by construction.
