## Reasoning
Q_problem is the only "yes": the Background paragraph is unusually explicit about the pre-existing gap ("evidence for the effectiveness of INF administration for RD is scant... requires further exploration, specifically the use in various age groups and for various underlying diseases") and about the ingredient (INF) already existing and already being used this way in adults and in one narrow infant series. Q_means is "no" — INF is not new to the field or the authors; it is described as "a rather new treatment approach" in pediatric pain management generally but is already established for RD specifically in adults, and the chart-review method is routine. Q_idea is "no" — nothing resembling a stated proposition organizes the paper; the three stated aims ("first... second... third...") are purely descriptive/enumerative. Q_accretion is "no" — this is primary clinical data from one institution, not an aggregation. Reversed order never has to arbitrate here since only one question returned "yes."

## Alternative reading
Idea-first is not plausible: there is no claim-about-the-world stated ahead of the chart review that the design tests; the paper reports what happened rather than testing a hypothesis. Means-first is the more tempting alternative reading if one weights "retrospective chart review across two care settings, 2010–2016" as a novel data-assembly capability — but per v1.1, means-first requires a capability new to the authors or the field, and chart review of controlled-drug registries is routine clinical-research infrastructure, explicitly not novel.
