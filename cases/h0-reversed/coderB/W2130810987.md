## Reasoning
Q_problem is the only clean "yes." The Background paragraph is a textbook problem-first setup: established prior knowledge (aneuploidy is prognostic across several cancers; CGH shows tumor-specific chromosomal patterns), then an explicit open question ("we now evaluated whether..."), closed entirely with cited, pre-existing methods — several imported from the authors' own prior work on a *different* cancer type (the SSI genomic-instability threshold validated on breast carcinoma, ref. [16]). That self-citation pattern (own platform, new target) is exactly the v1.1 tell for problem-first over means-first. Q_means fails because nothing methodological is new to the authors or the field — every assay is cited. Q_idea fails because the driving sentence is phrased and functions as a question, not a risked claim; nothing in the introduction commits to a specific directional prediction before the analysis. Q_accretion is trivially "no." Only one "yes," so the reversed order does not have to arbitrate.

## Alternative reading
Idea-first is the only plausible second reading, on the theory that "genomic instability, not histology, drives expression changes" is implicitly the authors' working hypothesis inherited from their broader research program (Auer/Ried are established genomic-instability researchers) and could have been written down beforehand. But the text itself frames this as the question under test, not a claim staked in advance, and the codebook is explicit that "a gap statement is not a proposition" — I did not upgrade this to idea-first on the strength of an inferred prior belief the dossier does not state.
