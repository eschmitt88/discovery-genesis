## Reasoning
Q_means is the clear driver, and unusually the paper's own title names the enabling capability directly. The question itself (spontaneous mutation rate and spectrum in E. coli, and how MMR shapes it) is old — the reference list traces mutation-accumulation methodology back to 1991 and DNA-repair/mismatch-repair mechanism papers into the 1980s — but what this paper adds (strand-of-origin transition bias, 5'ApC/TpG and GpC/CpG context hotspots, a precise indel-to-substitution rate ratio) are all genome-wide statistics that require sequencing every mutation across the whole genome, not just at a reporter locus. The two explicitly cited enabling tools (BWA, Dindel) are both within 2-3 years of publication, squarely inside the v1.1 "cited ≤2y before → means-first" window. Q_problem only reaches "partial": the question predates the paper by decades, but the problem-first test specifically requires the *ingredient* to be pre-existing, and here the decisive ingredient (genome-wide sequencing at this resolution) is new, not old — which is precisely the boundary the v1.1 rule is designed to sort into means-first. Q_idea and Q_accretion are both clearly "no."

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is the genuine alternative, and a coder weighting the age of the *question* over the age of the *ingredient* could defend it — mutation-rate estimation in E. coli is about as old and well-trodden a question as exists in this literature. I resolved the tie toward means-first because the codebook's own worked distinction (means-first: "the capability is introduced in this paper, or first cited ≤2 years before it") points directly at exactly this configuration — an old question, closed for the first time by a very recently available technology — and that is the scenario the rule seems written to capture.

## Notes for the codebook
Good canonical means-first example, and possibly worth adding as a named worked case alongside the v1.1 "next-target" example: an old, well-studied question (mutation rate/spectrum) resolved at qualitatively higher resolution by a capability that is new not because it's exotic, but because it's *recent* (WGS + specific alignment tools cited within 2-3 years). Distinguishing "new capability, old question" (means-first) from "old capability, new question" (problem-first) is cleaner in cases like this than in the "mature platform on a new target" cases the rule was originally written for.
