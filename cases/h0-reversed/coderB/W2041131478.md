## Reasoning
Q_problem is the clean "yes," anchored in an unusually explicit, citation-backed gap statement naming exactly what was missing (adult data in resource-limited settings generally; any data at all in Zambia specifically). Q_means is "no" because pulse oximetry, the sole technology used, is described as already "established... as the fifth vital sign" elsewhere — its deployment here is not a capability new to the authors or the field, only new to this hospital. Q_idea required deliberate scrutiny because the paper does contain an explicit "We hypothesized that..." sentence — exactly the construction codebook §1 flags by name as something v0 wrongly credited and v1 deliberately excludes ("a gap statement is not a proposition... v0 let 'we hypothesised that…' boilerplate count"). Read on its own terms the sentence restates the gap ("undiagnosed hypoxemia is present") with an expected direction, not an independent claim about how the world works that organizes a design beyond the measurement itself — so under the codebook's own explicit guidance I do not count it. Q_accretion is trivially "no." Only one clean "yes," so reversed order changes nothing here.

## Alternative reading
Idea-first is the closest call, precisely because of the explicit hypothesis sentence, and a coder without the codebook's specific "boilerplate hypothesis" exclusion in mind could reasonably code this idea-first. I followed the codebook's explicit instruction on this exact linguistic pattern rather than the surface form of the sentence.
