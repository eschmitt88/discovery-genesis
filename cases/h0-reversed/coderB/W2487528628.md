## Reasoning
Q_problem is the clear "yes," and the introduction is unusually explicit about the specific, contested state of prior knowledge it closes: not just a generic "poorly understood" gap but a directly cited, disputed prior conclusion (chromatin-topology hypothesis) and named methodological inconsistency across the existing literature (varying cell types, sample sizes, platforms). The tools used to close it (RNA-seq, shRNA screening) are both established and cited; the innovation is comprehensiveness of design (many cell types, many genetic backgrounds) rather than a new capability, so Q_means is "no." Q_idea required care: the paper does end with a strong causal proposition ("we propose that interferon activation... contributes to many of the clinical impacts of trisomy 21"), but the text places this explicitly after and because of the findings ("Taken together, our results identify... We hypothesize that IFN activation could contribute...") — it is the paper's conclusion, not a premise stated before the genomics work began. Q_accretion is "no." One clean "yes" leaves nothing for the reversed order to arbitrate.

## Alternative reading
Idea-first is the closest alternative, and a coder who treats the interferon-receptor-gene-dosage hypothesis as implicit background knowledge the authors already suspected (given chr21 encodes four IFN receptor subunits, a fact presumably known before this study) could argue the design was built to test that specific idea from the start. I did not take this reading because the text's own sequencing places the pathway identification as an unbiased finding of the "complementary genomics approaches," with the receptor-dosage mechanism proposed only afterward as an explanation.

## Notes for the codebook
A good example of "consolidation" as distinct from "gap-filling:next-target": the move here is resolving a *contested* prior conclusion via broader, more systematic data collection across contexts, not merely extending an established method to an untested target.
