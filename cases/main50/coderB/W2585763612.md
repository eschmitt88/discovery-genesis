## Reasoning
Both the theoretical framework (facework, dating to Goffman 1967 and Cupach & Metts 1994) and the analytic method (thematic analysis, 2006) are established tools from the communication/sociology literature, applied here to a specific, previously unstudied empirical target (post-weight-loss social negotiation) — the codebook's "established method, new target/population" pattern, i.e. problem-first with gap-filling:next-target. Evidence that would change my mind: if the paper had proposed the facework mechanism as a testable proposition before data collection rather than as an analytic lens applied after the fact, idea-first would be a stronger fit.

## Alternative reading
idea-first is the natural alternative — a "facework lens" could be read as a hypothesis about mechanism stated up front — but the abstract frames facework as an analytic lens for a thematic analysis of interview data, not as a proposition the design tests, so I rate idea-first less likely per the codebook's rule that "a gap statement is not a proposition."
