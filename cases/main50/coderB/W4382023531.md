## Reasoning
The electrochemical polymerization technique itself is well established across multiple metal substrates (copper, iron, magnesium) and even on aluminum with other triazine-dithiol substituents, going back at least to the 1990s for the general method and to 2006-2010 for aluminum specifically. This paper's specific contribution is a new monomer substituent (diallylamino, chosen for its rubber-bonding-relevant reactive chemistry) applied via the existing technique to the existing target substrate (aluminum) — a next-target extension within an established methodology family, matching problem-first.

## Alternative reading
means-first/instrument is plausible if the diallylamino substituent required a materially different polymerization mechanism or capability (the abstract does mention "speculating" a distinct radical polymerization mechanism via XPS), which could indicate a nontrivial mechanistic finding rather than routine application. Without full text to see whether the mechanism is presented as a genuinely new/surprising finding or a confirmatory extension of the mechanism already established for other triazine-dithiol/aluminum systems (2010 "Study of Influencing Factors and the Mechanism of Preparing Triazinedithiol Polymeric Nanofilms on Aluminum Surfaces" is a very close precedent), this could not be resolved with higher confidence.
