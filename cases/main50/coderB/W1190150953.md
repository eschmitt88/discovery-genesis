## Reasoning
The three assay families (colour/CIELab, puncture-compression texture testing, phenolic HPLC) are each cited to prior grape-science work going back to 2007–2010, not introduced here; the paper's own novelty claim is about the target ("these results on botrytised Chenin grapes are new") and the specific marker combination, not a new instrument. Per the v1.1 tell, this reads as an established methodological toolkit applied to a next material/target (Chenin blanc + noble rot), which is problem-first, not means-first. The combination of colour+mechanical+phenolic testing as a joint marker panel is close to recombination (methods normally reported separately, joined here to sharpen discrimination), so I kept it as a secondary label — removing it would understate that the joint-panel result is the headline finding, not just one test alone. Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing the puncture/compression protocol itself was newly engineered for this study rather than adapted from [2009]/[2010].

## Alternative reading
means-first is plausible if the "combination" of tests is read as a genuinely new integrated assay rather than a repurposing of three known tests — but the abstract frames the combination as an empirical finding ("this combination appeared to be the most adequate"), not as instrument development, so I rate this less likely.

## Notes for the codebook
