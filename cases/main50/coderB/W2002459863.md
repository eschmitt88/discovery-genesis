## Reasoning
This is a strong instance of the v1.1 "capability new to the authors" test applied the other way: the pan-genome concept and its finite-supragenome/power-law statistical models are explicitly the prior work of a co-author on this very paper (Tettelin, 2005 S. agalactiae pan-genome; 2007/2008 Streptococcus pan-genome reviews), cited here as established methodology rather than introduced. The large-scale sequencing itself is also not a new capability for this author team (several are genome-sequencing-center authors with prior S. pneumoniae genome papers cited, e.g. 2001, 2007). The genuinely new element is the specific target — S. pneumoniae's pan-genome and its relationship to Mitis-group relatives — which is a next-target application of an established framework, hence problem-first / gap-filling:next-target. Evidence that would change my mind: if the finite-supragenome or power-law model itself had been substantially revised or newly derived for this paper rather than cited to the 2005/2008 prior work.

## Alternative reading
means-first is a tempting alternative given the genuinely large new sequencing effort (24 newly sequenced strains) that makes the specific comparative analysis possible — one could read this as "a large new dataset is the reason the finding exists." I rate this less likely because the sequencing capability itself is not new to the author team (established via prior genome papers), and the codebook's rule specifically distinguishes new data volume from new capability; the analytical framework that turns the data into the paper's central claims (pan-genome openness, cross-species recombination) is explicitly the pre-existing Tettelin framework.

## Notes for the codebook
A useful worked example for the "capability new to the authors" rule in comparative genomics specifically: the concept-originator being a co-author on the "next species" paper makes the problem-first call unusually well-evidenced (the prior-work citation is to the same person), which is a stronger signal than the more common case where reuse must be inferred from an anonymous prior-work citation pattern alone.
