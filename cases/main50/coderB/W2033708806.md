## Reasoning
The citation context makes the motivating problem explicit and recent: BRAF-inhibitor relapse became a defined clinical issue only around 2010-2012 (vemurafenib trial data cited from 2011-2012), and the paper closes it not with a new instrument but by testing an existing, well-characterized pathway (PGC1α-driven mitochondrial biogenesis, established in adipose/muscle/cardiac biology since 1999-2003) against melanoma cells using established tools (PLX4720 from 2008, a PGC1a-luciferase reporter from 2003). That is rule 3 problem-first: the ingredient (PGC1α biology, the reporter assay) is not new, but the specific clinical question it answers is. The core move is `unification`: melanoma/BRAF-MAPK oncogenic signaling and PGC1α/mitochondrial-biogenesis metabolism had been largely separate research programs (the latter rooted in adipocyte/muscle/cardiac physiology), and this paper shows they are mechanistically the same axis (BRAF→MITF→PGC1α→oxidative metabolism) in melanoma. I would move toward means-first if the full methods section showed a newly introduced metabolic-profiling instrument (e.g., a first-time-adopted extracellular flux platform) was what enabled the observation, rather than the well-established reporter/inhibitor tools visible here.

## Alternative reading
`means-first` is the alternative if a genuinely new capability — most plausibly a metabolic-flux measurement platform not visible in this citation-context excerpt — was the actual reason the oxidative-phosphorylation phenotype was caught. Judged less likely because every tool explicitly traceable in the dossier (PLX4720, PGC1a-luciferase reporter, site-directed mutagenesis) is established years before this paper, and the abstract/context frame the logic as problem (resistance) → test known pathway → mechanism, not capability → novel observation.

## Notes for the codebook
Clean `unification` case: two research programs with genuinely different primary fields/origins (oncogenic signal transduction vs. metabolic/mitochondrial physiology) shown to be one mechanism, distinct from `transfer` because the paper isn't importing a foreign-field *method*, it's showing a foreign-field *phenomenon* is mechanistically entangled with the field under study. Worth a worked example distinguishing unification-of-phenomena from transfer-of-method for exactly this kind of mechanistic-discovery paper.
