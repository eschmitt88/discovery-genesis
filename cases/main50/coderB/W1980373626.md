## Reasoning
The abstract states the specific gap directly: mortality effects of roads on turtles are established, but effects on reproductive success/recruitment are not. The ingredient that closes this gap — microsatellite-based kin-group/diversity analysis — is standard population-genetics machinery already decades old and heavily cited in the reference list (FSTAT 1995, GENEPOP 1995, sibship reconstruction 2004/2009), not a capability introduced by this paper. That satisfies the v1.1 means-first exclusion (no capability new to the authors/field) and puts this squarely in problem-first. The specific move is closer to a first-measurement (kin-group structure as a proxy for reproductive success near vs. away from roads has, per the abstract, not been assessed before) than a next-target extension of a single established assay to one more species, so `gap-filling:first-measurement` was chosen over `next-target`.

## Alternative reading
`gap-filling:next-target` is plausible if one reads the paper as "apply the standard road-genetics toolkit (already used on salamanders, red-backed voles, etc.) to one more taxon, the painted turtle." The abstract's emphasis on a specific unmeasured *outcome* (reproductive success, not just genetic differentiation) tipped this toward first-measurement instead.

## Notes for the codebook
Road-ecology + population-genetics papers recur in this genre (cf. the 2007 salamander and 2009 "molecular road ecology" refs) — they are a small sub-family where the method is essentially fixed and only the target species and the specific outcome variable change. Might be worth a standing example in the codebook for the next-target vs first-measurement boundary.
