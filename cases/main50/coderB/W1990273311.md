## Reasoning
The paper closes a specific, narrow question (which cell type expresses FasL) using assay and antibody tools the same research group had already published (2006 flounder FasL cloning, 2009 flounder leukocyte mAbs) — the "serial reuse of an established platform on a new question" pattern the v1.1 tell assigns to problem-first, even though the self-citation count here (2, not the canonical 4-8) is thinner than the worked example. Genesis logic in the abstract runs question ("which cells make FasL?") → existing assay → answer, not capability → observation → claim.

## Alternative reading
`means-first` is plausible if the 2009 monoclonal-antibody panel is read as a capability new enough (published only 1 year before this paper, inside the ≤2-year means-first window) to be the reason the finding exists rather than a routine tool reuse. I judged problem-first more likely because the question itself ("which leukocyte expresses FasL") is what the abstract foregrounds, and the mAb panel appears to be the authors' own established resource rather than a capability introduced in this paper.

## Notes for the codebook
