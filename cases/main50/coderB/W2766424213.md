## Reasoning
The survey methodology (cross-sectional self-report questionnaires) is standard and not new; what appears new is the scale and the simultaneous inclusion of three stakeholder groups in one coordinated national study, which the broad, cross-sector author list (clinical academics, patient advocacy, employer/policy research, a design firm, industry funding) suggests required assembling a new collaboration rather than a new measurement capability. This favors problem-first (an established survey approach to a well-known perception-gap question, extended in this instance to a new, broader population/design) over means-first.

## Alternative reading
means-first is plausible if the three-stakeholder-group simultaneous survey design itself constitutes a genuinely new research capability for studying obesity perception gaps (rather than simply combining known single-group survey instruments) — the abstract's phrasing gives no way to distinguish "we built a new combined-stakeholder instrument" from "we ran three known-type surveys in parallel," so this was resolved toward problem-first on the weaker evidence but is not a high-confidence call.

## Notes for the codebook
`new-collaboration-or-team` as enabler fits well here even without full text: an unusual, cross-sector author list (clinical + advocacy + policy + design + industry) is itself a legible signal of "the enabling resource is who got together to do this," distinct from `new-instrument` or `new-dataset-or-resource` — worth keeping as an explicit heuristic for reading enabler off author/institution lists when the full text is unavailable.
