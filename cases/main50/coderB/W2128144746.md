## Reasoning
The abstract describes a textbook reliability-validation design: an established protocol (CAPS-A) and an established statistic (kappa, Cohen 1960) applied to (1) a training intervention and (2) a population/context the protocol hadn't previously been validated on (American/Canadian English speakers, via the Americleft Modification). No new instrument or measurement capability is introduced; the "ingredient" (CAPS-A, kappa/ICC) is old, and the specific question (does training help, does the modified protocol hold up) is what's new — rule-3 problem-first, with `gap-filling:next-target` as the move since the population/protocol-variant is the new target.

## Alternative reading
`means-first` is the alternative if the Americleft Speech Project itself — a coordinated multi-site international rater consortium — is treated as the enabling new capability (assembling and training a distributed panel of raters across many institutions is nontrivial). Judged less likely as the primary reading because the abstract and conclusion frame the contribution around the training/reliability results themselves, not around the formation of the consortium as a novel resource.
