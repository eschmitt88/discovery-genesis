## Reasoning
The abstract is explicit that the contribution is dimensional/geometric optimization within an already-established paradigm (magnetic absorber + embedded FSS to broaden bandwidth), and the reference list shows this exact combination pursued by multiple groups from 2000-2012, well outside the ≤2-year means-first window and not the authors' own prior work being reused, so it reads as the field's established method rather than the authors' newly-acquired capability. That places it under rule 3 (problem-first): the specific narrow question (broader bandwidth at this thickness via this loop geometry) is new; the tools (FSS design theory, HFSS simulation) are not.

## Alternative reading
`means-first` is the alternative if the treble-square-loop geometry itself is a genuinely new design capability (as opposed to a routine variant of the double-square-loop designs in the 2007/2010 references) that enables an observation not achievable with prior geometries. Judged less likely because the abstract frames the work as incremental optimization ("an optimized operating bandwidth can be obtained") rather than as reporting a new phenomenon enabled by a new tool.

## Notes for the codebook
Engineering-optimization papers in a dense, multi-group incremental literature (FSS-embedded RAM here) are a recognizable citation signature for problem-first: many near-identical prior works by different groups solving the same tradeoff with minor design variants. Worth naming as a worked example alongside the "authors' own platform reused" pattern, since the tell here is field-level rather than author-level repetition.
