## Reasoning
This is a clean means-first case: the paper's own stated logic is that a specific open question (carabrone's unclear mechanism) could not be answered with existing tools, so two new molecular probes were built specifically to answer it — a capability-first design (build imaging reagents -> observe mitochondrial localization -> claim mechanism). Prior carabrone literature in the reference list (2010, 2012, 2014) is about chemical synthesis and antifungal structure-activity relationships, not localization or mechanism, so there is no evidence the imaging approach is a reused platform from the authors' own prior carabrone work.

## Alternative reading
problem-first is a plausible second reading if these imaging techniques (fluorescent-conjugate design, custom antibody raising) are themselves a routine, oft-reused capability for this lab or field applied to "the next compound" (carabrone) rather than a new capability — several cited methodology papers (2012 fluorescent castasterone, 2013 fluorescent/biotinylated GABA-C antagonists, 2014 fluorescent glucose-fipronil conjugate) show fluorescent-surrogate design is an established general strategy in the field. It was not chosen as primary because, even if the general strategy (make a fluorescent surrogate) is established field-wide, this specific application (design, synthesis, and dual-method validation for carabrone specifically) is new to this paper and is presented as the reason the mechanistic finding exists.

## Notes for the codebook
A useful test for means-first when the "capability" is a bespoke reagent (a custom probe/antibody) rather than an instrument or dataset: even though the general strategy (fluorescent surrogate + antibody for localization) recurs across many other small-molecule mechanism papers field-wide, its application to a specific compound is still new-to-the-investigation and should count as means-first unless the same authors have applied it repeatedly to prior compounds themselves.
