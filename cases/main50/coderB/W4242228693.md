## Reasoning
This is one of the cleanest examples in the batch of the v1.1 "capability new to the authors or field" test: the reference list shows the same research group's own reductive-coupling/transfer-hydrogenation platform cited repeatedly (2003, 2004, 2007x2, 2008x2, 2010) as the method being applied here, and a directly analogous prior paper by the same group (2009) already used formaldehyde with a related unsaturated substrate class (dienes). The present paper's contribution — alkynes as the substrate, with complementary Ni-vs-Ru regioselectivity — is squarely a "next target" within an established methodology series rather than a new mechanistic capability, so problem-first applies despite the paper being a synthetic-methodology report (a genre that often defaults to means-first/instrument).

## Alternative reading
means-first/instrument is the natural default reading for a methodology paper announcing "a new reaction," and the complementary Ni-vs-Ru regiodivergence is a genuinely new selectivity outcome that could be read as a new capability. It was not chosen as primary because the underlying mechanistic strategy (hydrogen-mediated C-C coupling without stoichiometric organometallics) is explicitly the authors' long-running platform, and the specific substrate/reagent slot being filled (alkynes + formaldehyde) is the last obvious gap in a systematically explored substrate scope, matching the "serial reuse of an established platform on a new target" pattern more closely than a first-of-its-kind capability.

## Notes for the codebook
Strong confirmatory example that reference-list self-citation density (here ~6-7x for the same core methodology) is a reliable, checkable signal for the v1.1 tell even from an abstract-only, non-English dossier — worth keeping as the primary heuristic for synthetic-methodology papers, which otherwise default too easily to means-first/instrument by genre convention.
