## Reasoning
The abstract explicitly labels the piece a narrative review synthesizing existing literature, which is the accretion signature: the conclusion's organizing form (a layered anatomical schema) could plausibly have been sketched before assembling the specific supporting citations. `Consolidation` is the primary move — gathering many separately-published structural descriptions (fat compartments, ligaments, SMAS layers) into one account. `Formalisation` is kept as a second label because the paper's stated contribution is not just narrative synthesis but an explicit schematic/model representation ("a schematic facial model was created... the concept of the layered arrangement is a new way to understand") — giving previously informal, scattered anatomical facts a structured, named-layer representation is exactly what formalisation is defined to capture; removing it would drop that the paper's real contribution is the organizing schema, not merely a literature summary.

## Alternative reading
`idea-first` was considered under codebook rule 6 (framework papers with no data, where a stated structure organizes the design) since the five-layer model is a genuine organizing proposition. This was set aside as primary because the paper is explicitly framed and structured as a review of existing findings rather than as introducing and testing a new proposition from the outset — the model is presented as synthesized from the literature, not as a hypothesis stated up front that the paper's design follows from.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a good test case for the boundary between plain accretion/consolidation and the "framework paper" idea-first exception in rule 6 — a review that ends in a named organizing schema sits right on that line; worth a worked example distinguishing "review that summarizes" from "review that formalizes a new model from what it summarizes."
