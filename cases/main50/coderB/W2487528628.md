## Reasoning
This is a clean means-first case under the decision order: the paper's own stated logic is capability -> observation -> claim. No specific interferon-related hypothesis is stated before the data; instead, the authors describe assembling a cross-tissue, inter-individual-variation-controlled sampling design plus an unbiased genome-wide shRNA screen "in order to identify consistent signaling pathways," and the interferon finding emerges from that design rather than motivating it. The one competing signal against means-first — the authors' own lab citing its prior use of a differential-expression bioinformatics tool (Donner 2010, Sullivan 2012, Galbraith 2013) — is a narrow computational-pipeline reuse, not reuse of the overall discovery design (multi-cell-type panel + shRNA screen), which is assembled fresh for this specific question and is the actual reason the finding exists.

## Alternative reading
problem-first is the closest alternative: one could argue the "problem" (what genes are consistently dysregulated by T21) is old and well-posed, and the tools (RNA-seq, shRNA screening) are individually not new to the field, making this "established methods applied to a specific open question." This was not chosen as primary because the paper's explicit narrative logic is discovery-driven (design a filtering approach, then see what falls out), matching decision-order rule 2 rather than rule 3, and because the specific combination (cross-tissue + inter-individual-controlled + genome-wide screen) is presented as new to this investigation, not as a previously-established platform being pointed at T21 as the next target.

## Notes for the codebook
Good test case for the boundary between "new capability" and "authors' own reused tool": a lab can cite its own prior methodological papers (here, a bioinformatics pipeline) without that constituting the v1.1 "serial reuse of an established platform on a new target" tell, when the reused piece is a narrow technical component rather than the overall experimental design that produces the finding. Worth a worked example distinguishing tool-level self-citation from platform-level self-citation.
