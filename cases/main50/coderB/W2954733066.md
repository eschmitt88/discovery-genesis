## Reasoning
The paper's own two-step abstract logic (mtDNA divergence suggests cryptic species -> nuclear ddRADseq data are then generated specifically to test that suggestion) reads as an old-ingredient-closes-a-stated-question structure once ddRADseq's maturity is accounted for: the method has been in wide use since a 2012 methods paper and is applied here, as in the cited 2018 raven and 2019 lizard studies, as an established tool for testing mitonuclear discordance/cryptic speciation in a new taxon, not as a capability new to this field or these authors. This matches gap-filling:next-target under problem-first.

## Alternative reading
means-first is a real competing reading if ddRADseq's application specifically within Lepidoptera population genomics, or specifically to resolve mtDNA/nuDNA conflict at this resolution, were new to this author group. Population-genomics methodology sections are often where new-to-the-authors capability shows up, and without full text the authors' own prior use (or non-use) of RADseq cannot be confirmed. This was not chosen as primary because the near-simultaneous, structurally identical raven/lizard precedents in the same reference list are strong evidence this is an established genre of investigation being extended to a new case, not a first-of-its-kind capability.

## Notes for the codebook
A useful illustration that "capability new to the field" should be assessed at the level of the general research design/genre (here: "use genomic SNP data to test whether mtDNA-flagged cryptic species hold up") rather than at the level of the specific organism, since the reference list shows the same design pattern already applied contemporaneously to unrelated taxa.
