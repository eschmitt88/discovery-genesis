## Reasoning
Every named surgical technique and the assessment methodology predate this paper by years to decades (1970 functional anatomy, 1986 Levator reconstruction, 2003/2006/2010 palatoplasty variants) and are applied to retrospectively-gathered records from a specific, previously under-served patient subgroup (over age two). That matches the v1.1 problem-first tell (established methods reused, target/population is what is new) rather than means-first. `gap-filling:next-target` captures the "new target/population" framing precisely, since the paper's own conclusion is explicitly about extending applicability to an age group the existing schedule wasn't built for.

## Alternative reading
`gap-filling:bigger-n` was considered, since 245 patients pooled from case records resembles a cohort-size move, but the paper's stated contribution is about which technique works in an age subgroup, not primarily about sample size — next-target is the better fit.

## Notes for the codebook
Retrospective record-mining studies that isolate a specific age/population subgroup not covered by the standard protocol are a recurring surgical/clinical genre; worth confirming `routine-data-wave` (vs `existing-resource-repurposed`) is the intended enabler head for "mined an existing clinical case database," since both terms could plausibly apply.
