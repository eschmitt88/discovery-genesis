## Reasoning
This is a clean problem-first case per the decision order: the ingredient (pulse oximetry) is old and unmodified, and the paper explicitly frames a specific, named gap (no Zambia data; adult data scarce) that the existing tool closes. It combines two sub-moves — a first-ever measurement in this country (first-measurement) and extension of the standard pediatric-pneumonia study design to a new population/setting (adults and non-pneumonia illnesses, next-target) — both are load-bearing per the introduction's own framing, so both are kept.

## Alternative reading
means-first is not plausible here: nothing about the oximeter is new to the authors or the field (it is literally "handheld pulse oximetry," commercial donated units). idea-first is also not supported — a hypothesis is stated ("we hypothesized that a significant burden of hypoxemia was present"), but per codebook §1.4 this is boilerplate hypothesis language following from the stated gap, not a proposition that organizes the design ab initio.

## Notes for the codebook
Good example distinguishing `gap-filling:first-measurement` (first-ever Zambia measurement) from `gap-filling:next-target` (extending pediatric-pneumonia-only prior literature to adults/other illnesses) as genuinely co-primary rather than a padded second label — removing either changes what the introduction claims as novel.
