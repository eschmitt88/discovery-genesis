## Reasoning
Nothing about the risk-scoring instrument is new: REGICOR is explicitly described as the Spain-calibrated Framingham scale based on the 1998 Wilson version, applied here via routine chart review to a population and site with no prior local data ("no data have been reported... in the country" is not stated verbatim as in the Zambia oximetry card, but the discussion's comparisons to five other countries and the closing call to "replicate this scale in other centers of the country" make the local-first framing explicit). This is a straightforward problem-first / gap-filling:next-target case.

## Alternative reading
accretion could be argued if this were read as a purely descriptive local surveillance snapshot with no analytic claim, but the paper does state and test an implicit local question (what is this population's coronary risk profile, and how does it compare to other described cohorts), and frames specific findings (elevated hyperglycemia and hypertriglyceridemia relative to comparators) as its contribution, so problem-first is preferred over accretion.

## Notes for the codebook
Small, single-site, non-probabilistic-sample studies applying a well-known risk score to a new local population recur across low/middle-income-country journals in this corpus (cf. the Zambia hypoxemia card) — worth flagging as a recognizable sub-pattern of gap-filling:next-target distinct from the "next steel grade" / "next drug" examples: here the "target" is a population/geography rather than a material or agent.
