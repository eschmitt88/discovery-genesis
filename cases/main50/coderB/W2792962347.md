## Reasoning
The paper's own stated research logic is explicitly capability-driven ("we combine targeted optogenetic manipulations with electrophysiology... to explore how subpopulations... interact"), and the central mechanistic claim (the refractory-period interdependence model) is presented as emerging from this combined manipulation rather than being stated as a prior hypothesis to be tested. This matches decision-order rule 2 (means-first: capability -> observation -> claim).

## Alternative reading
problem-first is a real competing reading and the closest case to the codebook's own named ambiguous example. The lab's own prior papers (cited 2015 x2: "Optogenetic excitation of preBötC neurons potently drives inspiratory activity in vivo" and "Optogenetic perturbation of preBötC inhibitory neurons modulates respiratory pattern") already used ChR2 optogenetics with the same Cre-driver lines on the same preBötC circuit — a serial-reuse-of-own-platform signature. This was not chosen as primary because the "target" here is not moving to a new gene/species/material as in the codebook's worked examples; the same circuit is being probed more deeply with a substantially expanded joint E/I, phase-resolved, in-vivo-extended design that the paper explicitly credits with producing the finding — but this is a genuinely close call and a good candidate case for recording as an "unresolved" example per the report's finding-3 request.

## Notes for the codebook
Flagged as a hard case for the "capability new to authors" tell: a lab reusing its own optogenetic/Cre-line toolkit on the same anatomical circuit across successive papers is a different pattern from reusing a platform on a genuinely new target (next gene/species/material), because the object of study does not change — only the sophistication of the manipulation does. The current wording of the v1.1 tell does not clearly resolve which reading should win; a worked example distinguishing "next target" from "same target, deeper capability" would help.
