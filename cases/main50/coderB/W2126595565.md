## Reasoning
Nothing about the MRI grading system, the imaging modality, or the surveillance methodology is new to the authors or the field — the modified Peetrons classification and the UEFA injury-study data-collection framework both predate this paper by years. The paper's distinguishing feature per the abstract is scale and duration (23 teams, 2007-2011, 516 injuries) applied to a question (MRI grade vs lay-off time) that smaller studies had already addressed since at least 2001. This is a clean gap-filling:bigger-n case under problem-first.

## Alternative reading
gap-filling:first-measurement is a plausible second reading for the specific finding about grade 0/1 injuries accounting for 56% of lay-off days, since that particular breakdown may not have been reported before at this scale. It was not added as a second label because the central move — the same MRI-grade-vs-outcome question, run at much larger n — is what makes the card true; the grade-0/1 finding is a result of that larger n, not a separate move.
