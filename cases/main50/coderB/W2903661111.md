## Reasoning
Both halves of the paper follow the same pattern: an established technique (recombinant cloning in E. coli; SLP hot-emulsion encapsulation) is applied to a target it had not previously been used on (this specific bacterial strain's gene; this specific enzyme). The paper is explicit and repeated about the "first" framing in both cases, and neither technique itself is presented as new — the vector system, cloning strategy, and lipid-particle fabrication method are all cited to established prior protocols. This is a clean problem-first / gap-filling:next-target case.

## Alternative reading
means-first/instrument could be argued if the SLP-asparaginase combination required nontrivial new formulation development that functions as a de facto new method (the paper does report specific optimization, e.g. standardizing induction time and lipid ratios). It was not chosen as primary because the discussion frames these as routine optimization/characterization steps within an established fabrication method (cited to the 2014 itraconazole SLP protocol), not as a new capability being introduced.
