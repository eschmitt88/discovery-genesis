## Reasoning
The paper explicitly frames itself as extending an already-established treatment (INF, well documented for pediatric pain since 1999 and for adult/infant respiratory distress since 2008/2013) to a population and age range not previously studied — the codebook's "established method, new target/population" pattern (explicitly including "various age groups... various underlying diseases" as the named gap). The method used to answer it, retrospective chart review, is also standard, not new. This is a clean problem-first, gap-filling:next-target case. Evidence that would change my mind: if the authors had needed to develop a new assessment instrument or dosing protocol specific to this population, which they explicitly say is still lacking ("a standardized and validated tool... would help").

## Alternative reading
gap-filling:bigger-n is a weaker alternative if the paper is read as simply adding more patients to a small existing literature (one adult case series, one infant series) — but the paper's own framing emphasizes breadth of ages/diagnoses (a next-target expansion) rather than statistical power on the same narrow population, so I favor next-target.
