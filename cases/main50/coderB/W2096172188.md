## Reasoning
The paper closes a specific remaining gap (which repressors complete the MBW network, and how spatial coordination occurs) in an already well-established regulatory model (MBW activation complex, established 1986-2006) using an entirely standard molecular-genetics toolkit (Y2H/Y3H, GFP fusion, RNA extraction) that the same research group had already used in their own prior petunia papers (Schwinn 2006, Albert 2010, 2011). This is rule-3 problem-first with the v1.1 tell present (established platform, cited repeatedly in the authors' own prior work, applied to a new specific target — the repressor arm of the network).

## Alternative reading
`means-first` is the alternative if GFP-based live intercellular-movement tracking (rather than static localization) is treated as a materially new capability for this system. Judged less likely because GFP fusion technology itself dates to 1997, is used routinely across plant molecular biology by 2014, and the abstract frames the finding (movement of AN11 and MYBx) as one result among several network-architecture findings rather than as the paper's enabling device.

## Notes for the codebook
The abstract's closing sentence — that the network is "likely to be widespread in the Eudicots" by comparison to Arabidopsis — gestures at a broader unifying claim (reaction-diffusion-style activator-inhibitor pattern formation, cited via Turing 1952 and Gierer-Meinhardt 1972) beyond the core empirical network-mapping contribution. This sits at the boundary of `unification`/`formalisation` but is discussion-level framing rather than the paper's central move, so it was not listed as a second label per the v1.1 "only if removing it would make the card false" rule.
