## Reasoning
This is a tool paper where the platform itself (antiSMASH) is the authors' own established resource, reused across versions — which could look like the "serial reuse, problem-first" pattern — but the specific capabilities driving this update (SANDPUMA, the extended RODEO, and especially the trans-AT PKS alignment method explicitly cited as an in-preparation manuscript by co-authors) are new to the authors at time of publication, not previously used by them. Per the v1.1 rule's own tell ("the capability is introduced in this paper, or first cited ≤2 years before it"), that pushes this to means-first: the new algorithms are the reason the improved predictions exist. Evidence that would change my mind: if the "manuscript in preparation" trans-AT PKS method had instead been a already-published, oft-cited technique merely imported wholesale.

## Alternative reading
problem-first is a real alternative if the frame is "antiSMASH's purpose (BGC mining) hasn't changed, only its accuracy has improved incrementally, the way any actively maintained tool does" — under that reading this is gap-filling:bigger-n (same tool, more/better predictions) rather than means-first. I favor means-first because several of the specific new capabilities are original or first-time integrations rather than reuse of the authors' own long-standing methods, but this is a closer call than most cards in this batch.

## Notes for the codebook
Actively maintained software-tool papers (v2→v3→v4 update papers) are a recurring genre the codebook doesn't yet have explicit guidance for: they combine the "authors' own platform reused" pattern (which points problem-first) with "genuinely new capability integrated this version" (which points means-first) in the same paper, feature by feature. A rule of thumb — check whether the marquee new feature is cited as pre-existing (elsewhere) vs. new-here/in-preparation — worked here but might be worth stating explicitly.
