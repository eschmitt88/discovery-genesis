## Reasoning
The abstract names the gap in almost codebook-literal terms ("MBC responses have not been evaluated after BivWC vaccination. To address this knowledge gap, we measured..."), and the assay itself is inherited directly from a recognizable prior-Bangladesh MBC-in-cholera literature (2009–2012), not introduced in this paper. That is problem-first: an existing ingredient (the MBC assay) closes a specifically named, previously unaddressed question (does this particular already-deployed vaccine induce durable MBC). The move is `gap-filling:first-measurement` — MBC response to BivWC specifically had never been measured, distinct from a mere population swap because the vaccine formulation itself (not just the cohort) is the novel element being assessed for the first time.

## Alternative reading
`gap-filling:next-target` is a close alternative reading (established MBC-assessment methodology applied to a new vaccine/population, matching that sub-label's "new target/population" wording precisely). It was not chosen as primary because the paper's own framing centers on a specific unmeasured quantity (MBC durability for this vaccine) rather than a generic population extension — but the two sub-labels are genuinely close here and either would be defensible.

## Notes for the codebook
This card and the `next-target` alternative reading illustrate how close `first-measurement` and `next-target` can sit when the "new target" is a specific vaccine/intervention rather than a demographic population — worth a worked example distinguishing "new population, same intervention" from "same population type, new intervention" within gap-filling.
