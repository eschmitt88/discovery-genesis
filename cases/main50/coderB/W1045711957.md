## Reasoning
The reference list is almost entirely foundational ring theory (1943-1992: radicals, Von Neumann regular rings, exchange rings) plus a dense 2013-2015 cluster on nil-clean rings, clean general rings, and formal-matrix-ring constructions — exactly the citation signature of a paper closing a specific structural question ("is construction X nil-clean/strongly nil-clean?") with machinery (idempotent lifting, Peirce decomposition style arguments) that already existed. Nothing indicates a new proof technique, dataset, or instrument was introduced, so `problem-first` fits rule 3 of the decision order: the ingredient (nil-clean theory, only ~2 years old at publication) is not itself new to the authors, but the question — does it hold for this specific ring construction — is. I would revise toward `means-first` if the full text showed the paper's central contribution was a genuinely new proof technique or invariant rather than applying known idempotent/lifting arguments to a new class of rings.

## Alternative reading
`means-first` is the alternative: if the paper's core device is a novel structural tool (not visible from title+refs), the capability→observation→claim logic could dominate. This is less likely because every method-flavored reference (idempotent lifting 1977, exchange rings 1974-1979, clean-ring extensions 2001-2008) predates the paper by years to decades, with no citation cluster suggesting an introduced technique.
