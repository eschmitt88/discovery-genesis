## Reasoning
The AOPDF device itself traces to 2000 and the angular-dispersion theory used to analyze it to 2002/2004 — both well predate this 2012 paper, and the title names a characterization task on an existing device rather than a new device or method. That is rule 3 (problem-first): a specific, previously unaddressed property of an established instrument is measured using existing theory and technique. I would revise toward means-first if the full text showed a newly built interferometric or spectrally-resolved measurement setup was the actual enabling factor (one candidate, "Advances and limitations of phase dispersion measurement by spectrally and spatially resolved interferometry," is from 2008, only 4 years before this paper).

## Alternative reading
`means-first` is plausible if the 2008 phase-dispersion-measurement-by-interferometry reference names a technique adopted here as a genuinely new capability (inside or near the ≤2-year means-first window is a stretch at 4 years, but closer than most other references). Judged less likely as the primary reading because most of the reference cluster is much older (2000-2004) and the title frames the contribution as characterizing an existing device's known-but-unquantified property, not introducing new instrumentation.

## Notes for the codebook
Title+refs-only physics/optics papers where the title itself names a "characterize/quantify property-of-existing-device" task are a fairly reliable problem-first signature even without an abstract — worth noting as a heuristic alongside the citation-age pattern already in the codebook.
