## Reasoning
The abstract is unusually explicit about genesis: increased referral volume (a routine change in caseload/data flow, not a new instrument) is what surfaced enough cases to characterize. This matches problem-first (an old/routine ingredient — CT imaging, established craniofacial referral pathways — closes a specific question: what is this finding, how does it relate to craniosynostosis, what is the prognosis) rather than means-first, since CT and clinical assessment were already routine; the only "new" element is accumulated caseload, which the enabler vocabulary treats as `routine-data-wave`.

## Alternative reading
accretion could be argued if the paper were read as merely a case tally with no analytic claim, but the abstract goes further — it reports specific associations (craniosynostosis subtypes, syndrome) and management outcomes with follow-up, which is an analytic contribution beyond simple aggregation, so problem-first with first-measurement is preferred.
