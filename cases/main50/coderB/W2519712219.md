## Reasoning
The abstract's own logic is an explicit extension: a prior, narrower finding (one preserved region) motivates examining two much larger, already-defined brain networks using the same structural-MRI methodology. No new instrument or analysis technique appears; the target (which regions/networks) is what's new. That is rule-3 problem-first with `gap-filling:next-target` as the move — an established method and paradigm (superager cohort definition, cortical-thickness pipeline) reused on a broader, previously unexamined target.

## Alternative reading
`idea-first` is a real alternative given the abstract's explicit "We predicted that superagers would have preserved cortical thickness in critical nodes in these networks" — a stated proposition before the study. Judged less likely as primary under the decision order (problem-first is checked before idea-first) because the prediction is the natural, narrow extrapolation of the already-established gap (does preservation extend beyond one region to its containing networks) rather than a standalone novel proposition that redefines the study's design from first principles.

## Notes for the codebook
Good example of the idea-first/problem-first boundary the codebook flags: an abstract can contain an explicit "we predicted" sentence and still be problem-first under the decision order, because the prediction is a direct consequence of a named gap rather than a freestanding hypothesis. Worth a worked example distinguishing "prediction that operationalizes a named gap" from "prediction that constitutes the paper's genuinely new idea."
