## Reasoning
The CSI/CSP molecular-signature approach used to demarcate and formally name the new genera is this research group's characteristic, repeatedly-applied taxonomic-revision methodology (well known across the bacterial-systematics literature for having been run on numerous other genera), not a capability introduced in this paper — the serial-reuse-of-an-established-platform-on-a-new-target signature the v1.1 tell assigns to problem-first, with Mycobacterium as the new target genus. The paper's headline act — proposing four new genus names for previously only informally-suspected clades ("some of these clades have also been observed in earlier phylogenetic studies") — is why `formalisation` is listed alongside `gap-filling:next-target`: removing it would drop the central nomenclatural contribution (converting an informally-recognized clade structure into formal taxonomic status) in favor of only the methodological-reuse framing.

## Alternative reading
`means-first` is a defensible alternative: 150 fully sequenced genomes and 1941+136 core proteins represent an analysis scale only possible given the recent growth of the public genome database — if that growing data availability (rather than the established CSI method) is treated as the actual capability enabling this specific comprehensive resolution (where "earlier phylogenetic studies" could not achieve it), the paper reads as means-first (a new-compute-or-scale / new-dataset-or-resource enabler). This is one of the closer calls in the batch.

## Notes for the codebook
Taxonomic-revision papers that formally name new genera/species based on an established internal lab methodology (here, CSI/CSP) are a recognizable pattern combining `formalisation` (the nomenclatural act) with `gap-filling:next-target` (the established method's new-target reuse) — worth a worked example distinguishing this from pure `gap-filling:next-target` papers that report a finding without a formal naming/classification consequence.
