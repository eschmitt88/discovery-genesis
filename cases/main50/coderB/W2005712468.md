## Reasoning
The paper is explicit about its own move: an analytical method "traditionally used for habitat and food selection" (resource selection functions) is applied to a new target — debris-type preference in sea turtles — while the underlying ecological problem (does debris ingestion reflect selective preference, e.g. because debris resembles natural prey) is a specific, named open question closed with an existing statistical tool plus new field data (not a new instrument). That is rule-3 problem-first, `gap-filling:next-target` as the move.

## Alternative reading
`idea-first` is the alternative if the "debris resembles natural prey" hypothesis is read as a freestanding proposition stated before the study that organizes the whole design. Judged less likely as primary because the results section frames this as "lending support to the hypothesis" (an existing hypothesis from earlier debris-ingestion literature being tested), not as a novel proposition introduced by this paper.

## Notes for the codebook
Clean worked example of a method explicitly self-described by the authors as repurposed from one ecological sub-question (habitat/food selection) to another (debris selection) within the same field — supports keeping this squarely in `recombination`/`gap-filling:next-target` territory rather than `transfer`, since both source and target are within wildlife/marine ecology.
