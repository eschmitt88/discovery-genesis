## Reasoning
With no abstract or full text, the case rests entirely on the title and the reference list's composition. The reference list mixes two literatures that do not normally co-occur: classical coal/char gasification chemical-engineering papers (1952 steam-carbon kinetics, 1985/1992 metal-catalysed char gasification) and early-Earth impact/atmosphere-chemistry papers (1986–2013). That pattern — a chemistry methodology used elsewhere (industrial gasification) being brought to bear on a planetary-science question (does meteoritic iron/nickel catalyse reduction of N2/H2O to NH3/HCN at high temperature) — is the signature of `transfer` (different primary field: chemical engineering → planetary science). The specific finding (volatile yields under a temperature/interaction regime not previously tested) also reads as a first measurement in this system. `genesis_model` is called problem-first because the standing question (can early-Earth reducing-atmosphere volatiles form without invoking a shock event) predates this paper and the ingredient (gas–solid reaction chemistry) already existed; but per the codebook's evidence-based cap, `title+refs-only` forces `genesis_confidence: low` regardless.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is plausible instead: if the paper introduces a new high-temperature reactor/apparatus not previously used for meteorite–water–N2 interactions, the capability itself (rather than the standing early-Earth-atmosphere question) could be what generates the finding. Without the introduction's own framing this cannot be resolved — this is exactly the "papers I could not resolve" case named in the report requirements.
