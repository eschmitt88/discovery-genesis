## Reasoning
No individual technique in the pipeline is new to the field: nonlinear dynamics features and their EEG applications (including to depression, via DFA in 2007) predate this paper, as do the classifiers and genetic-algorithm feature selection. The paper's own stated logic is explicitly about combination and comparison ("compared and contrasted with other reported methods... combining nonlinear features, the performance is enhanced"), which is the textbook definition of recombination within a single field (biomedical signal processing / EEG analysis) rather than transfer across a field boundary.

## Alternative reading
gap-filling:next-target is a plausible alternative frame (nonlinear-feature EEG classification, already used for epilepsy 2005/2006 and Alzheimer's 2008, applied to depression as the next diagnostic target). It was not added as a second label because the abstract's own claimed novelty is specifically the feature combination and its accuracy gain over single features, not merely applying an established pipeline to a new diagnosis — DFA alone had already been applied to depression in 2007, so "depression" is not itself the novel target.

## Notes for the codebook
A useful boundary case for recombination vs gap-filling:next-target: when a paper's own claimed contribution is explicitly "combining previously separate features/methods for better performance" rather than "first time this established pipeline meets this application," recombination is the more faithful label even though a next-target reading is also defensible.
