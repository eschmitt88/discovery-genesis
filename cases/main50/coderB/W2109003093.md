## Reasoning
The abstract itself gives the means-first logic almost verbatim: the paper exists in the form it does because a rare natural event happened to coincide with an era of ubiquitous consumer recording devices, dense field-sensor networks, and modern lab isotopic techniques — capability (widespread, largely non-scientific instrumentation newly capable of capturing such an event) → observation (unprecedented multi-channel measurement of one airburst) → claim (calibration data for NEO hazard models). This is a capability new to the field for this class of event (no prior airburst had comparable ubiquitous civilian documentation), satisfying the v1.1 means-first bar.

## Alternative reading
`problem-first` is a defensible near-tie if one treats "near-Earth-object hazard calibration" as the driving old, established question (planetary defense has sought impact-energy/damage calibration data for decades) that this event opportunistically answered with an existing-but-rare ingredient (an instrumentally well-recorded large airburst). Under the decision order, means-first is checked before problem-first and the abstract's own explicit capability-first framing tips it that way, but this is the closest call among the batch.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a strong worked example for a `resource`-move, means-first genesis card: the "capability" is not a purpose-built scientific instrument but an ambient societal resource (consumer dashcams, social media, pre-existing sensor networks) repurposed opportunistically because a rare event happened to occur within its coverage. Also a good `is_primary: partial` case — the paper's contribution is fundamentally a calibration dataset/characterization of one event rather than a generalizable finding, distinct from a "tool paper" but sharing the artefact-over-finding structure the codebook's `partial` genre is meant to capture.
