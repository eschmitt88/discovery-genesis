## Reasoning
This is a serial-application case: the diagnostic method (multi-view foot radiography scored for named accessory ossicles/sesamoids) is unchanged from decades of prior literature (refs from 1921 through 2008 use essentially the same approach on different national/ethnic populations — Turkish, Arab, now Italian). Per v1.1, that is the explicit tell for problem-first over means-first. `gap-filling:next-target` fits precisely — "established method, new target/population" is the codebook's own wording, and this paper's stated contribution is exactly "new population, established radiographic method."

## Alternative reading
`gap-filling:first-measurement` was considered, since the abstract's "first detailed report... in Italian women" language also matches first-measurement's "quantity measured for the first time in this system." Next-target was preferred because the paper is explicitly one more national-population entry in a recognisable series (Turkish 2006/2008, Arab 2006, now Italian), which is closer to a population-swap than a genuinely new measurement construct.

## Notes for the codebook
Descriptive-epidemiology "prevalence in population X" papers are a recurring genre where next-target vs first-measurement is genuinely close; a worked example distinguishing "next country/ethnicity" (next-target) from "first-ever prevalence of this specific condition in any population" (first-measurement) would help.
