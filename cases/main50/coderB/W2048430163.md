## Reasoning
The reference list's breadth (glacier mass balance, remote sensing, reanalysis climatology, hydrological modelling, and Aral Sea/water-conflict social science, spanning 1988–2012) is far wider than a single empirical study would typically cite, and no single new dataset or method is signalled by the title — this fits the accretion test (the paper's conclusion form, "glaciers are retreating and runoff/water resources are at risk," could plausibly be sketched before assembling the specific numbers). Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing the authors collected or compiled a new unified dataset (e.g., a new regional glacier inventory or mass-balance reanalysis) as the paper's own contribution, which would push toward means-first or partial — dataset.

## Alternative reading
means-first is a real alternative if this is in fact a primary paper compiling a new synthesized dataset (e.g., a new glacier inventory or unified mass-balance record) presented as a "state of the Tien Shan" primary contribution — Nature Climate Change does publish both reviews and major synthesis-as-primary-contribution pieces, and title+refs-only cannot distinguish these, which is exactly why confidence is capped at low.
