## Reasoning
The paper's own stated logic is capability-first: a systematic biochemical screen (not previously applied at this scale to CD-NTases) is run first, and the diverse, partly unanticipated family of enzymes and products (including a novel cyclic-trinucleotide class) is the observation that follows from it — not the answer to a narrowly pre-stated question. Only one self-citation to the group's own prior single-enzyme (DncV) work appears, well short of the 4-8 self-citation problem-first tell, and the screen itself is a qualitative broadening (single enzyme to whole family) rather than the same method rerun on one more target. This satisfies the v1.1 means-first bar: a capability new to the authors' own published scope.

## Alternative reading
`problem-first` is the alternative if "what is the full diversity of bacterial CD-NTases beyond DncV" is read as an already-named open question (following the 2012 DncV finding) closed by re-running an existing single-enzyme assay approach at larger scale. Judged less likely as primary because the abstract/text frame the screen itself, and the resulting discovery of an unanticipated product class (cyclic trinucleotides), as the central novelty — a capability revealing something qualitatively new, not merely extending a known assay to the next gene.

## Notes for the codebook
Good worked example for the `instrument` + `scale` move pairing under means-first: `instrument` captures the new screening capability itself, while `scale` (currently unattested) captures that surveying an entire enzyme family — rather than one homolog — is what surfaces emergent diversity (the trinucleotide class) invisible at single-target scope. Removing `scale` would lose that distinction from a routine single-target `instrument` paper.
