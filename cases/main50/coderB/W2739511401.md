## Reasoning
The zein-nanoparticle-as-Pickering-stabilizer paradigm and the complexing-with-a-second-biopolymer strategy are both well established in the cited 2012–2017 literature (chitosan, tannic acid, lecithin partners already reported); gum arabic is simply the next complexing partner tested, following the codebook's "established method, new target/material" pattern for problem-first/gap-filling:next-target. Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing a genuinely new nanoparticle-formation technique (beyond antisolvent/electrostatic complexation) was developed for this specific pairing.

## Alternative reading
recombination is a plausible secondary reading (joining zein nanoparticle chemistry with gum-arabic complexation, two established food-hydrocolloid techniques) but I did not list it as a second label because it would not change the card's truth if dropped — the paper reads as one more entry in an already-established "zein + X" series rather than a first-time joining of two separate literatures.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a recognisable food-science/materials pattern: an established nanoparticle-stabilizer platform (zein colloidal particles) repeatedly re-paired with a new second biopolymer (chitosan → tannic acid → gum arabic → ...), each publishable as a next-target paper — a good additional worked example for the "serial reuse on next target = problem-first" rule outside biomedicine.
