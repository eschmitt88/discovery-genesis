## Reasoning
This is close to the textbook case the v1.1 rule targets: the method (convergence seminar, grounded in hypothetical retrospection) is cited to the authors' own near-identical prior study on a different technology (nanobiotechnology, 2009), and the present paper's contribution is running it on a new target (BMI) rather than developing a new deliberation method. That is serial reuse of an established platform on a new target → problem-first, gap-filling:next-target. Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing the convergence-seminar format itself was substantially redesigned for BMI (e.g., a materially different scenario-elicitation structure), which would push toward means-first.

## Alternative reading
means-first is the natural alternative if "convergence seminar" is treated as new to this paper rather than reused — but the 2009 nanobiotechnology paper with an almost identical title/structure ("Four Convergence Seminars" vs. "Five Convergence Seminars") makes reuse the more defensible reading even without full text.

## Notes for the codebook
This card is a good worked example for the codebook's own tell: title-pattern reuse across two dossiers ("N Convergence Seminars" on two different technologies) is a strong, cheap signal for the "capability new to the authors" test even absent explicit self-citation counts in the dossier.
