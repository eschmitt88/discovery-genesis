## Reasoning
The full text names the gap and the missing ingredient in the same breath: competing theories existed, but "there is a lack of systematic experiments to which any of the theories can be compared." This paper's contribution is precisely that missing systematic experimental capability (Hele-Shaw geometry, enabling direct visualization of front curvature) — a capability not used in the cited prior work on this system. That is means-first per the codebook: the capability is the reason the finding (front geometry, not contact-angle dynamics, controls the tip) could be established, and the paper's logic runs new-visualization → measurement → analytical model → resolved claim. `Instrument` captures the new experimental capability; `formalisation` is kept as a genuine second label because the paper's other stated contribution is a derived analytical formula for the tip angle from front geometry — removing it would drop half of the abstract's own claim ("We propose a model... and derive resulting tip angles analytically").

## Alternative reading
`Problem-first` is a plausible weaker reading if the Hele-Shaw geometry is treated as a routine visualization technique rather than a new capability for this specific system — freezing-front visualization methods exist elsewhere in solidification physics. It is less likely as primary because the paper's own framing treats the systematic experiment as the specific missing piece that had prevented resolution of the theoretical dispute, not as an incidental method choice.
