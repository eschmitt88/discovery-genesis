## Reasoning
The paper is explicit that its reason for existing is a new capability (automated large-scale standardized dataset construction via R-CNN) that removes the data-scarcity bottleneck blocking deep-learning dermatology diagnosis — this is means-first by the codebook's own definition ("capability → observation → claim"), reinforced by the authors' direct novelty claim ("no previous reports... using R-CNN" for this purpose). It is not the "serial reuse of an established platform" pattern the v1.1 rule assigns to problem-first, since the R-CNN-for-dataset-construction application is claimed new, not reused from the authors' own or others' prior work. Evidence that would change my mind: a citation showing this R-CNN dataset-construction approach had already been used for another skin condition by the same or other authors before this paper.

## Alternative reading
gap-filling:next-target is a real secondary reading — onychomycosis is simply the next skin condition in a sequence (skin cancer 2017, diabetic retinopathy 2016) to get a "deep learning matches specialists" demonstration — which is why I kept it as a second label; but the paper's own emphasis and the harder technical claim is the dataset-construction method, not the target disease, so I ranked instrument first.

## Notes for the codebook
Good example distinguishing this batch's two "AI applied to medical diagnosis" cases: the mitogenome paper (W2963245931) reuses an identical established pipeline on a next species (problem-first), while this paper explicitly claims a new enabling method (R-CNN dataset construction) as the reason its result is possible (means-first) — the discriminating signal in both cases was an explicit textual claim about whether the core method was reused or new, which is worth codifying as the primary evidentiary test rather than relying on venue or field intuition.
