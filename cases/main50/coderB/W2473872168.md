## Reasoning
The title's emphasis on "recurrent" against a reference list of prior single-episode H. cinaedi/XLA case reports (1994, 2004, 2012) suggests the specific new fact is recurrence itself, which fits gap-filling:first-measurement (a phenomenon — recurrence in this pathogen-host pairing — reported for the first time) more than next-target (which would fit a new patient with the already-known single-episode presentation). The diagnostic tooling (MALDI-TOF, blood culture) is established, not new, so problem-first rather than means-first. Evidence that would change my mind: full text showing this case required a genuinely new diagnostic approach to detect the recurrence (→ means-first).

## Alternative reading
is_primary as "case report" genre has no explicit slot in the codebook's `no — <genre>` list (which names review/guideline/consensus/editorial/essay/commentary/surveillance) — I coded it `yes` as primary clinical observation, but a `partial — case report` reading is plausible if the codebook intends narrow single-patient reports to sit alongside dataset/tool/protocol papers as "artefact rather than finding"; flagged here rather than silently picked.

## Notes for the codebook
Case reports are a distinct genre the current is_primary vocabulary doesn't explicitly address — worth a decision on whether isolated single-patient case reports are `yes` (primary but narrow), a new `no — case-report` genre, or `partial — case-report`, since they recur in medical dossiers and the codebook currently forces a judgment call each time.
