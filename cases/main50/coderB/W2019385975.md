## Reasoning
The paper's own title places the enabling method (whole-genome sequencing) front and center, and the resulting claims (strand-of-template-specific transition bias, specific trinucleotide-context hotspots, indel rate relative to substitution rate) require genome-wide coverage that the field's prior reporter-gene assays (rpoB, lacI) structurally could not deliver — those assays sample one locus. This is the means-first signature: a capability (genome-wide MA-line sequencing) new to this application is the reason the fine-grained findings exist, and the logic runs capability → observation → mechanistic claim. The move is coded primarily as `instrument` (the new capability is deploying WGS on MA lines) with `gap-filling:first-measurement` as a secondary label for the specific first-time-measured biases (context hotspots, strand asymmetry reversal under MMR loss) that the capability made visible; removing the second label would understate that several of the abstract's headline findings are explicitly "first observed" quantities.

## Alternative reading
`gap-filling:next-target` is a plausible weaker alternative if this is read as "apply whole-genome MA sequencing (already used in yeast, 2008) to E. coli, the next organism" — the yeast whole-genome MA-sequencing precedent (2008, cited) is real. This was set aside as primary because the paper's own framing and the very specific/mechanistic nature of the claims (leading vs lagging strand template bias, trinucleotide context) argue the capability itself, not just a species swap, is doing the explanatory work.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a good worked example for means-first in the "new instrumentation applied to an old experimental paradigm" flavor (MA experiments existed since 1991; WGS is the new ingredient) — distinct from the codebook's existing means-first examples, which tend to be assay/screen-driven rather than sequencing-driven.
