## Reasoning
The paper joins three separately well-established theoretical constructs (memorable tourism experience, place attachment/identity, and behavioral intention), all rooted in tourism/environmental/consumer psychology — the same broad field — into one structural model, tested via a standard SEM survey methodology (Fornell and Larcker, 1981) that likewise predates the paper by decades. No new instrument or measurement approach is introduced; the specific combination and context (local food consumption as the experiential driver) is what's new. That is rule-3 problem-first with `recombination` as the move, since the constructs originate within reach of the same field rather than crossing a field boundary.

## Alternative reading
`idea-first` is the alternative if the paper is read as testing one bold, freestanding proposition (e.g., "local food consumption is the primary driver of tourist place attachment") stated before the survey was designed. Judged less likely as primary because the abstract's framing ("to model the memorable travel experiences, place attachment and behavioral intentions") reads as an integrative empirical test of established relationships in a new context rather than a single novel proposition driving the design.
