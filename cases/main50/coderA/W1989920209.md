## Reasoning
The abstract and full text both foreground a new observational capability — imaging the solidification front's actual shape and dynamics in a Hele-Shaw cell, plus systematic angle measurement across conditions — as what makes the finding possible: it directly contradicts the field's standing planar-front assumption (quoted: "the front does not remain planar"), which had already been disputed in a published Comment/Response exchange one year earlier. That capability-to-observation-to-claim logic is means-first per codebook §1 rule 2. Two move labels are both load-bearing: `instrument` for the new visualization/measurement setup that drives the result, and `anomaly` for the paper's explicit role in resolving a documented discrepancy between the planar-front model's predictions and observation (evidenced by the cited Comment/Response pair) — removing either loses part of the story.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a plausible near-miss if Hele-Shaw visualization of solidification fronts is itself a well-established technique in this group's or the field's prior work (Hele-Shaw cells are a classical fluid-dynamics tool generally), in which case this becomes `gap-filling:first-measurement` (established visualization technique, first applied to directly resolve this specific front-shape controversy). The dossier's reference list shows no prior citation of Hele-Shaw use for freezing-front visualization specifically, which is why I favored means-first, but the general technique's age elsewhere argues for keeping this as the second reading rather than dismissing it.
