## Reasoning
Title+refs-only evidence caps confidence at low by rule. The reference list is telling: statistics/econometrics texts (1911-1994) supply the method, and a cluster of 2008-2011 papers on wind's price effects in Germany, Denmark, and ERCOT specifically supply the immediate context — including one 2010 paper on "Relationship Between Wind Generation and Balancing Energy Market Prices in ERCOT: 2007-2009," a near-identical precedent. The title's emphasis on "variance" alongside "level" suggests the paper's specific contribution is a first measurement of the variance effect, not just another level study — hence gap-filling:first-measurement over next-target. Evidence that would change this: an abstract confirming whether variance was already measured by the 2010 ERCOT precedent (in which case this would be bigger-n/next-target instead).

## Alternative reading
Could equally be gap-filling:next-target if "the Texas experience" is simply the next zonal market studied after Germany/Denmark, with variance a minor addition rather than the main claim — less likely given the title puts "and variance" on equal footing with "level."
