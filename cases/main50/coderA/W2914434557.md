## Reasoning
Explicitly self-labeled a narrative review synthesizing existing literature plus author experience; the five-layer schema is presented as an organizing synthesis of previously separate anatomical descriptions (SMAS 1976, retaining ligaments 1989, individual fat compartments 2007-2017), not a tested proposition or new dataset. Per codebook §1 rule 1, the review's conclusion form (a layered facial model) could be, and largely was, assembled from prior findings with the schematic detail filled in — accretion, `consolidation`.

## Alternative reading
The v1.1 "framework papers with no data" rule (`is_primary: partial — framework`, `idea-first` if the structure organizes the design) is a plausible alternative reading of the five-layer schema, since it is presented as a novel organizing model going forward into clinical practice. I did not use it because the paper is self-described as a review of existing literature rather than a proposed framework meant to generate new testable structure — the schema organizes what is already known rather than being advanced as a new proposition.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a useful boundary case between plain accretion/consolidation and the v1.1 "framework paper" rule: a review that produces a named organizing schema (here, "five layers") sits between the two. A worked example distinguishing "review that synthesizes into a descriptive schema" from "framework paper that proposes a structure for future work" would help future coders place these consistently.
