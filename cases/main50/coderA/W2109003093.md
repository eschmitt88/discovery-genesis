## Reasoning
This was the closest call in the batch between means-first and problem-first. The abstract's own framing leans toward "capability enabled this" (modern electronics/sensors made unprecedented measurement possible), which is the surface signature of means-first. But applying the frozen v1.1 rule, the relevant capabilities — global infrasound networks, meteor photogrammetry, meteorite classification, isotope dating — are not new to this author group or field; several co-authors (Jenniskens in particular) have prior papers on structurally similar, smaller events (2008 TC3 fall and recovery, Sutter's Mill meteorite) cited in the reference list, i.e. the same toolkit cited across multiple of the authors' own prior works, the explicit tell for problem-first. What's genuinely new is the target (this specific, uniquely large and socially consequential event), not the observational capability. I coded gap-filling:first-measurement because the paper's core contribution is measuring specific quantities (orbit, mass, energy yield, meteorite classification) for the first time for this system/event, using pre-existing methods.

## Alternative reading
Means-first remains a real possibility: the sheer ubiquity of consumer dashcam footage in Russia in 2013 was itself a new, not-previously-exploited data source for meteor science (distinct from the field's traditional dedicated camera networks), and if that specific data type is what made this level of characterization possible, the paper's logic would run capability (dashcam ubiquity) → observation → claim, which is means-first. I weighted the "same toolkit, same authors, larger target" pattern slightly more heavily, but confidence is only medium.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a strong candidate worked example for the "opportunistic exploitation of a serendipitous new data source (dashcams) vs. reuse of an established scientific toolkit on a bigger/singular target" boundary — the two readings are both defensible and the codebook could use an explicit tiebreaker for "capability that is new-to-the-world but not deliberately built by the authors" (crowdsourced/incidental data sources).
