## Reasoning
With no abstract or full text, the only signal is the title and the reference list, which is essentially the canonical bibliography of clean/nil-clean ring theory (Nicholson's exchange-ring and clean-ring papers, the 2013 "Nil clean rings" paper, several 2013-2014 papers on nil-clean matrix factorizations). The title itself ("Nil-clean and strongly nil-clean rings") reads as the next natural refinement in this line: nil-clean rings were defined in 2013, and "strongly" variants of clean-ring concepts are a standard move in this literature (cf. "strongly clean rings", cited via the 2001 "Extensions of clean rings" and related work). This is a specific, already-open structural question (how does the nil-clean property interact with a commuting/strong condition) closed with tools already in hand — no new instrument, dataset, or computational capability is implied by the reference list. That makes it problem-first rather than means-first. It is not accretion (this is not a survey or database) and not idea-first (there is no single falsifiable proposition stated before data collection — it is definitional/structural characterization). Evidence would change my mind: an abstract stating a genuinely new proof technique or computational search method not present in the prior nil-clean literature would push this toward means-first.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is the plausible alternative if the "strongly" variant required a genuinely new proof technique (e.g., a new matrix-ring construction) not reducible to prior clean-ring methods — the 2013 "A nil-clean 2×2 matrix over the integers which is not clean" and "A class of formal matrix rings" references hint at matrix-construction techniques that could be doing real work. Without the paper text I cannot rule this out, hence low confidence.

## Notes for the codebook
Pure-mathematics papers with no abstract are the hardest case for genesis_model: the reference list alone can support problem vs. accretion but rarely distinguishes problem-first from means-first, because a "new proof technique" enabler leaves the same citation footprint as a "reuse of standard tools" one. Worth flagging title+refs-only math papers as a distinct hard case alongside the abstract-only means/idea cap.
