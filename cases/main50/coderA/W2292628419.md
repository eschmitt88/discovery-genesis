## Reasoning
The abstract explicitly states the specific limitations of the prior art (poor adhesion, chemical susceptibility, translucency/modulation tradeoff of single-layer VO2 films) and the paper's contribution is combining three already-established, separately-studied coating technologies (VO2 thermochromism, SiO2 as a barrier layer, TiO2 photocatalytic self-cleaning) into one multilayer stack via a single established deposition method (APCVD). All three components and the deposition technique predate this paper (VO2/TiO2 multilayer designs even appear directly in the reference list from 2003). This is recombination — joining materials/techniques from within the same field (transition-metal-oxide thin-film materials science), not a field-boundary transfer — under problem-first, since the specific combination and its resulting improved properties is the new question closed with existing tools.

## Alternative reading
Means-first would require the specific SiO2-diffusion-barrier mechanism or the APCVD process itself to be a new capability rather than a known technique — but APCVD for VO2 is cited back to 2004 and TiO2/VO2 multilayers are cited directly in the 2003 reference, so the components are clearly pre-existing.

## Notes for the codebook
Materials-science "combine three known coating technologies into a multifunctional stack" papers are a clean recombination pattern (same field, no transfer); useful contrast with the radar-absorber paper (W2010048157) in this batch, which is next-target rather than recombination because it varies one component (unit-cell geometry) within an already-combined design rather than combining previously separate technologies for the first time.
