## Reasoning
The paper is explicit that its key methodological ingredient — the biophysics-based fitness landscape — is "a previously developed approach" (their own 2011 paper), deployed here specifically because it is "virtually parameter-free" and avoids the ad hoc DFE/epistasis assumptions of prior lethal-mutagenesis models. This is the frozen rule's paradigm case: an established capability from the authors' own prior work, reused to close a clearly stated open question (how N and U jointly determine extinction time) — problem-first, not means-first. The central move is recombination: joining two previously separate theoretical bodies (infinite-N lethal-mutagenesis theory and finite-N population-genetics/drift theory), both within theoretical population genetics/evolutionary biology (same field, so not transfer), via simulation.

## Alternative reading
Means-first would apply if the specific combination of the biophysical fitness landscape with finite-N stochastic simulation constituted a new computational capability not previously exercised even by the authors — but the text's own "previously developed approach" language argues against this, and the paper's stated motivation is explicitly the open question, not a new tool.
