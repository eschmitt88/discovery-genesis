## Reasoning
The full text is explicit and unambiguous: the paper reports "the first mode-locked laser based on a nonlinear monolithic high-Q factor resonator," names a new mechanism ("filter-driven four-wave mixing"), and states the key novelty is the resonator's dual role as filter and nonlinear element — none of which appears in the authors' cited prior work. Per the v1.1 tell, a capability introduced in this paper (not cited earlier, not reused 4-8x from prior self-citations) is means-first. Both move labels are needed: `instrument` for the new resonator-based mode-locking device itself, and `recombination` for how it was built — joining an already-established dissipative-FWM mode-locking concept (fibre lasers, 1997-2008) with already-established high-Q microresonator technology (previously used only for frequency-comb generation, 2007) in a new combined role.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first would apply if "filter-driven four-wave mixing" turns out, on closer reading, to be a straightforward relabeling of the existing dissipative-FWM mechanism applied to a new resonator platform rather than a genuinely new physical mechanism — the full text's emphasis on the resonator's novel dual role argues against this, but it remains the natural fallback reading.
