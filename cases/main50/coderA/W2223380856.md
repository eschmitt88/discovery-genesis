## Reasoning
The abstract frames a specific modeling gap (no prior study jointly modeled memorable food experiences, place attachment, and behavioral intention) closed by combining two pre-existing, separately-developed constructs and measurement instruments (memorable tourism experience scale 2010; place attachment scales going back to 1983) via standard SEM survey methodology. Both constructs and the method sit within the same field (tourism/consumer behavior research), so this is recombination rather than transfer, and problem-first rather than means-first since SEM survey methodology is standard and not newly introduced.

## Alternative reading
gap-filling:next-target is a plausible alternative primary label if this is read as simply applying the existing memorable-experience-plus-place-attachment model to a new content domain (food) rather than genuinely joining two separate literatures for the first time — I judged recombination more central because the abstract frames the joint modeling of the two constructs, not just the domain application, as the contribution.

## Notes for the codebook
Tourism/consumer-behavior SEM papers that combine two established scales for a new topical application are a recurring recombination-vs-next-target boundary case; worth a worked example distinguishing "joining two constructs for the first time" (recombination) from "applying an already-joined model to a new domain" (next-target).
