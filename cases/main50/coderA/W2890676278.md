## Reasoning
Although the genre is a review (an Accounts of Chemical Research "Account," which the open-coding rule would default to accretion), the abstract itself explicitly overrides that default: it frames the underlying research as capability-driven ("permits reactions... previously impossible") rather than as pure synthesis of pre-existing findings, and the reference list is dominated by the same authors' own 2015-2018 papers extending one precatalyst platform from N-C to O-C bond activation. That is a means-first genesis narrative (capability -> observation -> claim) reported via a review vehicle. `unification` is listed because the Account explicitly argues one mechanistic principle (controlled resonance/rotational barrier) governs both previously-separate amide-activation and ester-activation cross-coupling literatures; `instrument` because the precatalyst platform itself is the enabling new tool.

## Alternative reading
accretion is the closer default per the open-coding review rule ("code them as accretion unless the dossier shows otherwise") — if the underlying primary papers this Account summarises turn out to be closer to problem-first (established Pd-NHC chemistry serially applied to the next inert-bond target), the Account itself would be better read as a straightforward aggregation rather than a means-first narrative.

## Notes for the codebook
Good test case for the review-default rule's "unless the dossier shows otherwise" clause — Accounts of Chemical Research pieces routinely narrate a means-first or unification story about the authors' own primary work, and I think that should generally override the accretion default when the abstract itself uses capability-first language this explicitly.
