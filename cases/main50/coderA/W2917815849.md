## Reasoning
Unlike most of the batch, this paper's own logic runs capability → observation → claim: the systematic screen across many CD-NTase homologs (rather than the field's prior enzyme-by-enzyme characterization) is what surfaces products no one had looked for, and the paper's language repeatedly marks the result as unexpected ("Surprisingly...", "an unexpected class of cyclic trinucleotide compounds"). This is means-first: the screening-across-a-superfamily capability is what makes the discovery possible, not a specific named prior question. I coded anomaly as the primary move because the central finding (pyrimidine-containing and trinucleotide CDNs) directly contradicts the field's prior assumption that all natural CDNs are purine-only — a genuine surprise relative to the existing framework, not just a next-measurement. gap-filling:first-measurement is listed second because the paper does also report first characterizations of specific new compounds and structures, and removing it would understate that concrete new quantities/structures are measured, not just an anomaly noted.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a plausible alternative if the DncV/cGAS structural-biology toolkit used here (some from this group's own 2015 prior work) is judged not genuinely new, making this "established structural methods applied to the next homolog family" rather than a new capability — but the explicit framing as a systematic screen (a scope/scale change in how the family is studied) and the field-surprising result tip this toward means-first in my judgment.
