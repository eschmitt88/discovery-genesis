## Reasoning
Solid lipid particle encapsulation via hot lipid emulsion is a pharmaceutical/drug-delivery technique with its own separate literature, not previously used by these authors on this enzyme and explicitly flagged as first-reported here — this crosses a field boundary (pharmaceutics/nanomedicine into microbial enzyme biotechnology), matching transfer rather than recombination, and the "introduced in this paper" tell makes it means-first rather than problem-first even though SLP encapsulation itself is not new to the broader pharmaceutical field. Evidence that would change my mind: if a self-citation showed these authors already used SLP encapsulation on a different enzyme before this paper.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is a plausible alternative if SLP encapsulation is treated purely as a "field-established" ingredient regardless of who used it — the v1.1 rule's "capability new to the authors" clause is what tips this toward means-first instead; a stricter "new to the field" reading (which the rule explicitly rejects in favor of "new to the authors or field") would instead classify this as gap-filling:next-target.

## Notes for the codebook
Direct worked example of the "new to the authors, not necessarily new to the field" tell — this card would only flip to problem-first under a stricter novelty bar than what v1.1 currently states, which is worth flagging as a possible future ambiguity if disagreement recurs at n=50.
