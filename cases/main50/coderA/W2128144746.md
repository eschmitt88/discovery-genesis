## Reasoning
This is a reliability/validation study of an existing clinical instrument (CAPS-A) extended to a new population (American/Canadian English-speaking children, versus the original British-English validation) and paired with a training intervention. The specific open question — is the modified instrument reliable, and does training help — is new and explicitly stated; the instrument and statistical methods (kappa, ICC) are established. This fits gap-filling:next-target (established instrument, new target population) under problem-first. is_primary is coded partial rather than yes because the paper's contribution is fundamentally a psychometric/methods validation (establishing reliability of a protocol) rather than a substantive clinical finding, matching the codebook's "protocol papers: primary work whose contribution is an artefact [validated instrument] rather than a finding."

## Alternative reading
Means-first would require the CAPS-A Americleft Modification itself to be a qualitatively new assessment instrument rather than a modification of the existing British CAPS-A — the abstract's phrase "modified version of the CAPS-A" suggests incremental adaptation, but I cannot rule out a more substantial redesign without full text.
