## Reasoning
The paper's own framing (abstract) is diagnostic: a named, fairly specific question (markers of noble-rot extent in Chenin blanc) is closed with methods (texture tests, phenolic HPLC, colourimetry) that are all cited from 2007-2011 prior work, not introduced here. That is problem-first by codebook §1.3. The move is gap-filling:next-target: an established assay combination (already used for grape ripening/mechanical-property studies on other cultivars) applied to a new target system (noble rot specifically on Chenin blanc). Evidence that would change my mind: a full-text introduction showing the puncture/compression protocol itself was newly developed for this study rather than adapted from the 2009 reference.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is plausible if the specific combination of puncture+compression+colour+phenolics as a joint discriminating protocol counts as a "new assay," but each component method is individually pre-existing and cited, and the paper explicitly frames itself as answering an open characterisation question rather than showcasing a new instrument — so problem-first is more likely.

## Notes for the codebook
Borderline case for whether "combining several existing single-purpose assays into one discriminating panel" should ever count as means-first (new-assay-or-method) versus gap-filling:next-target. I read the "capability new to authors/field" bar as not met here since each component assay is cited as external prior art, but a rule of thumb for "how many components before combination = new assay" would help.
