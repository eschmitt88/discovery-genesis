## Reasoning
No new imaging technique or classification scheme is introduced — MRI and the Peetrons grading are pre-existing. The paper's contribution is running the existing question at a scale (23 teams, 4 seasons, 516 injuries) well beyond prior single-team or shorter studies, which is the definition of gap-filling:bigger-n. Evidence that would change my mind: a full text showing a novel imaging protocol modification, which the abstract does not suggest.

## Alternative reading
Gap-filling:first-measurement is a weaker alternative — the specific finding that grade-0/1 injuries account for the majority of lay-off days could be framed as a first measurement, but the study's design (large multi-team prospective cohort) is centrally a bigger-n extension of an already-studied MRI-prognosis association.
