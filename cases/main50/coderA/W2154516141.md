## Reasoning
This is the exact "mature assay/platform applied to the next target" case the v1.1 rule was written to resolve: bevacizumab is not new (already tested by GOG in ovarian and cervical cancer per the reference list), and the phase II trial infrastructure itself is a decades-long GOG series applied serially to different agents and different tumor types. The specific open question (does bevacizumab work in endometrial cancer specifically) is what's new, not the ingredient. Evidence that would change my mind: if the introduction described a novel biomarker-stratification design not used in prior GOG trials.

## Alternative reading
Means-first would require the trial to hinge on a new capability (e.g., a new VEGF-A assay); the abstract does mention VEGF-A assessed by immunohistochemistry and ELISA, but these are described as standard correlative assays, not the trial's central enabling capability — the design is squarely an agent-in-new-tumor-type test.

## Notes for the codebook
This is close to a canonical worked example for the v1.1 problem-first tell (agent cited in the group's own prior trials across multiple tumor types) — could be used directly in the codebook.
