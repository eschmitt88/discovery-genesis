## Reasoning
The abstract's own narrative is capability-first: a new analytical model is derived, then used to design a fiber whose measured crosstalk and attenuation are reported as record-low. Per codebook §1 rule 2 this is means-first. The enabling "capability" here is genuinely a new formal model rather than an established platform reused on a new target — the MCF field itself is only ~2 years old in the reference list (earliest MCF-specific paper 2009), which argues against the "serial reuse of an established platform" pattern that would flip this to problem-first. Two move labels are needed: `instrument` for the new predictive model that drives the design, and `recombination` for combining trench-assisted core design (previously used for single-core macrobend-loss reduction) with a hexagonal multi-core architecture and pure-silica cores — removing either loses part of why the fiber achieves both record-low crosstalk (trench+geometry) and record-low attenuation (pure silica), which the abstract calls out as two separate headline numbers.

## Alternative reading
Problem-first is plausible if, with full text, the "new approximation model" turns out to be a straightforward combination of two long-established tools (1972 coupled-mode theory, an unremarkable equivalent-index approximation) rather than a genuine new capability — in that case this becomes `gap-filling:next-target` (established modeling approach, new target: this specific trench-assisted 7-core geometry). The abstract alone cannot fully resolve this, hence medium rather than high confidence.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a good candidate worked example for "capability new to the field" (as opposed to new to the authors specifically) — the MCF sub-field is young enough (2009 origin) that no author has 4-8x self-citation history to lean on, making the means-first call rest on field-youth rather than the usual authors'-own-prior-work tell.
