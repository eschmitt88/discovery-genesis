## Reasoning
The abstract's own framing is that the generation technique (impact piston) is old ("a centuries-old technology") and that the specific gap is quality/reproducibility of the resulting slug — closed here by applying an existing diagnostic (PVDF shock gauge, previously used for droplet impact forces per the 2010 reference) to measure, apparently for the first time in this specific configuration, the coherence and stress waveform of a slug from this generator. This favors problem-first with gap-filling:first-measurement: the quantity (coherence/endurance of the jet, water-hammer waveform) is measured for the first time in this system, using tools (piston generator, PVDF gauge) that already existed.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is plausible if the PVDF-in-Kel-F-target embedding scheme constitutes a new measurement capability rather than a straightforward reuse of the 2010 droplet-impact PVDF technique — the abstract's phrase "impact impulse...was measured...using an embedded PVDF shock gauge" doesn't distinguish routine application from novel instrumentation. This is the main source of my medium (not high) confidence.

## Notes for the codebook
Instrumentation-adjacent physics/engineering papers where an existing diagnostic (PVDF gauges, standard sensors) is redeployed to characterize an existing but previously unmeasured process are a recurring first-measurement pattern; worth a worked example in the codebook alongside the biomedical ones, since the "capability new to authors/field" test applies just as cleanly to instrumentation reuse as to assay reuse.
