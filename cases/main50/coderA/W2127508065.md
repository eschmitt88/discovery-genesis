## Reasoning
The method (radiographic identification of named accessory ossicles/sesamoid bones) traces to 1921 and has already been used for prevalence surveys in other populations (Turkish 2006/2008, Arab 2006) per the reference list. This paper's contribution is a new-population prevalence survey using that established method — problem-first, `gap-filling:next-target`. It also reads plausibly as `gap-filling:first-measurement` (first prevalence rate for this specific subgroup); I chose next-target because the codebook's own definition of next-target explicitly includes "new target/population."

## Alternative reading
`gap-filling:first-measurement` is nearly as strong a fit given the abstract's own "first detailed report" framing — the two sub-labels overlap substantially for population-prevalence-survey papers like this one.

## Notes for the codebook
Population-prevalence surveys using an old, standard measurement protocol are a recurring source of ambiguity between `next-target` and `first-measurement`; a worked example distinguishing them (or a note that they may co-occur) would help.
