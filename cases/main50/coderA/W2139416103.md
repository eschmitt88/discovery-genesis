## Reasoning
This is the cleanest exemplar in the batch of the frozen v1.1 rule: the abstract explicitly frames the paper as extending an established method ("the pattern perturbation method is extended to the WDM case") from its original single-channel scope to a broader target (multi-channel WDM systems), and the reference list shows this exact method cited across five entries spanning 2003-2009 in a narrow sub-field where two of the three authors (Forestieri) recur — the "same method cited 4-8 times in the authors' own prior work" tell named directly in the codebook. This is problem-first, gap-filling:next-target: an established method extended to the next target (from single-channel to multi-channel systems), not a new capability.

## Alternative reading
Means-first could be argued if extending the state-space to include multiple channels' bit patterns simultaneously is judged a qualitatively new computational capability (making tractable an estimation problem that was previously intractable) rather than a straightforward generalization. I judged this problem-first because the paper's own language frames it as an extension of an existing method to a broader domain, not as introducing a new technique from scratch.
