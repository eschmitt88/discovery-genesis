## Reasoning
This is the clean, textbook case the codebook's v1.1 rule was written for: "next species" is named explicitly as a worked example of the established-platform/next-target pattern, and the paper's own full text signals this by reusing an almost identical descriptive sentence to four prior single-species mitogenome papers. Sequencing kit, annotation approach and Bayesian phylogenetic method are all cited as pre-existing; the only new element is the target species itself. That is problem-first / gap-filling:next-target with high confidence given full-text evidence removes ambiguity.

## Alternative reading
means-first would apply only if the sequencing technology itself were new to this group or the field, which the full text gives no indication of (a routine commercial sequencing service is used); accretion is not applicable since the paper reports a specific new empirical result (one species' genome and its phylogenetic placement), not an aggregate survey.

## Notes for the codebook
This dossier is a strong worked example for the codebook's "next species" tell — the near-verbatim reused sentence across five papers (four cited plus this one) is about as explicit a signature of serial platform reuse as this corpus is likely to produce, and could be cited directly as the codebook's canonical example.
