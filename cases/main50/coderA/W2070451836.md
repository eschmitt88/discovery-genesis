## Reasoning
No new instrument, dye, or protocol is introduced — the paper takes an existing, mature commercial kit and existing readout platforms (microplate reader, microscope) and tests them against new targets (multiple species, both readout types) to see where the established assumptions break. That is the gap-filling:next-target pattern applied to a methods-validation paper rather than a substantive biological question. Evidence that would change my mind: if the authors had designed a new dye or labeling chemistry rather than reusing the commercial kit as-is.

## Alternative reading
Could be coded as an anomaly paper (the Gram-negative-specific SYTO9 artifact is an unexpected finding), but the paper's stated aim and design are explicitly a systematic validation sweep, not a report of a single surprising observation — so gap-filling fits the paper's own framing better than anomaly.

## Notes for the codebook
Methods-critique/validation papers (existing assay tested for failure modes across new conditions) don't map cleanly onto next-target vs first-measurement vs anomaly; "next-target" is my best fit but the fit is loose — worth a worked example in the codebook.
