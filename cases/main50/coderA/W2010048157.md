## Reasoning
The abstract frames this as an engineering optimization within an established design space: FSS-embedded magnetic composite RAMs have a substantial prior literature (double-square loop absorbers 2007, ultra-thin FSS-embedded designs 2010, resistive FSS absorbers 2012, circuit-analog double-square-loop 2013). The paper's specific contribution — a treble-square-loop geometry and the resulting bandwidth/thickness tradeoff — is a next design point in this established line, verified with the standard simulation tool (HFSS) already used across the field. No new instrument, dataset, or theoretical framework is introduced; the ingredient set (FSS theory, EW-filled silicone-rubber absorber, HFSS) all predates this paper. This is problem-first with gap-filling:next-target: an established method (FSS-in-magnetic-RAM) applied to a new design/target (a novel unit-cell shape optimizing bandwidth further).

## Alternative reading
Means-first would require the treble-square-loop geometry itself to constitute a qualitatively new design capability rather than an incremental variant of the double-square-loop family. Without full text I cannot confirm whether the geometry required a new design method (e.g., a novel optimization algorithm) versus routine parametric HFSS sweeps, which caps confidence at medium.

## Notes for the codebook
Applied-engineering "new unit-cell / new composition, same established design methodology" papers are common and cleanly fit gap-filling:next-target; the risk is over-labeling every such paper as means-first because a specific geometry or material combination is "new" — the frozen rule (capability new to authors/field, not just the specific artifact) is the right filter here.
