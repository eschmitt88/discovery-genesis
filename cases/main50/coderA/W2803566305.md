## Reasoning
The abstract reads as a field-history narrative rather than a report of new data — it describes what "has been" developed and shown, cites clinical trials run by named companies rather than the authors' own experiments, and the reference list spans three distinct research eras spanning 50+ years. That matches `is_primary: no — review` and, per the review default rule, `genesis_model: accretion` (the conclusion's form — "GalNAc conjugates solved liver delivery, next is other tissues" — could be stated before assembling the specific citation list).

## Alternative reading
The embedded story is itself a clean means-first narrative at one level down: ASGPR receptor biology (1970s-80s) is an old capability that, once combined with a specific engineering advance (multivalent triantennary GalNAc conjugation, cited ~2014), enabled potent hepatocyte-targeted RNAi. If this dossier were the primary GalNAc-conjugate paper itself rather than a review of it, means-first would be the call — but this specific paper is the retrospective review, not that primary paper.

## Notes for the codebook
Review articles that narrate a means-first discovery story (a capability unlocking a therapeutic class) are a recurring pattern — the codebook's "reviews with an embedded unsignposted dataset" note covers unsignposted primary data, but not this related case of a review whose *narrated history* is itself a clean genesis story about a different (cited) primary paper. Worth a note that the review's own genesis_model is scored on the review's contribution, not the story it tells about others' work.
