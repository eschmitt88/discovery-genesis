## Reasoning
No new instrument or method is introduced; CT and standard clinical follow-up are routine. What changed is that a referral-pattern shift (accessibility to the center) accumulated enough cases to characterize the finding as a group for the first time, where prior literature was single case reports. This is a first-measurement gap-filling move — a quantity (associations, prognosis, rate of concomitant craniosynostosis) measured for the first time in a series rather than anecdotally. Evidence that would change my mind: a full text revealing a new imaging protocol rather than routine CT.

## Alternative reading
Could be read as accretion (case-series/registry aggregation) rather than problem-first, but the abstract poses and answers a specific clinical question (relationship to craniosynostosis, prognosis) rather than simply reporting descriptive statistics with no analytic aim — so problem-first fits better.

## Notes for the codebook
