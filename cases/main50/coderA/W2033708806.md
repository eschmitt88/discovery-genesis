## Reasoning
The abstract explicitly states the clinical motivation (BRAF inhibitors cause cell-cycle arrest/apoptosis but efficacy is limited) and the mechanistic finding is a specific, previously unrecognized regulatory connection between two established literatures — MAPK/MITF signaling in melanoma and PGC1α-driven mitochondrial biogenesis (established since 1999-2001 in other tissue contexts). Because both halves of the connection, and the standard techniques used to test it, predate this paper, I coded this problem-first rather than means-first: the open question ("why don't BRAF inhibitors work better") is what's new, not the toolkit. Recombination is the central move (joining two previously separate regulatory literatures within cancer/molecular biology, same field, so not transfer); gap-filling:first-measurement is listed second because the specific MITF→PGC1α regulatory link is measured for the first time and removing it would understate that this is also a discovery of a new causal edge, not just a recombination of ideas.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is plausible if this group's specific combination of metabolic-flux profiling with BRAF-inhibitor treatment in melanoma models required a new assay platform not previously used in melanoma research — the abstract doesn't specify methods in enough detail to confirm or rule this out.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a good example of the recombination/gap-filling:first-measurement pairing being genuinely load-bearing rather than a default second label: recombination explains why the paper is interesting (joining two literatures) but gap-filling:first-measurement is needed to capture that a specific new causal claim, not just a synthesis, is being made.
