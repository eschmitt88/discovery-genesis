## Reasoning
The instrument (pulse oximetry) is decades old and explicitly described as established practice elsewhere; nothing about its use here is new to the field or to these authors. What is new is the target population — general inpatients, not just pneumonia cases, in a setting where oximetry had not previously been systematically deployed. This is the "first-measurement" gap-filling sub-type: a quantity (hypoxemia prevalence in this population) measured for the first time. Evidence that would change my mind: if this exact hospital/population combination had already been studied by a cited paper (none appears to have been).

## Alternative reading
Could be read as gap-filling:next-target (established instrument, new population) rather than first-measurement — the two sub-labels overlap here since the "new target" and "first measurement" are the same act. I favor first-measurement because the abstract's own framing is about an unmeasured prevalence, not a new demographic per se.

## Notes for the codebook
This is a clean instance where next-target and first-measurement nearly coincide (new population = first time anyone measured this quantity in it); a rule for which sub-label wins when both technically apply would help.
