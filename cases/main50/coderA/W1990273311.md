## Reasoning
The abstract (via europepmc, treated as the authors' real abstract) frames a specific, narrow open question — which leukocyte population expresses FasL in this species — closed using assays (PHA stimulation, antibody-based phenotyping) that the reference list shows were already established for flounder leukocytes by apparently the same group (2009 "Production and characterization of monoclonal antibodies to leukocytes...in Japanese flounder"; 2007 "In vitro leukocyte-encapsulation model in rainbow trout"; 2010 "Activity of Granulocytes and Chemokines..."). FasL itself was already cloned and functionally characterized in this species in 2006. This is a textbook case of the v1.1 rule: an established platform (this group's flounder immunophenotyping toolkit) redirected at the next specific molecular question, not a new capability — problem-first, gap-filling:next-target.

## Alternative reading
Means-first would apply if the PHA-binding/phenotyping combination used here was itself newly assembled for this study rather than reused from the group's prior papers — the abstract doesn't make clear whether the dual PHA-binding-plus-phenotype gating strategy is novel methodology or a straightforward application of existing tools. Full text would resolve this.

## Notes for the codebook
Comparative-immunology "characterize X in species Y" papers are common and mostly resolve cleanly to gap-filling:next-target once the reference list shows the assay platform predates the paper (often from the same group's own prior species-specific work) — a good template case for the frozen means/problem rule.
