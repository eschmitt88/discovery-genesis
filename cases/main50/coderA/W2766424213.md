## Reasoning
No new measurement instrument is introduced; the survey scales and general design approach for studying obesity attitudes/barriers are established (cited from 2001-2013). What is new is scale and simultaneity — a much larger sample than the cited 2006 pilot, and three stakeholder groups surveyed together rather than one at a time — the textbook bigger-n gap-filling pattern. Evidence that would change my mind: a full text showing a novel psychometric instrument developed specifically for this study rather than established scales.

## Alternative reading
gap-filling:first-measurement is plausible if the tri-stakeholder simultaneity (not just larger n within one group) is treated as a qualitatively new measurement rather than a bigger version of an old one; I favor bigger-n because the core survey content (perceptions, barriers, responsibility) directly extends the cited 2006 pilot's questions rather than measuring a genuinely new construct.

## Notes for the codebook
Multi-stakeholder national surveys that extend a named smaller pilot study are a clean bigger-n case; the "new-dataset-or-resource" vs "routine-data-wave" enabler distinction was the harder call here — I used new-dataset-or-resource because this reads as a purpose-built one-off industry-sponsored survey (ACTION-IO), not a recurring institutional bulletin.
