## Reasoning
No single new instrument or assay drives this paper; instead, three already-established assay families — allele-specific-expression quantification (used since the late 1990s for IL-2/IL-4/IL-10), hnRNP-H/splicing-regulation binding assays (used for many other genes since 2000), and microRNA-mediated knockdown (used in the authors' own 2012 paper) — are combined to answer a specific, previously open mechanistic question about one gene. The abstract's framing of Isoform 2 as previously "of unknown function," directly following on the authors' apparent 2012 prediction that it was non-functional, argues for problem-first: an existing finding (two isoforms exist; function of one unknown) closed with existing techniques. Both `gap-filling:first-measurement` (first functional/mechanistic account of Isoform 2 and its splicing regulation) and `recombination` (joining three assay types not previously combined on this target) are needed — removing either loses part of the story: first-measurement covers the novel functional claim, recombination covers how the mechanism was established.

## Alternative reading
Means-first is plausible if the exon 9b polyadenylation-competition assay itself, rather than being a routine application of known hnRNP-H biology, required inventing a genuinely new detection method — abstract alone does not show this, hence medium rather than high confidence.

## Notes for the codebook
A useful example of "recombination as the mechanism-building move within a single problem-first genesis": the genesis model turns on the target problem being pre-existing (isoform 2's function), while the move label captures that closing it required joining several previously-separate assay traditions on one gene — the codebook's guidance already supports listing both, but a worked biomedical example like this would help calibrate when `recombination` earns a slot alongside a `gap-filling:*` label rather than being the default second label the v1.1 rule warns against.
