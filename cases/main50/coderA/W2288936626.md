## Reasoning
No abstract, so `genesis_confidence` is capped at `low`. The title alone identifies the move: a first-principles study finding new high-pressure polymorphs of one specific oxide (MoO2). The reference list shows this exact type of study — DFT-based discovery of new high-pressure polymorphs — already carried out for closely related rutile-structured dioxides (TiO2 in 2001, SiO2 in 2008), using the same generic DFT toolchain (VASP/PAW/PBE, standard since the mid-1990s, plus DFT-D dispersion correction from 2010-2011). That is problem-first, `gap-filling:next-target` — established computational method and search strategy, new target material.

## Alternative reading
Means-first would apply if the specific structure-search algorithm or dispersion-correction scheme used here were itself new to the field rather than the standard DFT-D approach cited from 2010-2011 — full text would be needed to confirm the search methodology is routine rather than novel.

## Notes for the codebook
Computational materials-science "apply DFT to predict polymorphs of material X" papers are a recurring, low-ambiguity `next-target` pattern — the method (periodic DFT plus dispersion correction) is essentially always field-standard rather than paper-specific, so the "capability new to the field" test resolves quickly even without full text, once precedent for the same move on related materials is visible in the reference list.
