## The argument (secondary-source consensus, cross-checked across multiple
LBD reviews)

Swanson noticed two disconnected literatures that never cited each other:
one showing dietary fish oil reduces blood viscosity, platelet
aggregation, and vascular reactivity (A→B); another showing that reduced
blood viscosity/vasospasm improves outcomes in Raynaud's syndrome (B→C).
Neither literature cited the other and no study had tested fish oil on
Raynaud's patients directly. Swanson proposed the untested transitive
hypothesis A→C (fish oil may relieve Raynaud's) purely by combining the
two published literatures — no new data, no new instrument, just a
recombination made visible by systematic literature search. This became
the founding case of his ABC model of literature-based discovery, later
subjected to a clinical trial with positive results.
