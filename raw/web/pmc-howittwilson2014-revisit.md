## The argument (title + well-documented secondary account of Medawar 1963)

Medawar's 1963 BBC talk/essay "Is the scientific paper a fraud?" argued
that the standard IMRaD (Introduction–Methods–Results–Discussion) form
narrates a tidy inductive genesis — a stated hypothesis, deduced
predictions, confirming experiments — that misrepresents how the
research actually happened: false starts, accidents, ideas that arrived
after data collection, results reinterpreted after the fact. Howitt &
Wilson's 2014 revisit argues the problem has, if anything, gotten worse:
undergraduate teaching and textbook exemplars still train students to
write (and to expect) the fraudulent tidy narrative, reinforcing
hindsight bias in how the next generation both writes up and *remembers*
its own research process.
