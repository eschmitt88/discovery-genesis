## Secondary-source summary (Wikipedia, "Multiple discovery", fetched 2026-08-22)

- Merton distinguishes a **multiple** (a discovery made independently by two
  or more scientists/groups at about the same time) from a **singleton** (a
  discovery made uniquely by one).
- His claim: multiples, not singletons, are the *common* pattern in science
  — in the limit, "all scientific discoveries are in principle multiples."
  Apparent singletons are multiples that, for social/historical reasons,
  were not completed independently, or where the independent claimant
  never published or was suppressed.
- Improvements in scientific communication (postal service, printing,
  now digital) accelerate recombination of existing ideas and hence the
  rate of multiple discovery.
