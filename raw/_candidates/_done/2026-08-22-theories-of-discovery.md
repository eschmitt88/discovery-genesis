## 1. Atypical Combinations and Scientific Impact (Uzzi, Mukherjee, Stringer, Jones, 2013)

- url: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1240474
- type: paper
- summary: Across 17.9M papers, the highest-impact work pairs an exceptionally conventional core of references with an atypical (rare-pairing) tail, and teams are more likely than solo authors to produce that combination.
- reason: This is the direct empirical basis for H1's "conventional core plus atypical tail" prediction — the paper the plan's hypothesis is built to replicate on the project's own case/twin pairs.

## 2. Accelerating Science TRIZ Inventive Methodology in Illustrations (arXiv exposition)

- url: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.00536
- type: paper
- summary: A worked exposition of Altshuller's TRIZ — 40 inventive principles and the contradiction matrix distilled from tens of thousands of patents, illustrated on real cases.
- reason: TRIZ is the project's explicitly named closest precedent (patent-corpus-derived move catalogue); this is a citable, fetchable primary-adjacent account of the method the project is imitating for research papers.

## 3. Semantic TRIZ feasibility in technology development, innovation, and production: A systematic review (Heliyon, 2023)

- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10788813/
- type: paper
- summary: A systematic review concluding TRIZ's effectiveness rests mainly on practitioner case studies and surveys prone to self-selection and post-hoc rationalization, with little theory-grounded validation and no accepted standard for application.
- reason: Argues directly AGAINST taking a TRIZ-style catalogue on faith — exactly the risk the plan calls out (a catalogue "taken on faith") and the reason H2 requires coverage/agreement statistics rather than hand-picked exemplars.

## 4. Computer Models of Creativity (Boden, AI Magazine, 2009)

- url: https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/view/2254
- type: paper
- summary: Boden's own restatement of her three kinds of creativity — combinational (new combinations of familiar ideas), exploratory (novel structures within a fixed conceptual space), and transformational (changing the space's own rules) — with AI modeling examples of each.
- reason: A candidate high-level cut of the move taxonomy (H2); useful as an orthogonal axis to genesis_model, since combinational/exploratory/transformational could each occur under idea-first, means-first, or problem-first genesis.

## 5. The Blind-Variation and Selective-Retention Theory of Creativity (Simonton, 2010)

- url: https://www.cogsci.msu.edu/DSS/2012-2013/Simonton/2010BVSRcombmodPoLR.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Simonton's own statement of BVSR — creative ideas arise from blind (non-foresighted) combinatorial variation followed by selective retention of the useful ones, extended with a "sightedness" parameter for how much foresight really operates.
- reason: BVSR is the strongest existing theoretical account for why an "idea-first" genesis might be largely illusory even when reported that way — directly bears on H0 and on the plan's warning about hindsight-narrated genesis.

## 6. A Propulsion Model of Types of Creative Contributions (Sternberg, 1999)

- url: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1037/1089-2680.3.2.83
- type: paper
- summary: Sternberg's eight-type taxonomy of creative contributions (replication, redefinition, forward/advance forward incrementation, redirection, reconstruction, reinitiation, integration), sorted by whether they accept or reject the field's current paradigm.
- reason: A second independent, previously-published finite taxonomy of "moves" to cross-check H2's codebook against — useful as a sanity check on taxonomy size (~15) and on whether paradigm-acceptance is a move dimension worth coding.

## 7. How Scientists Think: On-line Creativity and Conceptual Change in Science (Dunbar, 1997/1999)

- url: https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/classes/AY2013/cs7601_spring/papers/Dunbar.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: In-vivo videotaped observation of molecular-biology labs finds most "discoveries" are unexpected findings (inconsistent with the lab's working hypothesis) that get reinterpreted in real time via local, in-lab analogies rather than via a prior stated idea.
- reason: The primary empirical grounding for the means-first genesis model — inconsistent findings noticed during ongoing work, then explained after the fact, is the in-vivo signature the coding protocol needs worked examples of.

## 8. Singletons and Multiples in Scientific Discovery (Merton, 1961)

- url: https://garfield.library.upenn.edu/histcomp/merton_p-am-philo-soc_v105_p470/index-aus.html
- type: paper
- summary: Merton's argument, from a large catalogue of documented independent-discovery cases, that all discoveries are "multiples in principle" — the same idea becomes reachable by several groups once its enabling conditions are in place — with true "singletons" as the rare, harder-to-explain case.
- reason: The evidentiary base for the problem-first / adjacent-possible genesis model: near-simultaneous independent discovery is the signature the plan lists for "the move was in the adjacent possible." Note: this link is a citation-index record confirming the 1961 Proc. Amer. Philos. Soc. 105:470 publication; full text sits behind JSTOR and was not independently re-verified as OA.

## 9. Fish Oil, Raynaud's Syndrome, and Undiscovered Public Knowledge (Swanson, 1986)

- url: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3797213/
- type: paper
- summary: Swanson's founding ABC-model case — combining the separately-known facts "fish oil reduces blood viscosity" (A→B) and "reduced viscosity eases Raynaud's" (B→C) to propose an untested A→C hypothesis, purely from disconnected literatures, no new data.
- reason: The cleanest documented case of accretion/recombination genesis with zero new empirical ingredient — a limit case for H4 ("all ingredients cited") and a concrete literature-based-discovery precedent for automating the "ingredients" field of the genesis card.

## 10. Is the Scientific Paper a Fraud? (Medawar, 1963) — via "Revisiting 'Is the scientific paper a fraud?'" (2014)

- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4210093/
- type: paper
- summary: Medawar's argument (reprised and contextualized in this open-access revisit) that the IMRaD paper narrates a tidy inductive genesis — hypothesis, then confirming experiment — that misrepresents the messy, error-strewn, often-retrofitted actual process.
- reason: Directly underwrites the plan's central methodological caution — that the "authors_story" field must be kept separate from the coded genesis_model because introductions systematically over-report idea-first origins. One of the required against-a-clean-idea-step viewpoints.

## 11. Creativity in Science and the Link to Cited References: Is the Creative Potential of Papers Reflected in their Cited References? (Tahamtan & Bornmann, 2018)

- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00224
- type: paper
- summary: Case studies and author interviews on landmark scientometrics papers find that unusual reference combinations do not reliably track where the creative idea actually came from — authors more often credit practical problems, colleague discussions, and interdisciplinary conversation than the cited literature itself.
- reason: A direct empirical challenge to H4's premise that ingredients are "almost always in the references" — the second required against-a-clean-idea-step (or at least against-a-clean-reference-trace) viewpoint, and a concrete risk the genesis-card protocol should budget for (an "external_story"/oral ingredient not visible in the reference list).

## 12. Long-run Patterns in the Discovery of the Adjacent Possible (Tria, Loreto, et al.; Industrial and Corporate Change, 2022)

- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00907
- type: paper
- summary: A computational/empirical test of Kauffman's "adjacent possible" — modeling innovation as expansion of a constantly-restructuring space of reachable next combinations — against long-run data on real innovation processes, with predictions on rate and diversification that are borne out.
- reason: Gives the enabler/problem-first genesis model a quantitative, falsifiable form (rather than just the metaphor) and a recent (2022) computational methodology the project's own H1/H4 feature pipeline can borrow from.

## 13. Accelerating Science with Human-Aware Artificial Intelligence (Sourati & Evans, Nature Human Behaviour, 2023)

- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01495
- type: paper
- summary: Models trained on simulated "digital doubles" of what scientists could plausibly infer from the literature predict actual future discoveries and their discoverers far better than content-only models, and can also be tuned to surface "alien" (currently unimaginable) hypotheses.
- reason: The closest existing precedent for H6's retrodiction test — it already frames discovery prediction as "given the prior state of the field, what comes next," and its human-aware vs. alien-hypothesis contrast is a ready-made baseline category alongside the plan's SciMON-style comparator.

## 14. Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? A Large-Scale Human Study with 100+ NLP Researchers (Si, Yang, Hashimoto, 2024)

- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109
- type: paper
- summary: A blinded, format-controlled head-to-head between LLM-generated and expert-generated NLP research ideas, rated by 100+ researchers: LLM ideas score higher on novelty but slightly lower on feasibility, with weak idea-to-execution correlation flagged as a limitation.
- reason: The most rigorous existing template for the H6 evaluation design (blinded judges, controlled format, novelty vs. feasibility separated) and a concrete illustration of exactly the novelty/impact-conflation risk the plan wants to avoid by keeping the two axes separate.

## 15. Thomas Kuhn (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

- url: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/thomas-kuhn/
- type: post
- summary: Authoritative secondary synthesis of Kuhn's view that discovery is triggered by anomaly — an observation the reigning paradigm cannot assimilate — and of the qualification that some anomaly-driven discoveries (e.g. DNA's structure) occur within normal science rather than full paradigm change.
- reason: Anomaly-noticing is a distinct discovery trigger from both "stated idea" and "new instrument" and belongs as its own row or sub-case in the enabler/genesis_model decision rules, especially for means-first cases where the anomaly appears mid-experiment.

## 16. Image and Logic: A Material Culture of Microphysics (Galison, 1997)

- url: https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/981761.Image_and_Logic
- type: book
- summary: A history-of-science account of twentieth-century particle physics as built from the interaction of three semi-autonomous subcultures — theory, experiment, and instrumentation — that communicate through local "trading zones" rather than one unified logic.
- reason: The primary source for the means-first / instrument-driven genesis model at the level of a whole subfield rather than a single lab episode; its "trading zone" idea is a plausible candidate move (cross-subculture translation) for the H2 codebook.
