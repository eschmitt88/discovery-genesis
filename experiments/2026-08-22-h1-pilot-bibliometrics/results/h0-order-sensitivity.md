# H0 decision-order sensitivity — is "idea-first is rare" a codebook artefact?

**The worry.** Codebook v1.1 assigns `genesis_model` by a cascade —
accretion → means-first → problem-first → idea-first, first match wins — and
sets a strict bar for idea-first ("a gap statement is not a proposition").
Across 192 blind codings of the main sample, idea-first was assigned **once**.
A label that the procedure reaches last and defines strictly could be
suppressed by the procedure rather than by the papers.

**The test.** 31 papers, stratified over the cascade's own verdicts
(18 problem-first, 8 means-first, 5 disputed; primary research only), re-coded
by two fresh blind coders under a **reversed** procedure: each of the four
models gets an independent yes / partial / no hearing with its evidence
recorded, and ties break toward idea-first — the exact opposite of the
cascade's bias.

## Result

| order | problem-first | means-first | idea-first | accretion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cascade (v1.1) | 41 (66 %) | 20 (32 %) | **1 (2 %)** | 0 |
| reversed, independent hearings | 42 (68 %) | 15 (24 %) | **4 (6 %)** | 1 (2 %) |

*(62 codings each — the same 31 papers by two coders under each order. The
subset deliberately over-samples means-first and disputed cases, so these are
not population rates; the comparison between orders is what matters.)*

- Coders kept the same label under both orders on **23/31** and **24/31**
  papers.
- Independent `q_idea` judgement across 62 codings: **no 53, partial 5,
  yes 4**.
- **No paper was coded idea-first by both coders** under reversal. The four
  idea-first calls are four different papers, one coder each.

## Conclusion

**The near-absence of idea-first is not primarily a cascade artefact.**
Giving idea-first the first and most favourable hearing moves it from 2 % to
6 % of codings, and none of those calls survives a second coder. Two thirds of
papers read as problem-first under either procedure. What the cascade *does*
suppress is a specific, real pattern that coder A identified: a comprehensive
modern follow-up organised around **someone else's** decades-old published
proposition (e.g. trisomy-21 interferon-receptor dosage, proposed 1974–78,
tested comprehensively in 2016). Under the cascade that stops at problem-first
— old question, existing tools — and the antecedent claim is never weighed.

## What this licenses, and what it does not

- **Licensed:** "Read from the published record, the genesis of an impactful
  primary paper is very rarely a proposition the authors could have stated
  before starting. Two thirds read as an existing open question closed with
  existing ingredients." This is robust to the order of judgement.
- **Not licensed:** any claim about what happened in the authors' heads. The
  record is written after the fact and is exactly what Medawar warned about
  ([[literature/papers/medawar1963fraud]]). The finding is about what the
  paper can show, not about how the idea arrived.
- **Codebook consequence (v1.2, not yet applied):** add a fifth question
  asked of every paper regardless of the cascade — *is there an identifiable
  antecedent proposition, by anyone, that this work is organised around?* —
  and record it as a separate field rather than letting the cascade decide
  whether it is ever asked.
