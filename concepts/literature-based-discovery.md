# Literature-based discovery

## Definition

Swanson's ABC model: two literatures share an intermediate concept B (one
shows A→B, the other shows B→C) but never cite each other, so the
transitive hypothesis A→C is testable purely by finding the disjoint
pair — no new data, no new instrument. The founding case (fish oil →
reduced blood viscosity → eased Raynaud's syndrome) generated a testable
clinical hypothesis from nothing but a systematic search over existing,
citable public knowledge.

## Why it matters here

It is the cleanest available limit case for H4's "ingredients are almost
always cited" claim: every component of the discovery was already
published, only the arrangement — finding the disjoint A/C literatures
via a shared B — was new. It gives `genesis-card`'s `ingredients` field
an operational, algorithmic precedent (ARROWSMITH-style AB-BC search) for
detecting when a paper's genesis was pure recombination, and it is a
concrete accretion/problem-first hybrid: the "problem" (Raynaud's
treatment) was old, the "ingredient" (the B-linking literature) was
already public, and the move was search, not invention.

## Connections

- Sits inside [[adjacent-possible]]: LBD is a formal, searchable version

- The ABC model requires the connecting concept B to be lexically
  explicit and locatable by keyword search in both literatures — real
  accretion cases may have a fuzzier, non-lexical connection that this
  method alone would miss; a limitation to test against `cases/` once
  the pilot runs.
