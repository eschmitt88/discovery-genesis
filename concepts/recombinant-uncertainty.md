# Recombinant uncertainty

## Definition

Fleming's (2001) finding: combining unfamiliar components or unfamiliar
component-combinations raises the *variance* of an invention's outcome
quality, not its mean — more failures, but also more breakthroughs.
Familiarity is a property of the inventor/field's prior search history,
not of the underlying knowledge space, so the same recombination can be
low-variance for one lab and high-variance for another.

## Why it matters here

This is the mechanism this project's twin design is implicitly betting
on. If novel recombination only shifted the mean of impact upward, every
atypical move would tend to land; instead it widens the spread, which
means for any given impactful atypical-combination paper there should
exist real papers that made the *same* move and landed in the low tail —
exactly the twins H5 goes looking for. It also gives H4 a reason novelty
alone can't be the whole story: the move explains variance, something
else (problem choice, timing, execution, community) has to explain which
tail a given instance lands in.

## Connections

- Sharpens [[novelty-vs-impact]]: "correlate weakly" is not a null result
  to explain away — recombination theory predicts weak correlation with
  the *mean* by construction; the interesting quantity is the *spread*.
- Gives [[matched-control-twin]] a theoretical reason to expect same-move
  twins to be common rather than rare, ahead of any empirical pilot check.
- Distinct from [[atypical-combination]]'s bibliometric operationalization
  (a measurement of the reference list) — this concept is about the
  underlying *mechanism* (search under unfamiliarity) that atypicality
  measures are trying to detect the fingerprint of.
